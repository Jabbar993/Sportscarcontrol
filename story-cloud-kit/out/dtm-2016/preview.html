Auer wins from pole at the Lausitzring as Ekström charges to second

Lucas Auer converted pole into his first DTM win for Mercedes-AMG Team Mücke by 3.763 seconds from Mattias Ekström, who rose from ninth, while third place gave Robert Wickens the championship lead.

Build-up · #11 #53

Saturday’s Audi one-two sets up Sunday

Saturday had belonged to Audi. Miguel Molina won Race 1 from pole, Jamie Green made it a one-two, and Robert Wickens took third for Mercedes. Marco Wittmann’s fourth place, as BMW’s best, put him top of the standings on 47 points, with Edoardo Mortara on 44 and Wickens and Paul di Resta level on 43. Sunday’s 60-minute race was a different proposition from the 40-minute sprint of the day before, because it carried a mandatory pit stop and so opened up far more room for strategy, a lever that would prove decisive.

Qualifying · #22 #8 #16

Mücke’s pink Mercedes take the front row

Lucas Auer took pole position with a 1m16.861s, a lap he found in the final seconds of the session. It was his second DTM pole, and it handed the Berlin-based Mücke team a one-two on its home weekend, because Christian Vietoris was just 0.070 seconds slower on 1m16.931s. Timo Glock, the Red Bull Ring winner, was third on 1m16.943s as the leading BMW, with Wickens fourth on 1m17.000s.

Mike Rockenfeller was fifth in the best of the Audis on 1m17.029s, a thousandth of a second ahead of Gary Paffett, with Green, Wittmann, Mattias Ekström and Tom Blomqvist completing the top ten. The whole top ten was covered by 0.281 seconds. Wittmann had led the session for a spell before dropping to eighth. The stewards then moved Vietoris back five places for blocking Ekström on a fast lap, so he started seventh and Glock moved up to the front row.

For the Saturday winner it was a poor session. Molina managed only 14th, while di Resta, who had started the weekend as championship leader, could do no better than 21st again. Mortara was 22nd, Timo Scheider 23rd and Martin Tomczyk last on 1m18.003s.

Opening laps · #17 #18 #99

Auer escapes, Molina takes out Farfus

Auer nailed the start and led the field into the first corner, while Wickens, third on the grid, went past Glock for second. Wickens stayed within a second of Auer early on but soon dropped back, and by the end of the first stint Auer had a lead of more than three seconds. Rockenfeller, fourth on the grid and the best-placed Audi, jumped the start, and that mistake wrecked his hopes of a good result.

Molina, 14th on the grid, never had the pace of the leaders and then ruined his afternoon in the opening laps. Approaching the first corner he tried to pass Augusto Farfus’s BMW, ran into it, and sent the BMW off the track and out of the race. Molina was given a drive-through penalty for the contact. Farfus said Molina had braked far too late, while Molina saw it as a 50:50 incident and felt Farfus had blocked him. The Saturday winner eventually finished 19th.

Pit stops · #2 #48 #5

Ekström waits, Glock’s stop goes wrong

The mandatory stop turned the race. Wickens and Glock were the first of the front-runners to come in, and Glock’s stop was a poor one that cost him several places. Paffett stopped a lap later and rejoined well ahead of the BMW. Auer’s own stop came soon afterwards, and his crew got him out quickly.

Ekström did exactly what he had done at the Red Bull Ring two weeks earlier and waited longer than any other leading driver. He ran at the front for a long stretch while the others cycled through the pits, and he finally came in during the second half of the race. He rejoined behind Green and Paffett, but on far fresher tyres, and he passed Paffett and then Green on his way back up the order. Wickens, meanwhile, complained of being stuck in traffic after his own stop.

Mortara, who had started 22nd, was involved in a more serious incident during his stop, when he struck one of the mechanics in the pit lane. The man was injured and taken to hospital. Mortara finished 12th.

Slow zone · #12 #84 #3

Two Mercedes stop out and a slow zone falls

In the second half of the race, two of HWA’s Mercedes stopped out on track. Maximilian Götz’s car stopped first, after 30 laps, and Daniel Juncadella’s followed after 33. Their stoppages brought out a brief slow zone. Ekström later said the slow zones made it hard to keep the tyres up to temperature, but he could see that the drivers ahead of him were struggling with oversteer.

Di Resta, in the other HWA Mercedes, was classified 21st, five laps down. Green set the fastest lap of the race on lap 26, a 1m17.840s, but was caught by Ekström on his older tyres and had to settle for fourth.

The finish · #6

Auer wins by 3.763 seconds

With the stops done Auer was back in front with a cushion of about five seconds over Wickens and was never in danger of losing the race. The excitement was behind him. Ekström, on the freshest tyres in the field, caught Wickens, who said he had no grip left after the slow zone, and took second place with a DRS move two laps from the end. Green also closed in, but Wickens held on to third by 0.384 seconds.

Auer took the flag after 46 laps in 1h02m11.261s, 3.763 seconds ahead of Ekström, who had started ninth. Wickens finished 6.386 seconds behind the winner and Green 6.770. Paffett was fifth, 12.292 seconds back. Wittmann was sixth and the best BMW, having held off Vietoris, who was seventh. Nico Müller climbed from 15th to eighth, and Bruno Spengler and Glock took the last two points.

Aftermath

First win for Auer and for Mücke

Auer said he had never had to work so hard for a win, calling the first few laps critical and praising a perfect stop from his crew. It was his first DTM victory in his 23rd race, the first by an Austrian in the series and the first for the Berlin-based Mücke team, which achieved it on its home weekend. Mercedes team principal Ulrich Fritz called it a commanding performance from start to finish. For Mercedes it ended a short drought: the only win it had taken all year was di Resta’s in the second Hockenheim race in May.

Ekström, who had called his qualifying disappointing, said patience was the key. Once the stops began he knew his moment had come, and he found himself quicker than Green, Paffett and Wickens, who he said were fighting oversteer on tired tyres. Wickens lost second place late on but collected a second podium of the weekend and the championship lead, and said the traffic after his early stop had made life hard. Green took 30 points from the weekend with a second and a fourth, and moved up to fourth in the table.

It was a weekend to forget for several others. Molina went from winning on Saturday to 19th on Sunday after the contact with Farfus. Rockenfeller finished 17th after his jump start. Mortara called it a terrible weekend and said such an accident in the pit lane must never happen again, while Abt team principal Hans-Jürgen Abt said the Italian could not be blamed and that the team was sorry for the injured mechanic, who had to spend a night in hospital. Di Resta went without points for the third race in a row.

The championship was now wide open. Wickens led on 58 points, three clear of Wittmann on 55, with Ekström on 46, Green on 45, Mortara on 44 and di Resta on 43, so that only 15 points separated the first six drivers with 12 races still to run. A crowd of 67,500 had watched the Motorsport Festival over the weekend, and BMW’s Jens Marquardt called the event a complete success. The DTM next heads to the Norisring street circuit in Nuremberg for Races 7 and 8 on 25–26 June.

Mortara wins the finale but Wittmann takes the title

Edoardo Mortara won Race 2 at Hockenheim for Audi by 3.040 seconds from Christian Vietoris, but Marco Wittmann's fourth place gave him the DTM title by four points.

Build-up

A 17-point gap and one race

Saturday had narrowed the title fight without settling it. Miguel Molina won Race 1 for Audi and Marco Wittmann finished second, but Edoardo Mortara climbed from eighth on the grid to third, leaving him 17 points behind with 25 for a win still to come. Wittmann led the standings on 194 points to Mortara's 177 and needed to finish fifth or better on Sunday to be sure of the championship; Jamie Green had been eliminated on 141. Mortara needed at least second place, and even a win would only be enough if Wittmann finished seventh or lower. Only the 25 points of Sunday's race were left to win.

Qualifying · #13 #2 #31

Da Costa on pole again, the title rivals on row three

António Félix da Costa took his second pole position of the weekend in his final DTM qualifying session, going to the top of the order in the closing minutes with a 1:32.632 and improving to 1:32.525. Gary Paffett's Mercedes was 0.014 seconds behind, with Tom Blomqvist third in the RBM BMW, 0.064 off the pole time, followed by Maxime Martin. Timo Glock had set the first benchmark for BMW, which Paffett and Green beat in turn, and Wittmann had climbed to second on his early runs before the final-run shuffle pushed him back.

The title rivals were on the third row. Wittmann was fifth with a 1:32.616 and Mortara sixth, just 0.028 seconds slower, after a session disrupted by a technical problem with his Audi that left him short of running. Wickens was seventh, Glock eighth, Christian Vietoris ninth and Green tenth, so that five BMWs started in the top ten. Di Resta qualified only 18th, while Molina, Saturday's winner, was 22nd on the grid, with Rast 23rd and Maximilian Götz at the back of the field in 24th.

Opening laps · #6 #36

Mortara sweeps to the front

Mortara lost places at the start from sixth on the grid, but he recovered quickly. He passed Wickens, Martin and Blomqvist in the first two laps while da Costa led from Paffett. Their fight ended when Paffett tagged the back of the BMW as it moved across to take the racing line, sending da Costa off the track. Paffett was given a drive-through penalty and da Costa, who said afterwards that Paffett had been impatient, went on to retire from the race.

That promoted Wittmann to the lead, for only a short time. Mortara was past him by lap six, and the race turned into a matter of how far Wittmann could fall. Mortara built a lead of more than six seconds before the stops.

Pit stops · #8 #3 #16

Mercedes drivers pass Wittmann

The mandatory stops put the championship under pressure. Mortara and Wittmann both stopped late, and Wittmann rejoined behind the Mercedes of Vietoris and di Resta and, for a time, Blomqvist's BMW. Fifth place was enough for the title, but he was no longer comfortable. Di Resta, who had climbed from 18th on the grid, did not give way when Wittmann tried to go through, and once the Audis that had yet to stop were out of the way, Vietoris and di Resta settled in second and third.

Wittmann passed Blomqvist and then settled into fourth, with fellow BMW drivers Glock, Martin and Blomqvist following in his wake. Glock set the fastest lap of the race on lap 30. Behind them, several cars did not see the flag: Adrien Tambay, Farfus, Rosenqvist and Daniel Juncadella were all out.

The finish · #48 #11 #53

Mortara wins, Wittmann champion by four points

Mortara crossed the line in 1:02:52.131, 3.040 seconds ahead of Vietoris, for Audi's win in the final race of the year. Di Resta completed the podium in third, 4.294 seconds behind, a recovery from 18th on the grid that gave Mercedes two podium places. Wittmann took the flag in fourth, 5.030 seconds back, and won the title with 206 points to Mortara's 202.

Glock, Martin and Blomqvist took the next three places, Green was eighth, Wickens ninth and Martin Tomczyk took the last point in tenth on the final start of his DTM career. Paffett finished 15th and da Costa was out after 21 laps. Molina, who had won on Saturday, was 14th. Audi's Nico Müller and Rockenfeller were 13th and 11th.

Aftermath

Wittmann's second title, Audi's bitter win

Wittmann said he had tried to bring the car home, called it unbelievable to be champion for the second time and said that his 2014 title had come with a more dominant car and more wins, while this year the team had had to be clever. It was his second DTM crown, following the 2014 one, and BMW's Jens Marquardt said his consistency and speed had made the difference in a close field.

For Audi it was a bitter victory. Mortara won the race, and Audi took the manufacturers' title and the team title for Audi Sport Team Abt Sportsline, but he fell four points short of Wittmann. Audi's DTM chief Dieter Gass called it the bitterest win he could remember and said that far fewer than ten points had been missing, the number the marque felt it had lost at Zandvoort in July. Mortara said that it was very disappointing but that there was nothing he could do about it.

Mercedes finished third in the manufacturers' standings, but Ulrich Fritz said the double podium was what the team had wanted at the end of the season and that it would try to come back stronger in 2017. Several drivers also left the series. Da Costa will not return, and said afterwards that he would have liked a podium in his last race, while Tomczyk and Timo Scheider also completed their final DTM weekends.

Wittmann finished the season on 206 points, four ahead of Mortara on 202, with Green third on 145, Wickens fourth on 124, di Resta fifth on 116 and Blomqvist sixth on 113. Blomqvist called sixth in the standings a good result, saying the key had been the team's qualifying form. With that, the nine race weekends of 2016, 18 races in all, were over.

Wittmann wins Red Bull Ring Race 1 from pole as BMW takes a one-two

Marco Wittmann led every lap from pole to win DTM Race 1 at the Red Bull Ring by 0.726 seconds for BMW Team RMG, with Blomqvist second and Mortara's Audi third.

Build-up · #3

Paul di Resta arrives as leader, BMW arrives hungry

The DTM's second weekend of 2016 was also its first trip abroad, to the Red Bull Ring in the Styrian mountains, and it began with Paul di Resta at the head of the table. The Mercedes-AMG driver had won Sunday's race at Hockenheim for HWA and led the championship on 37 points, ahead of Robert Wickens on 28 and Edoardo Mortara on 25. Mortara had taken the Saturday opener for Audi, and Mercedes had generally had the upper hand over the opening weekend.

BMW had left Hockenheim with podiums but no win. Augusto Farfus and Maxime Martin were second and third behind di Resta on Sunday, while Marco Wittmann, the 2014 champion now driving the Red Bull-liveried M4 for Team RMG, collected only four points from the whole weekend. Spielberg is the home circuit of his new sponsor, and he rated it a track that suited him. Sitting at almost 700 metres above sea level, it is the highest circuit on the calendar, with steep climbs and descents across its 4.326 kilometres and two long straights that invite overtaking.

The surface was new, and the drivers spoke of plenty of grip. Saturday's race was set at 40 minutes plus one lap, and with the points given to the top ten only, every place in the middle of the field was worth fighting for.

Qualifying · #11 #31 #27

BMW locks out the front row by 0.019 seconds

Qualifying began with a benchmark from practice, a mid-1m23s lap by Mike Rockenfeller, that was beaten within minutes. Timo Glock improved slightly, then Tom Blomqvist produced a 1m22.814s on his second attempt, only for Team RMG's Wittmann to go quicker still with a 1m22.795s. For the first half of the session the pair were unchallenged, and at one point all eight BMW drivers sat inside the top ten.

Audi came back shortly before halfway. Adrien Tambay moved up to third for Team Rosberg on 1m22.903s and Edoardo Mortara slotted in right behind him on 1m22.932s, giving the Audis the second row. Neither Wittmann nor Blomqvist could improve at the end, so the order stood: Wittmann on pole, Blomqvist 0.019 seconds behind, a BMW front row for Spielberg. Glock was fifth, ahead of Martin and António Félix da Costa.

Mercedes had a difficult session on the new asphalt. Di Resta was the best of them in eighth on 1m22.998s, with Götz and Wickens twelfth and thirteenth and Christian Vietoris last of the 24 on 1m23.534s. The grid itself was not quite the qualifying order: Félix da Costa started tenth, three places below where he had qualified, so di Resta lined up seventh, Tomczyk eighth and Ekström ninth.

Opening laps · #13 #36 #5

Wittmann gets away, Tambay goes backwards

Wittmann made his pole count at the start and led into the first corner with Blomqvist tucked behind him. Mortara got past his Audi team-mate Tambay to take third, and the Frenchman's difficult opening stint was only beginning: Glock passed him into Remus and Martin Tomczyk followed on the exit, so that four BMWs were suddenly in the top five.

There was contact further back at the first corner, where Martin, Félix da Costa and Ekström brushed together, an incident that dropped Ekström down the order. Martin recovered quickly, passed di Resta early on and set off after Tambay, who continued to drift down the field.

Yellow flags at Remus · #12 #8

A Mercedes collision and a drive-through

Trouble for Mercedes came on the sixth lap, when Daniel Juncadella tried to pass his team-mate Vietoris and the pair collided. Juncadella's front-left suspension was damaged in the clash, which brought out yellow flags in the final sector. The two Mercedes completed only four and five laps.

Two laps later the same corner decided the order among the midfield. Tambay ran wide and Martin got past him in time, but Félix da Costa passed under the yellow flags and was given a drive-through penalty for it. The Portuguese dropped to the back and finished last of the cars that completed the distance, in 22nd. Di Resta, who had also passed Tambay, was promoted to seventh, which kept him as the best Mercedes in the field and protected his lead in the championship.

Mortara holds the line · #48 #16 #100

Mortara keeps BMW from a podium sweep

At the front the gap between Wittmann and Blomqvist stayed at around half a second throughout the first half of the race, with Mortara hanging on in third. Wittmann, who said the rivals' DRS made the lead hard to defend, gradually added a few tenths and made the win look comfortable.

The more urgent battle was for the last podium place. Glock pressed Mortara in the middle of the race, but the Audi driver used better brake balance and straight-line speed to keep the Team RMG car behind him, and Glock then faded. Tomczyk stayed close behind Glock for the rest of the race without finding a way past, while Martin settled sixth. Further back Nico Müller was making ground from around 14th, passing Spengler and Wickens and arriving at the back of Farfus' car.

The finish · #51 #18

A sixth win for Wittmann and a first of 2016

Wittmann crossed the line after 30 laps and 42 minutes 10.795 seconds, 0.726 seconds ahead of Blomqvist, for his sixth DTM win and his first of the season. Mortara completed the podium 1.251 seconds behind the winner, and he kept the pressure on Blomqvist right down to the final lap. Glock was fourth, 5.566 seconds back, with Tomczyk 6.222 seconds from the winner and Martin sixth.

Six of the eight BMWs finished in the top ten, with Farfus ninth. Di Resta was seventh and the only Mercedes to score, ahead of Tambay in eighth. Müller took tenth and with it the final point, 15.750 seconds behind, while Wickens was eleventh. Ekström, who had been shuffled down at the start, still set the fastest lap of the race, a 1m23.442s on lap 19, though he finished only 16th.

Wittmann's win moved him up to third in the table, on 29 points. Di Resta kept the lead on 43 after seventh place, Mortara was second on 40, and Wickens, Martin and Blomqvist followed on 28, 27 and 26. Wittmann said afterwards that the Red Bull Ring seemed to suit him very well, and that the team deserved the result.

Mortara leads an Audi top six at the Hungaroring

Edoardo Mortara led from pole to win Race 1 by 1.090 seconds from Jamie Green, with Audi filling the top six and Marco Wittmann's championship lead cut to 14 points.

Build-up · #11 #48

Wittmann's lead faces its biggest test

The DTM reached the Hungaroring for the eighth of its nine weekends with the title fight narrowing but still tilted towards Marco Wittmann. The BMW driver had won Race 1 at the Nürburgring two weeks earlier and left Germany on 170 points, 33 clear of Edoardo Mortara, who had hit back with Audi's win in Race 2. Robert Wickens was third on 120 points, Jamie Green fourth on 119, and Paul di Resta and Tom Blomqvist completed the top six on 100 and 93.

With 18 races spread over nine weekends in 2016, only four remained: two in Budapest and two at Hockenheim. A win was worth 25 points, so Wittmann's cushion, comfortable as it looked, could be consumed in a few races. Each Hungaroring race had its own qualifying session, a 40-minute sprint on Saturday and a 60-minute race on Sunday, which meant that a poor Saturday morning could be put right a day later but also that a single mistake could cost a contender a whole race. Audi came to Budapest with the momentum, and the open question was whether Mercedes and BMW could keep pace on a tight, twisting circuit where passing is hard and the grid matters.

Qualifying · #53 #88 #6

Audi locks out the first four rows

Audi dominated the session. Timo Scheider set the first benchmark, but Green and Mortara soon took over at the front. Green went to provisional pole inside the last five minutes, only for Mortara to answer on his final attempt, with the chequered flag already out, with a 1m35.282s that beat Green by 0.082 seconds.

Miguel Molina took third for Abt, 0.167 seconds off pole, ahead of Mike Rockenfeller, Nico Müller and Adrien Tambay, with Scheider seventh and Mattias Ekström eighth, 0.528 seconds behind Mortara. All eight Audi RS 5 DTMs therefore filled the first eight places. Felix Rosenqvist was best of the rest in ninth for Mercedes, 0.588 seconds off pole, and he outqualified Wittmann, who was tenth and the fastest of the BMWs, 0.611 seconds adrift. Blomqvist, Martin Tomczyk and Timo Glock were the next BMWs.

It was a hard session for the other Mercedes. Daniel Juncadella was their second-best in 16th, and Wickens, the manufacturer's leading title hope, could only manage 18th, 0.799 seconds off the pace. After grid adjustments he lined up 17th, with António Félix da Costa, who had been 15th in qualifying, starting from 20th.

Opening laps · #5 #100 #17

Mortara escapes, Turn 2 catches Ekström

Mortara made a better start than Green and had a lead of about a second by the end of the first lap, with Molina third. Behind them the opening corners were messy. At Turn 2 Tomczyk's BMW and Ekström's Audi spun after a tangle that also involved Maxime Martin, and the Swede, who had started eighth, rejoined at the back of the field. Tomczyk was given a 30-second time penalty for the incident and finished 22nd.

Wittmann had the best start of the contenders. He gained three places from tenth on the grid, getting ahead of Scheider, Ekström and Rosenqvist, and ran seventh at the end of the first lap. Wickens made up even more ground, climbing from 17th into the top ten on lap one. Up front Mortara and Green quickly pulled clear of Molina.

Mid-race · #3 #13 #51

A quiet middle stint with one retirement

Green closed to around half a second behind Mortara during the first half of the race, but the gap never grew beyond about a second and a half and each time the Italian answered. Nothing suggested that Green would launch a real attack.

The only retirement came after di Resta and Félix da Costa came together at Turn 1. The Mercedes, which had started 22nd, took damage and the Scot stopped after completing 11 laps. Elsewhere the order settled. Müller took fifth from Tambay in one of the few significant passes of the afternoon, while Rosenqvist and Scheider fought over eighth some way behind Wittmann.

The finish · #99 #10

Mortara wins by 1.090 seconds as the gap to Wittmann shrinks

Mortara never had to defend and took the flag 1.090 seconds ahead of Green, his fourth win of the season. Green stayed within reach for most of the race but never mounted an attack, and Molina completed an all-Audi podium, 9.240 seconds behind the winner. Rockenfeller was fourth, Müller fifth and Tambay sixth, so that Audi filled the top six.

Wittmann limited the damage with seventh, 23.177 seconds behind the winner and the only BMW in the points, ahead of Rosenqvist, Scheider and Wickens, who took the last point after his strong start. Ekström, the only Audi outside the top ten, finished 19th, and Mortara also set the fastest lap, a 1m36.959s on lap four. The result cut Wittmann's lead from 33 to 14 points, 176 to 162, with Green third on 137 and Wickens fourth on 121.
