Graff wins Portimão as G-Drive takes the LMP2 title

Graff's Oreca #40 won the 4 Hours of Portimão by 38 seconds after United's Ligier was penalised for pit-lane speeding, and fourth place secured G-Drive's LMP2 title from third on the grid.

Build-up · #22

A new circuit for a title decider

The European Le Mans Series ended its 2017 season at the Algarve circuit near Portimão on 22 October, seven years after its predecessor last raced there. The finale replaced the usual date at Estoril, and 35 cars entered, with 12 of them in LMP2, the first year of the new-generation prototypes: Oreca 07, Ligier JS P217 and Dallara P217 were all on the grid. It was a round where all three class titles were still open, and in LMP2 the fight was between two teams that had split the season. G-Drive Racing by DragonSpeed arrived 18 points clear on 98, with the #22 Oreca of Rojas and Roussel and a third driver, Hirakawa, who had missed two rounds because of his racing commitments in Japan and so was not part of the drivers' title equation. The team had won only once, at Monza, and finished second at each of the other four rounds, consistency rather than dominance carrying it to the final race.

United Autosports' #32 Ligier of Owen, de Sadeleer and Albuquerque had won at Silverstone and the Red Bull Ring but had slipped to sixth at Monza and fifth at Le Castellet. The crew needed to win and then hope for trouble for the leaders, since a top-five or top-six finish for G-Drive was enough to end the argument. Graff, with the #40 Oreca of Allen, Bradley and Yacaman, had won at Spa and sat third on 61 points with an outside chance of the runner-up place, while SMP Racing's Dallara, winner at Le Castellet, was fifth in the table after missing the first two rounds.

The wider LMP2 picture was noisy. A week earlier the ACO had confirmed one-off 'Joker' updates for the Ligier, Dallara and Riley, but not for the Oreca, and Oreca's president said the company felt unfairly penalised. G-Drive had also used the Tuesday test to give Audi factory driver Nico Müller eleven laps in an LMP2 for the first time ahead of his Shanghai WEC debut with the team, on a day of mixed weather. On track DragonSpeed's #21 Oreca topped Friday practice through Lapierre with a 1:34.182, and Hirakawa went quickest of the weekend in Saturday morning's session with a 1:34.034 for the points leaders.

Qualifying · #23 #21 #39

Panis Barthez steals pole

Qualifying was a ten-minute session cut short by a red flag when Pizzitola spun the Algarve Pro Racing Ligier #25 into the gravel at Turn 16 after about three minutes, leaving seven on the clock at the restart. Hirakawa set the early pace for G-Drive with a 1:34.235, but Nathanaël Berthon beat it with a 1:33.575 in the Michelin-shod Panis Barthez Ligier #23. It was the team's first pole of the year, its previous best grid slot having been fourth at the Red Bull Ring, and the first for a Ligier P217 in LMP2 competition that season. Berthon called it 'mega' and credited a long run of work across the season.

Nicolas Lapierre, who had spun at Turn 14 earlier in the run, recovered to take second for the #21 DragonSpeed Oreca with a 1:34.090, 0.515 seconds off pole, which pushed the championship leaders onto the second row. Hirakawa held third, and the Graff #39 of Guibbert was fourth on 1:34.240, five thousandths slower than the points leaders. Filipe Albuquerque put the United Ligier #32 fifth on 1:34.551, ahead of the Idec Ligier #28 of Chatin on 1:34.746 and the Cetilar Villorba Corse Dallara #47 of Belicchi on 1:35.110. SMP's Dallara #27 of Isaakyan was eighth and the Graff #40 of Bradley ninth.

With the grid set, G-Drive needed only a steady race. Albuquerque, racing at his home circuit, said United would take risks and go for the win, since the team had little to lose and wanted to make G-Drive feel the pressure.

Hour 1 · #32 #47

De Sadeleer goes from fifth to first

The race started at 13:00 and the United Ligier #32 moved immediately. Hugo de Sadeleer, starting from fifth, climbed through the field in the opening stint and took the lead from Fabien Barthez in the pole-sitting Panis Barthez Ligier. A first Safety Car then slowed the field when Henrik Hedman put the DragonSpeed Oreca #21 into the gravel at Turn 5. Behind the leaders Léo Roussel held third in the title-chasing G-Drive Oreca, with Sernagiotto fourth for Cetilar Villorba Corse in the #47 Dallara and Yacaman fifth in the Graff #40. At the restart Roussel went past Barthez into Turn 1 to move to second, and Yacaman gained places too.

Hours 2–3 · #27

Four cars lead, then the penalty

At the two-hour mark de Sadeleer led and Roussel was in the position to win the title, 44 seconds behind. The race then opened up. During the first round of stops Egor Orudzhev briefly put the SMP Racing Dallara #27 in front, and as the middle phase wore on the Ligier #32, the G-Drive Oreca, the Graff Oreca and the SMP car all took a turn at the front. Shortly before halfway James Allen took the lead for Graff by passing de Sadeleer at Turn 14, and the United car lost a few seconds in the pits when it would not fire up at its next stop.

The race turned on a pit-lane offence. With less than 80 minutes remaining, Will Owen, leading in the #32, was given a stop-and-hold penalty plus 55 seconds for speeding in the pit lane. It cost the car the lead and dropped it to third, and with it United's chance of the title, since G-Drive was running comfortably in the top three. Graff's own afternoon was not trouble-free: the fuel tank was short by roughly 20 litres at each stop, which forced an extra visit to the pits late on.

The finish · #40

Bradley wins, Albuquerque takes second

In the closing stages Paul Petit spun the Graff #39 at the last corner and speared across the track into the barriers at the end of the pit lane, blocking the entrance. The marshals cleared it under local yellows. Richard Bradley, now in the #40 Oreca, caught and passed the United Ligier to take the lead and kept it to the flag. Isaakyan was second in the SMP Dallara until five minutes from the end, when Filipe Albuquerque, driving in front of his home crowd, passed him to put the United Ligier back into second.

Bradley won by 38.169 seconds from the #32, with the Dallara of Isaakyan and Orudzhev third, 59.254 seconds behind the winner. Hirakawa finished fourth in the G-Drive Oreca, 1.944 seconds from the SMP car and well clear of the fifth-placed Cetilar Dallara, which finished a lap down, as did Panis Barthez in sixth after starting from pole. DragonSpeed's #21 recovered from its gravel trip to ninth, two laps down, with Hanley setting the race's fastest lap of 1:35.048. For Graff it was a second win of the season after Spa; Bradley said the stressful final stint was played out without knowing for certain that the car would make the flag.

Aftermath

A first title for G-Drive and DragonSpeed

Fourth place was enough. G-Drive Racing by DragonSpeed won the LMP2 teams' title, and Rojas and Roussel took the drivers' crown, after a season of one win, at Monza, and second places at Silverstone, the Red Bull Ring, Le Castellet and Spa. It was G-Drive's second ELMS LMP2 title in a row, DragonSpeed's first international championship and the first for the Oreca 07 chassis. Team principal Elton Julian praised the team effort and said the whole story of how the programme was put together would come out one day. Roussel called it the best moment of his life, and the team ended up in the swimming pool outside the media centre. Rojas said winning in Europe had been a dream since he left racing in America.

The ELMS awards on Sunday evening honoured several LMP2 names: Cetilar Villorba Corse was named Team of the Year after moving up from LMP3 with the Dallara P217, Hugo de Sadeleer won Rookie of the Year, and DragonSpeed's Hedman was named Gentleman Driver of the Year. United's trio, Owen, de Sadeleer and Albuquerque, finished the season as runners-up to G-Drive.

Graff's win gave Dunlop its 17th ELMS victory in a row, and G-Drive's title was the 10th for the tyre manufacturer in the ELMS LMP2 championship, the ninth in a row. G-Drive's title also brought the team the sole Le Mans entry through the LMP2 results, one of the automatic places confirmed that Sunday alongside entries for United, JMW and TF Sport.

The 2018 LMP2 picture already had a new element, with the Joker updates for the Ligier, Dallara and Riley still to be homologated through wind-tunnel tests. G-Drive's own next step was the World Endurance Championship round at Shanghai, where Nico Müller was set to drive its Oreca after his Portimão test laps.

United snatches Silverstone opener from G-Drive

Filipe Albuquerque, Will Owen and Hugo de Sadeleer won the 4 Hours of Silverstone in the #32 United Ligier by 6.3 seconds after G-Drive's Oreca ran out of tyre life in the closing minutes.

Build-up · #28

A new LMP2 generation arrives

The 4 Hours of Silverstone on 15 April 2017 opened the European Le Mans Series season and was the first European race for the new-generation LMP2 prototypes. The Gibson-powered Oreca 07, Ligier JS P217 and Dallara P217 all made their series debut, while the Riley/Multimatic chassis was absent. The entry list held 35 cars, 12 of them LMP2, one fewer than the ACO had announced over the winter because the SMP Racing Dallara, also missing from the Monza Prologue, did not appear.

The field had been reshaped. United Autosports, Tockwith Motorsport, Graff, Cetilar Villorba Corse, High Class Racing and Racing Team Nederland were all new to the category, and the line-ups were largely those seen at the Prologue. The exception was the IDEC Ligier, where David Zollinger joined Paul and Patrice Lafargue for Silverstone and Le Mans only, with Olivier Pla due to take the third seat at the other rounds. Several LMP2 teams also tested at Silverstone the Monday before the race.

The cars were immediately fast. In the opening practice session Ryo Hirakawa put the G-Drive Racing Oreca #22, run by DragonSpeed, top with 1:44.699, more than five seconds under the lap record Harry Tincknell had set in 2015. Ben Hanley's sister DragonSpeed Oreca was second, and Andrea Pizzitola's Algarve Pro Ligier was the best of the Ligiers. Hanley went quickest again in the second session with 1:44.647, with Richard Bradley's Graff Oreca second and Filipe Albuquerque's United Ligier third. Léo Roussel's G-Drive car was recovered on a flatbed that morning after a left-rear wheel came off.

Qualifying · #21 #39 #40

Hanley takes DragonSpeed's first pole

Hanley took pole with 1:44.040 in the #21 DragonSpeed Oreca, his first ELMS pole, and set a new track record that was roughly six seconds faster than the 2015 mark. The session was interrupted early when Bradley put the #40 Graff Oreca into the gravel at Becketts, bringing out a red flag. Hanley had to restart his preparation after the delay and found the time on his final run, while Albuquerque had briefly held provisional pole with 1:44.314.

That left the United Ligier second, 0.274 seconds adrift. Enzo Guibbert put the #39 Graff Oreca third on 1:45.216 ahead of a recovered Hirakawa, who had also spun early but made it back out to take fourth for G-Drive. Pizzitola completed the top five for Algarve Pro and High Class Racing's Anders Fjordbach was seventh in the new Dallara. Bradley's off left the #40 car 28th on the grid, behind the whole LMP3 field.

Hour 1 · #25 #49

Hanley leads, Fjordbach and Bradley surge

Hanley led the opening hour from pole, but the early running was crowded with changes at the front. Fjordbach took the Dallara #49 to the lead briefly around the end of the first hour, a sign of the car's pace on its series debut, before Bradley, who had started 28th, hauled the #40 Graff Oreca into the lead. Between them the Oreca, Ligier and Dallara all led at some stage of the afternoon.

The pole-sitting car did not last. A couple of spins by Henrik Hedman and then gearbox trouble dropped the #21 Oreca out of contention, and it lost six laps in the pits, finishing the race 10th. The Algarve Pro Ligier #25 of Andrea Roda, Pizzitola and Matthew McMurry was running among the leaders at that stage.

Hours 2–3 · #22 #34

Roussel takes over, United fight back

The middle of the race belonged to G-Drive. The G-Drive Oreca had started fourth, and Roussel made up the time lost during Memo Rojas's stint, then took the lead at Copse around half distance from the Algarve Ligier, having passed the #40 Graff and the #25 car within two laps. The Algarve car soon retired with a gearbox failure on track, the only one of the new LMP2s to stop for good, and Roussel built a gap that stood at roughly half a minute in the closing stages.

United had been in the top-three fight early on but dropped back after a drive-through penalty for the #32 Ligier, which Albuquerque later said had left it around 45 seconds behind. Nigel Moore and Phil Hanson ran quietly for Tockwith Motorsports in the #34 Ligier and ended up fifth, while Bradley's early lead faded and Franck Matelli and James Allen brought the #40 car home fourth.

The finish · #32 #23

Albuquerque catches the G-Drive Oreca

Hirakawa led by about 30 seconds in the final stint, but his tyres were fading and the lead evaporated. Albuquerque managed his own rubber, then closed the gap dramatically over the last 15 minutes, working through traffic that included the sister DragonSpeed Oreca of Nicolas Lapierre at Vale and Club to get on the tail of the G-Drive car.

He took the lead with less than three minutes left and went on to win by 6.3 seconds, leaving Hirakawa, Rojas and Roussel to settle for second after a drive that had dominated the middle of the race.

The podium covered all three chassis makers: United's Ligier, G-Drive's Oreca and High Class Racing's Dallara #49 of Dennis Andersen and Fjordbach, 28 seconds back on its series debut.

Aftermath

A debut win and a podium for every chassis

Albuquerque was delighted after the race, saying it had been a win despite the drive-through, with the car well behind at one point. It was also the first European race won under the new LMP2 rules.

In LMP2, G-Drive's Hirakawa, Rojas and Roussel took second, 6.339 seconds behind, and High Class Racing completed the podium, 28.158 behind. Graff's #40 was fourth, 53.426 behind after starting 28th, behind the entire LMP3 field, and earned 12 championship points for fourth. Tockwith was fifth and Cetilar Villorba Corse sixth. The #39 Graff of Trouillet, Petit and Guibbert finished seventh, a lap down, with the IDEC and Panis Barthez Ligiers three laps off. DragonSpeed's #21 was 10th, six laps adrift after its gearbox problems, and Algarve Pro's #25 was not classified after 70 laps.

The weekend also brought a minor administrative note, as DragonSpeed, Algarve Pro Racing, Graff and United were among LMP2 teams given a suspended 1,000-euro fine for using the previous year's seat belts and asked to fit the new ones for the next round.

The next round of the season is the 4 Hours of Monza on 14 May.

Falb and Rayhall win for United’s #2 as the pole-sitter fails to finish

Falb and Rayhall won LMP3 in United’s #2 Ligier by 2.4 seconds from Inter Europol’s Hippe after starting fifth in class, while pole-sitter 360 Racing was not classified.

Build-up · #8 #17

A tight title race and a fourth Norma

LMP3 arrived at Paul Ricard with four crews inside ten points. United Autosports’ #2 Ligier of Falb and Rayhall and the #17 Ultimate Ligier of Matthieu and Jean-Baptiste Lahaye and Heriau were level on 45 points, the #18 M.Racing – YMR Ligier of Cougnaud, Jung and Ricci had 43, and Mondini’s Eurointernational crew stood eight further back. United’s tally had been dented at the Red Bull Ring, where the #2 crossed the line first but lost the win to a post-race penalty.

The paddock talking point was the Norma M30. Duqueine Engineering swapped its second Ligier for an M30, the #8, with Nicolas Melin joining Beltoise and Légeret, which took the number of Normas in the series to four alongside the Duqueine #7, M.Racing – YMR’s #19 and Oregon Team’s #10. The track itself had changed too: a new chicane on the Mistral Straight, aimed at slowing the 2017-spec LMP2 cars, made LMP3 and GTE cars roughly eight to ten seconds a lap slower than on the old layout.

Practice was tight. RLR MSport’s Kapadia led the first session in the #15 Ligier, just 0.103 seconds clear of 360 Racing’s #6, with M.Racing – YMR’s Norma the first of its kind some seven tenths down. Boyd put United’s #3 on top in the second session, less than five hundredths ahead of Ehrlacher’s Norma, and the first eleven cars were covered by about a second. That session was red-flagged when Légeret put the Duqueine #8 into the barriers at Turn 2, and the #2 stayed in the garage to save its tyres.

Qualifying · #6 #19

Kaiser pole in the final minute

Ross Kaiser took pole for 360 Racing with a 2:02.186 in the #6 Ligier he shares with Woodward and Wells. RLR MSport’s Kapadia had held provisional pole on 2:02.471, but Kaiser moved from third to first in the closing minute to give the class an all-British front row. It was 360’s first pole of the season.

M.Racing – YMR filled the next two places, Cougnaud’s Ligier #18 on 2:02.608 ahead of Ehrlacher’s Norma #19 on 2:02.817. Rayhall put the title-chasing #2 fifth on 2:02.934, with Capo’s Eurointernational #12 less than two hundredths behind, so the top six were separated by less than eight tenths. Behind them Panis Barthez, Oregon, Eurointernational’s #11 and Ultimate’s #17 were all within a second of the pole time, while By Speed Factory’s #5 was almost five seconds off in 2:07.052.

Hour 1 · #2

A slow start for the eventual winner

The four hours began at noon in hot, sunny conditions. Rayhall took the start for the #2 from fifth in class and was down to seventh during the opening hour. The team had gone for a low-downforce set-up that he later called a big mistake, but his stint turned around before the first change of drivers, as he picked his way through the field and handed over to Falb in the lead.

Hours 2–3

Most of the field makes mistakes

With Falb at the front, Inter Europol Competition became the main threat. Smiechowski and Hippe’s Ligier #13 started from 27th on the grid, 15th in class, but ran a trouble-free and quick race in which most of the other teams made errors and faltered. The low-downforce set-up meant the #2 had to be driven cleanly to get the most from it, and the lap times were not spectacular.

Hour 4 · #13

Hippe closes in, then the yellows come

Hippe was charging towards the front and into Falb’s lead, but his charge was interrupted twice by Full Course Yellow periods for stranded LMP3 cars, which left him little time to catch the #2. With 15 minutes to go Falb was told the margin was down to six seconds and that he should push.

The finish · #18

Falb holds on by 2.4 seconds

Falb took the chequered flag 2.418 seconds ahead of Hippe’s #13, which had come from 15th in class on the grid to claim second place. Cougnaud, Jung and Ricci were third in the #18, about 26 seconds from the lead, with Eurointernational’s #11 fourth and Panis Barthez’s #16 of Debard, Gachet and Bean fifth.

Aftermath · #15 #7 #12

United’s second win and a long list of non-finishers

The #2 won on 107 laps, as did the next five finishers: the #13, #18, #11, #16 and Ultimate’s #17, which was sixth. RLR MSport’s #15 and Eurointernational’s #12 were seventh and eighth on 106 laps, then By Speed Factory’s #5 on 103 and Cool Racing’s #4 on 98. Post-race, the #15, #12 and #5 each received 30-second penalties for speeding under Full Course Yellow. Seven cars were not classified, among them pole-sitter #6 with 88 laps, the Duqueine Normas #7 (75 laps) and #8 (42 laps) as well as the #19, #10, #3 and #9. Borga’s #7 nevertheless set the class fastest lap, a 2:04.268.

It was Falb and Rayhall’s second class win of the season after Silverstone, and it left the #2 12 points clear of the #18 with two rounds remaining. The ELMS moves on to Spa-Francorchamps on 24 September.

Griffin passes JMW to give Spirit of Race its first win of the year

Cameron, Griffin and Scott won in Spirit of Race’s #55 Ferrari after Griffin caught and passed JMW’s Fannin late on, while Thiim’s Aston Martin beat the sister Ferrari to third.

Build-up · #77 #99

Six GTE cars and a new Ferrari at JMW

The GTE field was back to six cars after Larbre’s Corvette, a one-off at Monza, did not return. The rest of the field was unchanged apart from JMW, which swapped the retired Ferrari 458 for the 488 GTE it had taken to Le Mans, with Smith the only driver present who had raced it there. JMW had won at Monza in the 458’s final appearance, after TF Sport’s Aston Martin took the opener at Silverstone.

Practice went to the Ferraris and Proton. Cairoli’s Proton Porsche #77 was quickest in FP1 on a 1:29.728, only a few thousandths ahead of Thiim’s TF Sport Aston Martin, before Bertolini’s Spirit of Race #51 Ferrari topped FP2 with a 1:28.407, about three tenths clear of the Porsche. Turner’s Beechdean Aston Martin #99 was third. All running so far had been dry, though the forecast showed scattered storms and a growing chance of rain towards the end of the race.

Qualifying · #51 #55

Spirit of Race take the front row

Spirit of Race locked out the front row. Bertolini set the benchmark on his third flying lap, finished on 1:28.297 and stayed on top to the end of the session. Griffin’s #55 sister car was second on 1:28.476, less than two tenths away, and held the place only in the last moments after a fight with Cairoli, who took third for Proton on 1:28.606.

Cocker’s JMW Ferrari was fourth on 1:28.713 and Thiim fifth for TF Sport on 1:28.862, with Turner sixth for Beechdean on 1:28.906. All six cars were within 0.609 seconds of pole.

Hours 1–3 · #66

Three Ferraris at the front

For most of the race the GTE class was a fight among the three Ferrari 488s: the two Spirit of Race cars and JMW’s #66. The Aston Martins were quiet for much of the distance, and the Proton Porsche, third on the grid, slipped back to finish fifth.

Hour 4 · #90

Fannin’s lead lasts a few laps

The decisive moment came in the last half hour. At JMW’s final stop Fannin took over the #66 and left the pits with a lead of less than five seconds over the #55, now driven by Griffin. Griffin said he could see the JMW as he exited the pits and knew he was quicker. He caught Fannin patiently and passed him in traffic as Fannin struggled to lap one of the LMP3 Normas.

The finish

Thiim takes third late

Once in front, Griffin cruised to the flag well clear of the JMW. The last podium place was settled by the closing laps: Thiim attacked Giorgio Roda’s #51 Ferrari in the final half hour, passed him for third and held it to the flag despite relentless pressure from the Italian.

Aftermath

First win of the season for the #55

Cameron, Griffin and Scott took the first win of the season for the #55 Ferrari, 155 laps. Smith, Fannin and Cocker were second in the JMW Ferrari, ahead of Yoluc, Hankey and Thiim in the TF Sport Aston Martin, with Gianluca Roda, Giorgio Roda and Bertolini fourth in the #51 and the Proton Porsche of Ried, Camathias and Cairoli fifth, all on the winner’s lap. Howard, Gunn and Turner were sixth for Beechdean, one lap down, and Griffin set the class fastest lap on a 1:29.147. The series returns to Paul Ricard on 27 August.
