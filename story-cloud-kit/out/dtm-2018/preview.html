Wittmann wins a chaotic Hungaroring Race 2 as BMW sweeps the podium

Marco Wittmann won from 14th on the grid for BMW as a rain shower, pit-lane crashes and a red flag upended the race, with Timo Glock and Philipp Eng completing a BMW podium.

Build-up · #3

Mercedes lead the way into Sunday

Saturday had gone Mercedes-AMG's way. Paul di Resta won Race 1 from pole ahead of Lucas Auer, Gary Paffett took the lead of the championship on 79 points, seven clear of Timo Glock, and BMW went home from the first race without a point, the best of its six cars being Bruno Spengler in 12th. Sunday promised heat of around 30 degrees, but clouds hung over the circuit and the threat of a shower was never far away. The race was again set for 55 minutes plus one lap, with a compulsory pit stop.

Qualifying · #22 #94 #33

Auer fixes his mistake and heads a five-Mercedes top five

Auer had thrown away a possible pole on Saturday, and on Sunday morning he put it right. His 1m36.717s was 0.632 seconds quicker than di Resta's pole time the day before, and it gave Mercedes-AMG a clean sweep of the top five places. Pascal Wehrlein joined him on the front row 0.063 seconds back, di Resta was third on 1m36.977s, and Edoardo Mortara and Paffett followed, the latter 0.308 seconds off the pace.

René Rast was the best of the rest, sixth for Team Rosberg on 1m37.046s and the only driver to keep the Mercedes cars from taking the first six places, ahead of Daniel Juncadella's Mercedes. He was frustrated nonetheless: he had expected to find more in the final run and felt a lap good enough for pole had slipped away when others improved. Glock lined up eighth, the best of the BMWs, and Nico Müller and Jamie Green completed the top ten.

The rest of the BMW line-up had a difficult time. The six M4s had ended third practice at the bottom of the order, and in qualifying Marco Wittmann could manage only 14th on 1m37.453s, with Philipp Eng 15th, Joel Eriksson 17th and Spengler last, 1.033 seconds off the pole time. Mike Rockenfeller's Audi was 16th. Wittmann admitted afterwards that he had not expected to score from that position.

Opening laps · #16 #51 #99

Glock spins, BMW stops early and the rain arrives

Auer made the best start and kept the lead from Wehrlein, and the Mercedes cars held the first six places. Further back it went wrong for Glock. He was spun around in Turn 2 after contact with Müller and dropped from eighth to last, and he came in at the end of the first lap for his mandatory stop, together with Eriksson and Rockenfeller. Glock said afterwards that he had stopped early precisely because the spin had put him at the back. Augusto Farfus followed a lap later.

The decisions looked mundane for a few laps. Rain began to fall at around lap four, light at first and then heavier, and the track turned greasy, which allowed Wehrlein to sweep past Auer at Turn 1 and take the lead. Farfus came back in for rain tyres, the first to do so, and several others started to turn in towards the pit lane as the water worsened.

Pit-lane crashes · #48 #7 #15

Three cars slide into the pit lane and the race is stopped

At the end of lap six Auer turned in to change from slick tyres to wet ones. The concrete of the working lane was awash and as slick as ice, and the Mercedes could not be stopped: it slid into the pit building and trapped marshals between the car and the wall. A lap later Mortara had the same problem and hit his own pit gantry, which fell onto his car, while Spengler's BMW ran into mechanics outside his garage.

The safety car had been deployed when the ambulance entered the pit lane, but several drivers were already on their way in, and the race was red-flagged after eight laps once the third car had slid. A statement from the organisers said one marshal had severe leg injuries and two more people had moderate injuries, none of them in a critical condition. The marshal was flown to hospital.

The stoppage lasted about half an hour. The sun came out while the cars waited on the grid, and by the time the race was ready to resume the track was close to dry.

Restart · #2 #23 #53

The dry track turns the order upside down

The race restarted behind the safety car with about 28 minutes on the clock. Auer, di Resta and Mortara had all stopped before the flag, so the Mercedes of Wehrlein, Paffett and Juncadella led, followed by Müller and Green, and these five still had a compulsory stop to make. The BMWs of Eng, Wittmann, Glock and Eriksson, along with Rockenfeller, had all made their stops already and so ran at the head of the virtual order.

That meant the five leaders had to come in again while the cars behind them did not. Paffett, whose team had left wet tyres on his car, dropped through the field like a stone and was passed by almost everyone, at one point down to 17th, and Rast was spun at the restart. Juncadella passed Wehrlein for the lead, built a lead of nearly three seconds and then stopped with eight minutes left, but a slow stop dropped him to 17th, and he finished 14th.

The finish · #11 #25

Wittmann takes the lead from Eng and wins from 14th

Wittmann had passed Eng by then, and once the five leaders had made their stops he took over the effective lead and went on to win after 26 laps. The two-time champion took the flag in 1h17m50.129s, 5.870 seconds clear of Glock, who had worked his way back from last place after his early spin. Eng completed an all-BMW podium 13.457 seconds behind, and held off a late attack by Rockenfeller by 0.480 seconds.

Di Resta climbed through the field in the closing laps to finish fifth, the best of the Mercedes drivers, and Eriksson was sixth. It was Wittmann's first win of 2018 and came the day after all six BMWs had finished outside the top ten. Paffett, still struggling after his tyre problem, finished 18th, which cost him the championship lead.

Aftermath

Three drivers excluded, Glock back in front

BMW Team RMG dedicated the win to everyone hurt in the pit lane, and Wittmann said his team had called him in on exactly the right lap as the rain began, in line with a plan to run until it came. Glock and Eng both pointed to the timing of their stops and said they had been lucky that the red flag arrived just after they left the pits. BMW's motorsport staff called the weekend a roller-coaster, and said the pace had been better on Sunday than on Saturday. Rockenfeller said the result was secondary after the images from the pit lane.

Mercedes left Sunday almost empty-handed after locking out the front of the grid. Team principal Ulrich Fritz called that bitter after such a strong weekend, and said that in hindsight showing the red flag earlier would have been right; he added that Mercedes had decided before the race not to stop any driver early, to give each of them the same chance of winning. Paffett was angrier, saying it had been “the most embarrassing DTM race I've been in”, and arguing that the race should have been stopped after Auer's crash. The organisers' response was that the extent of the first injury was not yet clear, and that the safety car had been used to close the pit lane. Audi's Dieter Gass praised Rockenfeller's fourth place, but conceded that after seeing who won, Audi might have done better to bring a car in early.

Hours after the finish the stewards excluded Auer, Mortara and Spengler for breaking the rule that requires drivers to slow enough to stop safely at their garage without endangering others. Before the decision Spengler had been seventh, Auer ninth and Mortara tenth. Mortara and Spengler also received a 10-place grid penalty for the next race, suspended for two race weekends, because yellow flags had been waved and their teams had warned them by radio. Wittmann said that none of the drivers deserved the blame, and Loïc Duval called it “the most slippery pitlane I have ever had”.

In the table, Glock regained the lead from Paffett and went into the break on 90 points to Paffett's 79, a gap of 11, while di Resta stayed third. The DTM next went to the Norisring in Nuremberg on 22–24 June.

Rast sweeps the Nürburgring weekend with Race 2 win

René Rast won again from pole, by 2.739 seconds from Paul di Resta, completing a perfect weekend for Audi as a slow pit stop dropped Gary Paffett to fifth.

Build-up

A new points leader after Saturday

Saturday had belonged to Audi and to René Rast, who won Race 1 from pole by 0.862 seconds after Bruno Spengler's BMW closed in over the last laps. The other big story was behind the podium: Paul di Resta was spun by Philipp Eng's BMW, finished last without points and handed the championship lead to his Mercedes-AMG team-mate Gary Paffett, 194 points to 186.

Sunday brought a chance for both to answer. The minimum tyre pressures introduced for the weekend were still in force, the weather stayed dry, and Rast, who had already won the first race, was one qualifying lap away from a second pole.

Qualifying · #33 #2 #25

Rast pole again, by six thousandths

Qualifying was again dry and sunny, and again it ended with Rast on top. His 1m22.140s was just 0.006 seconds quicker than Paffett's 1m22.146s, with Eng third on 1m22.159s and Timo Glock fourth on 1m22.203s, so that the first four were covered by only 0.063 seconds. Rast said that when you are on a run you are on a run, after a long dry spell at the start of the year, and Audi's Dieter Gass admitted he had not expected the pole to be repeated.

Lucas Auer and Pascal Wehrlein were fifth and sixth on identical times of 1m22.331s. Mike Rockenfeller was seventh, the next Audi, ahead of Spengler, Marco Wittmann and Joel Eriksson. Di Resta could only qualify 11th on 1m22.566s, with Nico Müller 12th and Augusto Farfus 13th. Daniel Juncadella was 14th and Jamie Green 15th, while Loïc Duval, Robin Frijns and Edoardo Mortara filled the last three places, Mortara 18th on 1m22.925s.

Opening laps · #22 #53

Auer jumps to second, Glock hits him

Rast kept the lead at the start, while Auer made the best getaway of all, going from fifth to second and trying a move around the outside that Rast closed by cutting across his exit. Paffett and Eng both lost places in the opening moments. Green's race changed at the end of the first lap when contact left him with tyre damage and he went in for the mandatory stop immediately.

On lap two Glock dived down the inside of the Mercedes at the first corner, hit the side of Auer's car and spun him. The stewards gave Glock a drive-through penalty, he finished 16th, and Auer dropped down the order. With Auer gone, Paffett took over as Rast's closest challenger and used DRS to close in, but Rast defended and eased clear.

Pit stops · #47 #11

Paffett's slow stop opens the door

The stops decided the places behind the leader. Rast came in early, and Paffett's stop went wrong: a problem with the right-rear wheel cost him around five seconds and dropped him behind di Resta and the BMWs of Wittmann, Eriksson and Eng. His deficit to Rast grew to about twelve seconds, having been a podium certainty only moments earlier.

Spengler stopped a lap after di Resta, and on cold tyres he could not hold off the Mercedes, which took second place. Di Resta then fought off Wittmann for the position, and Paffett began a recovery, passing Eng and Eriksson within a handful of laps.

The finish · #3 #7 #94

Di Resta closes to 2.739 seconds

Rast's tyres wore away over the closing stage, as they had on Saturday, and a gap of around ten seconds to di Resta shrank to 2.739 seconds by the flag after 40 laps in 57m17.440s. He said the last part of the race was a fight for survival. Wittmann took third, 3.439 seconds back, with Spengler fourth, Paffett fifth, 7.177 seconds behind, and Eriksson sixth.

Farfus was seventh, Eng started third but finished eighth, and Wehrlein was ninth. Frijns climbed from 17th to tenth and Mortara from 18th to 11th. Müller dropped from the edge of the top ten to 14th, and Green was 15th. Glock was 16th, Juncadella was out after 38 laps, and Auer's race ended after 33. Paffett set the fastest lap, a 1m23.875s on lap 23.

Aftermath

Rast makes history, Paffett's lead cut to two points

Rast became the first driver in DTM history to take both poles and both wins in a weekend, the full 56 points on offer. He said it was hard to believe, a weekend that nobody had managed before in the series, and that leading looked easy from the outside but was mentally tough when his tyres were gone and a rival was closing. Gass said he was speechless and had not expected it, given where Audi had started the season, and that Rast's qualifying had been the foundation for both wins. Rast added that the sweep felt similar to winning a championship. Gass later argued that Audi's decision not to ask for rule concessions had been justified, because the car could now win on its own merits after a difficult start to the year.

The weekend also changed the shape of the championship. Paffett kept the lead on 206 points but di Resta, who limited the damage with second place, was only two behind on 204. Rast climbed to third on 149, the best non-Mercedes driver and 57 points off the lead, with Mortara fourth on 138 and Wittmann fifth on 137.

Paffett congratulated Rast and was blunt about his own result. He said the slow stop was frustrating because he had been comfortably second and then had to fight for fifth, costing the team a lot of points. BMW's Jens Marquardt called Rast's form fantastic. Mercedes team principal Ulrich Fritz, who called it stellar, said the pace had been better than on Saturday, that Paffett had driven well to limit the damage and that di Resta's second place was worth a lot, and the team would analyse both races to find answers before the next round.

That round is at the Red Bull Ring in Spielberg, two weeks later on 21 to 23 September, followed by the finale at Hockenheim on 13 and 14 October. Only two rounds remained, and Rast, 57 points back, would need a great deal to go his way.

Di Resta beats Auer to win Hungaroring Race 1 for Mercedes

Paul di Resta turned pole position into victory at the Hungaroring, finishing 0.587 seconds ahead of Mercedes team-mate Lucas Auer after the two swapped the lead through the pit-stop phase.

Build-up · #16 #2 #33

A one-point title fight leaves Germany

The DTM left Germany for the first time in 2018 when it reached the Hungaroring on 1–3 June, and it did so with the championship balanced on a single point. BMW's Timo Glock had defended the lead at the Lausitzring and arrived on 72 points, one clear of Gary Paffett on 71, with Pascal Wehrlein third on 39. Mercedes-AMG, who would leave the series at the end of the year, had won three of the first four races, while Audi had struggled badly at Hockenheim and the Lausitzring and travelled to Hungary without the favourite's tag it had carried the year before. BMW had put a car on the podium in each of the first four races, and its motorsport director, Jens Marquardt, wanted more of the same in Hungary. Reigning champion René Rast was back in the car after missing the Lausitzring Sunday race following his heavy crash there.

The Hungaroring is a twisty, 4.381-kilometre layout in a natural basin, where the heat builds quickly and the tyres suffer from the constant braking and acceleration. With 14 corners and little room to pass, the best chance to overtake comes at the tight right-hander at the end of the 800-metre start-finish straight, which put a premium on grid position and on the timing of the compulsory pit stop.

There was an unusual visitor in the BMW garage: Alex Zanardi spent the weekend shadowing the team to learn how a DTM operation works, ahead of a guest drive in a modified M4 at Misano in August. Each race on the weekend was scheduled for 55 minutes plus one lap, and Saturday's would run to 34 laps.

Qualifying · #3 #51 #22

Di Resta takes pole after Auer runs wide on his last lap

Rain fell before Saturday morning's practice and made for a slippery rehearsal, but the track had dried by the time the 18 cars went out for qualifying in bright sunshine. Auer set the early marker with a 1m37.564s that put him six tenths clear of Paffett, until the Audis, who had held back their first runs, came out and Nico Müller beat him by a little under a tenth.

The session then went quiet before a flurry of improvements in the final minute. Paul di Resta cut the benchmark to 1m37.349s, a tenth faster than Müller's best, and Auer was on course to beat the Scot after setting the quickest first sector. He ran wide at Turn 9, however, and could not improve, which left di Resta on pole for Mercedes-AMG Motorsport Remus with Müller's Abt Audi alongside, 0.135 seconds back, and Auer third on 1m37.564s.

René Rast held fourth for Team Rosberg on 1m37.598s, ahead of the Mercedes pair of Edoardo Mortara and Daniel Juncadella, who were separated by two thousandths of a second. Jamie Green slipped to seventh, and Augusto Farfus was the best of the BMWs in eighth, more than half a second off the pace. Paffett could manage only ninth, while Glock, the points leader, qualified 16th after getting the timing of his second run wrong. Audi had three cars in the top seven, a clear step forward after the first two rounds.

Opening laps · #53 #7 #25

BMW and Green gamble on a first-lap stop

Di Resta led away cleanly, and Müller kept second place after a brief battle with Auer. The Mercedes driver got his nose ahead at Turn 2, only for the Audi to take the place back a corner later. Behind them Mortara went past Rast for fourth on the opening lap.

The most aggressive strategies came straight away. At the end of the first lap Green, Bruno Spengler and Philipp Eng all dived into the pits for their mandatory stops, and a lap later Glock and Joel Eriksson did the same, so that four of the six BMWs were on fresh tyres almost before the race had begun. A few spots of rain were reported shortly afterwards, but the shower never developed and the hot asphalt dried the drops almost at once.

Up front, di Resta and Müller opened a lead of around a second over the rest. Auer was the first of the leaders to stop, coming in on lap six together with Farfus, and that choice would shape the afternoon.

Pit stops

Auer's earlier stop pays off, then di Resta strikes back

Di Resta waited three more laps and did not get the best of stops, and although he came out still in front, Auer, on warmer tyres, passed him easily at Turn 2. Müller changed tyres on the following lap and also rejoined ahead of Auer, but the Mercedes driver found a way past him as well. Di Resta later admitted that the stop was not the team's best and that Müller's must have been quicker.

The Scot then passed Müller after a short fight, and that tussle let Auer pull out a gap of more than two seconds. Auer said he had pushed very hard after his stop to undercut both of them, and that he then had to look after his used tyres when di Resta came back at him.

Strategies · #4 #28 #99

Late stoppers and early gamblers sort the midfield

Elsewhere the order was settled by a mix of strategies. Mortara, who had stayed out longest of the front runners, stopped on lap 14 and Rast a lap after, and the reigning champion took fourth place from the Italian with a forceful move once both were on new tyres. Paffett, who did not stop until lap 16, climbed from ninth on the grid to sixth. Robin Frijns and Loïc Duval, together with Mike Rockenfeller, ran far longer than the rest on their first set of tyres, and the first two had the best pace of anyone in the closing stages, moving up to seventh and eighth from 14th and 11th on the grid.

The finish · #48

Auer lets di Resta by and the Mercedes one-two is sealed

With the race past its midpoint, di Resta closed in on his team-mate and went by before Turn 2. From there the lead was never in danger for the last 15 minutes or so, and di Resta took the flag 0.587 seconds clear after 34 laps and 57m13.199s. It was his first win of 2018 and Mercedes' fourth in five races this season. Auer, who also set the fastest lap, a 1m38.812s on lap 10, completed the one-two for the Stuttgart brand.

Müller held on for the final podium place, 2.236 seconds behind the winner and his first top-three finish of the year, with Rast 0.662 seconds further back after a close fight in the last laps. Mortara was fifth, 6.695 seconds adrift, and Paffett sixth, 12.166 seconds behind di Resta. That result was enough for Paffett to retake the lead of the championship, on 79 points, seven clear of Glock, with di Resta up to third on 56.

For BMW it was a day to forget. The best of the six M4s was Spengler in 12th, Glock was 14th, and no BMW scored a point after the early-stop tactics failed to pay off. The team said that the balance of the cars had not been right in qualifying or in the race, and promised a big step for Sunday.

Paffett leads a Mercedes 1-2-3-4 at Zandvoort

Gary Paffett won Race 1 at Zandvoort from pole for Mercedes-AMG, 1.422 seconds clear of Paul di Resta, after a safety car undid René Rast's late-stop strategy and dropped the Audi to 17th.

Build-up · #33

The halfway point of a Mercedes-led season

Zandvoort hosted the fifth round of the 2018 DTM season on Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 July, two races that took the championship to its halfway point before the summer break. Gary Paffett arrived as the points leader, and Mercedes-AMG had won five of the first eight races: Paffett at Hockenheim and the Lausitzring, Edoardo Mortara at the Lausitzring and the Norisring, and Paul di Resta in Hungary.

BMW had taken the other three, through Timo Glock at Hockenheim and Marco Wittmann in Hungary and again at the Norisring, where Mortara had won on the Saturday and Wittmann on the Sunday. Audi had not won at all, and René Rast, the reigning champion, was still waiting for his first victory of the year.

Strategy mattered as much as qualifying pace. Every car on Hankook tyres had to make a mandatory pit stop during the race, and when to take it was a choice each team made for itself. Race 1 ran for 34 laps on the Saturday, and the question was whether anyone could beat the Mercedes front block, or whether the timing of the stops would shuffle the order.

Qualifying · #2 #94 #48

Mercedes fills the first two rows

Paffett took pole with 1:30.778, 0.060 seconds ahead of team-mate Pascal Wehrlein, and Paul di Resta (1:30.980) and Lucas Auer (1:31.063) made it four Mercedes across the first two rows. Auer said he was proudest of his qualifying, because the first set of tyres had worked so well.

Wehrlein was not happy with second place. He said Paffett had spoiled a lap when he came up behind the pole-sitter and was not waved through, and that he had been three tenths up before losing four over the final two corners. Paffett replied that it had happened on his out-lap, that it had not really affected the timed lap, and that he would talk to Wehrlein in person.

Glock (1:31.217) and Wittmann (1:31.337) were the best BMWs in fifth and sixth. Robin Frijns, racing in front of his home crowd, was the fastest Audi in seventh on 1:31.375, ahead of Nico Müller, with Rast ninth on 1:31.461. Mortara, a championship contender, started last after a broken brake-balance adjuster; his 1:31.967 left him 18th.

Opening laps · #4

Stops reshuffle the Mercedes four

Paffett kept the lead into the first corner with Wehrlein on his tail, and the four Mercedes held their grid order. Frijns passed Wittmann and Glock to take fifth and then leaned on the Mercedes quartet. Wehrlein hounded Paffett for the first eight laps before coming in to make his mandatory stop.

It went wrong for him. A wheel stuck and cost him several seconds, enough to drop him behind Di Resta, who had run third from the start. Paffett pitted a lap later without any problem and came out still in front. Frijns had taken his own stop on lap five, and by then more than half of the field had changed tyres.

Rast was the exception. The Audi driver, who had started ninth, stayed out on his first set of tyres while the others stopped around him, and with clear track he was lapping quickly enough to pull away from the leading Mercedes trio.

Safety car · #51 #3

A puncture ruins Rast's gamble

By lap 13 Rast led Paffett by almost 24 seconds and was the only driver yet to stop, which looked like a route to a very good result. On lap 19 that advantage vanished. Müller's Audi went off into the tyre barrier at the Gerlachbocht behind the paddock, ending his race after 18 laps.

The safety car caught Rast before he could pit, and the field was bunched behind him. Recovering the Audi took a long time, and the race restarted in IndyCar style with Rast still to stop, leading Paffett and Di Resta. Wehrlein tried to attack Di Resta into Tarzanbocht and the two Mercedes touched, and on the exit of the corner Auer moved ahead of Wehrlein to take third.

The finish · #22 #16 #23

Paffett back in front, Rast down to 17th

Rast led the restart laps but had to stop close to the end, and he rejoined 17th, 39.538 seconds behind the winner. That handed the lead back to Paffett, who stayed in front through the restart run and took the flag 1.422 seconds clear. Auer was third, 1.865 behind, and Wehrlein fourth, 2.290 back, which completed a Mercedes 1-2-3-4.

It was Paffett's third win of the season and the 23rd of his DTM career. Frijns finished fifth, 2.697 seconds from the winner, as the best of the Audis and the best non-Mercedes. Glock was the leading BMW in sixth, ahead of Wittmann, Farfus, Eriksson and Jamie Green, who took tenth. Mortara could make little progress from the back and finished 13th.

Paffett now had 127 points to Di Resta's 106, a lead of 21, with Glock third on 100. Rast, who had looked on course for a big result, was left with a 17th-place finish.
