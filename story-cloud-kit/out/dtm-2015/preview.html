Félix da Costa wins from pole as BMW take the top five

António Félix da Costa took his first DTM victory from pole at Zandvoort by 0.473 seconds from Augusto Farfus, as BMW filled the top five and Jamie Green's championship lead shrank to five points.

Build-up · #1

A Saturday that belonged to BMW

Race 2 on Sunday 12 July followed a Saturday that had belonged to BMW. Marco Wittmann won Race 1 from second on the grid and the manufacturer took the first seven places for the first time in the series, with António Félix da Costa second on his first DTM podium. Jamie Green, the championship leader, ran into the gravel while sixth and scored nothing, but still led Mattias Ekström by 11 points, 81 to 70, with Pascal Wehrlein third on 68. Sunday's race was longer at 60 minutes plus one lap, with a mandatory pit stop, and it was run under cloud at about 18 degrees.

Qualifying · #13 #7 #17

Félix da Costa takes his first pole

The morning qualifying session repeated Saturday's pattern. António Félix da Costa, who had never started a DTM race from pole position, lapped the 4.307-kilometre circuit in 1:30.483 for the first pole of his career, nearly eight tenths quicker than Farfus's pole time the day before. Bruno Spengler was second for MTEK, 0.058 seconds behind, with Augusto Farfus third on 1:30.623 and Wittmann fourth on 1:30.650. Each of BMW's four teams had one car in the top four, and Félix da Costa said afterwards that the lighter BMWs let the team get the tyres working at Zandvoort.

Miguel Molina was fifth in 1:30.674, the best of the Audis, and Wehrlein sixth in 1:30.809 as the leading Mercedes. Timo Glock was seventh, followed by Daniel Juncadella, Gary Paffett and Christian Vietoris, with Maxime Martin 11th, Edoardo Mortara 12th and Martin Tomczyk 13th. The championship contenders fared badly: Robert Wickens was 15th, Ekström 16th and Green 18th on 1:31.239, while Paul di Resta was 23rd and Lucas Auer, the Mercedes rookie, last of the 24 in 1:31.719.

Opening laps · #36 #94

A clean getaway at the front

Félix da Costa made a perfect start from pole and was clear of the field before the first corner. Spengler, who said he had struggled with his clutch all weekend, got away less well and had to defend hard against Farfus and Wittmann at the first corner, but he kept second place. Wehrlein also had a poor start and fell to ninth.

Behind the leaders the field soon got untidy. On the second lap Martin, third the day before, was hit by Tambay's Audi and dropped to the back of the field, where he would stay and finish 17th. Tambay faced no penalty for the contact.

Pit stops · #16 #18 #22

Spengler loses second in the pits

The mandatory stops began at around the 20-minute mark. Glock was the first of the front-runners to stop, and the early stop lifted him from seventh to fourth. Spengler stopped before the leaders, but his crew had to hold him in the box for a moment to avoid an unsafe release, and when the leaders pitted Farfus came out ahead of him in second. Félix da Costa kept the lead through the stops.

Wittmann came out of the pits behind Lucas Auer, whose Mercedes had not yet stopped. Shortly afterwards Wittmann forced his way past and turned the Mercedes around in the process, and the Austrian went off the road.

Contact in the pack · #77 #48 #12

Bonnet, airborne Mercedes and a drive-through

The disturbance in the pack after the stops led to a series of contacts. Tomczyk, who had gained five places at the start, was squeezed and pushed from behind in the bunch, touched Molina and lost the fastenings of his bonnet, which lifted and blocked his view. The BMW retired with damage.

On lap 21 Mortara and Juncadella touched at Turn 6. Juncadella, who said he had been pushed wide, hit a kerb, went airborne and landed hard, and he complained of back pain afterwards; neither car continued. Elsewhere Tom Blomqvist turned Wickens around at Tarzan, and Blomqvist was given a drive-through penalty. Blomqvist finished 18th, Wickens 19th.

The finish · #5 #53

Félix da Costa holds off Farfus

Félix da Costa won in 1:02:07.601 after 39 laps, his first DTM victory and one day after his first podium. Farfus stayed on his gearbox to the flag but never found a way past, finishing 0.473 seconds behind. Félix da Costa said he had kept his distance before Turn 1, because that was the only good place to pass. Spengler was third, 1.091 seconds behind, Glock fourth, 1.710, and Wittmann fifth after a hard race, 4.975 seconds from the winner. Glock also set the fastest lap, a 1:33.635 on lap 15.

Wehrlein recovered from his poor start to take sixth, 10.954 seconds back, as the best driver outside BMW. Ekström was seventh from 16th on the grid, ahead of Vietoris, Adrien Tambay and Paffett, who had run in the top ten early before a slow stop. Green was a quiet 13th, one place ahead of di Resta, a third race in a row without points for the championship leader, and his advantage had shrunk to five points.

Aftermath · #6

BMW celebrate, the middle of the field complains

BMW Motorsport director Jens Marquardt called the weekend very positive and said it had all come together, but warned that the competition was much stronger than it looked and that the margins between the manufacturers were tiny from circuit to circuit. Félix da Costa said he was speechless after a fantastic weekend, with the pole and the win coming a day after his first podium. Schnitzer team principal Charly Lamm spoke of a great relief at standing on the top step, and said Félix da Costa had earned it.

Spengler said his car had been perfect but that the wait in the pit box for the safe release had cost him second place. Wittmann said he had lost out at the start behind his BMW colleagues and then had hard but fair duels with Molina; the contact with Auer damaged his car, he said, though his speed stayed good, and the gap to the front was too big to close. Martin called the second-lap contact an unfortunate situation in a hard-fought race and kept the memory of his Saturday podium. Tomczyk, who retired for a second race in a row, said he had been sandwiched in a bunch of cars and pushed from behind before the contact with Molina.

The racing in the midfield was the talk afterwards. Wickens called it a terrible race and said he had been touched by three Audi drivers within two laps. Juncadella said some drivers can only touch rather than race properly, and that this had happened with Mortara many times before. Former Mercedes motorsport chief Norbert Haug, working as a television expert, thought the pace was too rough and compared it to a pub brawl in which everyone joins once one person starts; he said the Mortara and Juncadella incident should be raised at the next drivers' briefing, and he questioned why Tambay had not been penalised.

In the championship Green still led on 81 points, but Wehrlein and Ekström were now level on 76, five behind. Mortara was fourth on 58, Wickens fifth on 57 and Spengler sixth on 52 after his podium. BMW had won both races of the weekend and filled all six podium places. The next round was the Red Bull Ring in Spielberg, Austria, from 31 July to 2 August, the fifth of the nine race weekends.

Ekström wins wet Hockenheim Race 2 as Paffett charges to third

Mattias Ekström won a wet Race 2 at Hockenheim for Audi by 7.361 seconds from Edoardo Mortara, who took the points lead, while Gary Paffett climbed from 23rd to third.

Build-up · #53 #94 #10

A busy Saturday sets up Sunday

Saturday’s opener had gone to Jamie Green, who led from pole for Audi Sport Team Rosberg ahead of Pascal Wehrlein and Paul di Resta and ended Saturday at the top of the standings with 25 points, seven clear of Wehrlein. It was also a destructive race: eight cars retired, and Timo Scheider hurt his wrist in a first-lap accident, though he said there was no fracture and expected to be able to race again.

Sunday’s race was the longer one, an hour of racing with a compulsory tyre change, and rain returned to the area overnight. Mechanics had until the morning warm-up to get the damaged cars ready.

Qualifying · #99 #17 #2

A drying session ends with Rockenfeller on pole

The morning warm-up was run in the wet, and qualifying began on a damp track. Drivers started on wet tyres and switched to slicks as the surface dried, and the pole position changed hands between ten different drivers as lap times fell by about ten seconds across the 20 minutes. Di Resta led the way onto slicks, and Ekström went top with less than a minute left.

Mike Rockenfeller beat him on the last lap. His 1m34.516s in the #99 Audi Sport Team Phoenix car took pole, with Miguel Molina’s Audi Sport Team Abt Sportsline car only 0.030s behind on 1m34.546s and Ekström third-fastest on 1m34.721s, making it an Audi 1-2-3 on the timesheet. Molina nevertheless started fifth, which promoted Ekström to the front row, with Marco Wittmann’s BMW Team RMG car third on the grid as the best of the BMWs. Edoardo Mortara lined up fourth.

Paffett was eighth-quickest and the best of the Mercedes drivers, but his Euronics/BWT car was sent to the back of the grid because the rear-wing spacer that blocks the DRS flap during qualifying was not fitted; he started 23rd, with Tambay’s Audi last. Green was 17th, Wehrlein 15th and di Resta tenth, so none of Saturday’s podium finishers started in the top eight.

Opening laps · #5 #1 #22

Ekström and Wittmann trade the lead

Rockenfeller made a poor start and Ekström went past him into the first corner, but the lead did not stay with him for long. At the hairpin on the opening lap Wittmann slipped past him to take first, and it took Ekström until lap four to get the place back. In the dry phase he stretched his advantage to somewhere between two and three seconds, while Wittmann, Mortara, Tomczyk and Rockenfeller scrapped over the places behind.

Lucas Auer never took part. He crashed his Euronics/BWT Mercedes on the way to the grid and was a non-starter.

Rain and pit stops · #48 #36 #27

Mortara’s early stop costs Ekström the lead

Around lap 11 the rain arrived. Mortara and Wittmann were among those who came in for wet tyres a lap earlier than the leader, and it paid off: Ekström stopped one lap later, and Mortara emerged in front. Maxime Martin also had a busy stop, serving a drive-through for an unsafe release in the pit lane.

Ekström was the quickest man on the damp track even before his own stop. He passed Mortara for the lead on lap 17 at the hairpin and then pulled away in the rain. Behind them, the wet conditions produced overtaking all down the order, and Adrien Tambay’s Audi was out after 12 laps.

The finish · #77 #3 #13

Paffett completes a climb to the podium

Ekström took the chequered flag after 35 laps in 1:02:43.856, 7.361s ahead of Mortara, completing an Audi win in both races of the weekend. The third podium place went to Paffett, who had started 23rd and made up the places with a string of passes in the wet. He took Tomczyk at the hairpin in the closing stages and finished 8.171s behind the winner, only 0.810s behind Mortara.

Tomczyk was fourth, the best BMW, 10.6s behind Paffett, with Wittmann fifth and Rockenfeller sixth after the pair had run in the lead group in the dry. Robert Wickens was seventh, Wehrlein eighth, Bruno Spengler ninth and Glock tenth. Green could only improve from 17th to 13th, and di Resta finished 22nd and last of the classified runners after Nico Müller turned him around while the two fought over a place.

Aftermath · #7 #16

Audi sweeps the opener; Mortara leads

Ekström said he had enjoyed the race from start to finish and that experience counts for more in the rain, when conditions were difficult for everyone. Stopping a lap later than Mortara, he said, had not mattered much to the outcome, because the car was perfect. The win completed an Audi sweep of the two opening races.

Paffett was disappointed at being sent to the back after qualifying for what he called a small thing, but said the team’s strategy was perfect and that his car was far faster than the others in the rain. After a weekend in which Wehrlein and di Resta had finished second and third on Saturday, he said Audi held the advantage but Mercedes had strong race pace and could fight. Mercedes left Hockenheim with three podium finishes across the two races.

BMW took no podium, though four of its M4s finished in the top ten in both races. Tomczyk and Wittmann were the most successful BMW drivers with 12 points each, and the team’s motorsport director praised Tomczyk for a faultless race. Wittmann lost pace once the rain arrived, and Tomczyk said he had struggled with tyre pressure in the wet.

In the standings Mortara took the lead with 30 points, ahead of Green and Ekström on 25 each, Wehrlein on 22, Rockenfeller on 18 and di Resta on 15. Fifteen of the 24 drivers had scored points in the opening weekend, with 16 races to come. The next round is at the Lausitzring on 30–31 May.

Wehrlein wins wet-dry Norisring Race 1 as Mercedes take the top four

Pascal Wehrlein won Race 1 at the Norisring by 0.356 seconds for Mercedes-AMG, starting fifth on slicks on a drying track, as Mercedes took the top four places and ended Audi’s winning run.

Build-up · #48 #5

Audi arrives as the hunted at Mercedes’ street circuit

The DTM reached the Norisring for the third weekend of 2015, a season in which every event carries two races, with Audi in the unfamiliar position of defending a perfect record. The manufacturer had won all four races so far, three of them through Jamie Green of Audi Sport Team Rosberg, who arrived leading the championship on 75 points. Edoardo Mortara and Mattias Ekström shared second on 58, while Pascal Wehrlein, the 20-year-old Mercedes Formula 1 reserve driver, was down on 32.

The Norisring is the only street circuit on the DTM calendar, a 2.3-kilometre loop around the Dutzendteich lake in Nuremberg, and it has traditionally been strong ground for Mercedes. The weekend therefore pitted Audi’s momentum against Mercedes’ form there, with a further complication in the rules. Drivers who score well must load extra performance weight after each event, so the Audis that had been winning were carrying the most ballast.

BMW, meanwhile, had yet to win in 2015 and came to Nuremberg looking for its first podium of the year. With Mortara and Ekström both well placed in the standings and Green carrying one of the heaviest cars in the field, Saturday’s race looked like a test of how much the weight would cost on a street circuit.

Qualifying · #8 #2 #53

Vietoris takes pole on a drying track as Green splits the Mercedes

Qualifying on Saturday 27 June was run on a surface that was drying out, with drivers starting on wet tyres and switching to slicks as the session went on. Mercedes made the most of it. Christian Vietoris took pole in the gooix/Original-Teile Mercedes-AMG #8 with a 50.640, only 0.039 seconds clear of Gary Paffett’s Euronics/BWT car #2, and five Mercedes-AMG cars made the top ten.

Green broke up the silver wall by qualifying third in the Rosberg Audi #53 on 50.687, 0.047 seconds off pole, and said afterwards that the car’s weight made the result a good one. Paul di Resta was fourth on 50.751, 0.111 behind, with Wehrlein fifth on 50.807. Mercedes DTM team boss Ulrich Fritz called the top-ten picture a dream result for the brand but cautioned that anything could still happen in the race.

Augusto Farfus was the best of the BMWs in sixth on 50.876, more than two tenths from Vietoris, ahead of Robert Wickens, Marco Wittmann, Miguel Molina and Martin Tomczyk. Bruno Spengler could manage only 13th in the MTEK BMW and his team-mate Timo Glock 15th, Mortara was 16th, and Ekström was a long way back in 21st on 51.404. Timo Scheider was last of the 24 cars on 51.539, less than a second from pole.

Opening laps · #22 #17 #99

A shower before the start turns tyre choice into a lottery

Heavy rain fell shortly before the start and the track, which had largely dried in qualifying, was wet again on the grid. The shower had stopped by the time the lights went out, leaving every team to guess. Hankook’s motorsport chief described the choice as a lottery. Seven drivers started on wet tyres. Mercedes leaned towards wets, though Wehrlein started on slicks, while Green, Audi’s best hope, was one of those on slicks.

The wet-tyre runners gained at once. Wickens and di Resta passed Green, who was pushed back to 11th, and Mike Rockenfeller, who had started 22nd on wets, was soon pushing towards the top ten. Behind them the first corner brought chaos: Lucas Auer’s Mercedes tagged Molina’s Audi on the exit of Turn 1, leaving both cars across the track, and the safety car came out for the opening minutes.

When racing resumed, Vietoris, Paffett, Wickens, di Resta, Tomczyk, Juncadella and Rockenfeller drew away at the head of the field as the track dried, with Daniel Juncadella up from 11th on the grid and Rockenfeller from 22nd.

Slicks against wets · #94 #3 #12

Wehrlein bides his time, then inherits the lead

Wehrlein, running eighth and some 30 seconds behind the leaders, was the best of the slick-tyre starters, and his patience was the story of the race. As the track dried, the advantage of the wet-tyre runners disappeared, and roughly a third of the way through the 46 laps Paffett became the first of the wet-tyre runners to come in for slicks. The rest followed within a lap. All of them rejoined behind Wehrlein, who was now in the lead with a buffer approaching four seconds.

Further back, the stop cycle brought a hard clash at the chicane. After di Resta’s pit stop Spengler dived down the inside of the Mercedes, and Green tried to follow him through, only for the gap to close and the two cars to make contact. Di Resta retired with right-rear damage after 17 laps. The stewards decided against any further action and treated it as a racing incident. Maximilian Götz in the Petronas Mercedes also retired after 17 laps, while the BMWs of Maxime Martin and Tom Blomqvist had already retired.

The finish · #6 #7 #77

Four Mercedes in four places, with 0.356 seconds to spare

The final laps were a chase on fresher rubber. Wickens, Paffett and Vietoris, now on slicks that had been fitted later than Wehrlein’s, reeled in the leader lap after lap, and the gap that had once stood near four seconds came down to 0.4 with eight minutes left. Wickens then worked past Vietoris for second and Paffett followed him through, but nobody made a serious attempt on the lead. Wehrlein said his race had been won in the first half, when he had to keep the car on track on slicks, and that he only had to avoid mistakes afterwards; he did not think the others would have caught him.

He crossed the line after 46 laps in 41:02.170, 0.356 seconds ahead of Wickens, with Paffett 0.742 behind and Vietoris, the pole-sitter, 1.362 back in fourth. It gave Mercedes the first four places, and Audi lost for the first time in 2015 after four straight wins. Spengler led the BMWs home in fifth, 2.784 seconds from the winner, ahead of Tomczyk, with Green seventh from third on the grid after his early lost places. He still set the fastest lap, a 49.297 on lap 35.

Mortara finished 11th, Rockenfeller recovered from 22nd to 14th, and Ekström, who had started 21st, was 17th. In the standings Green led on 81 points, 23 clear of Mortara and Ekström on 58, with Wehrlein up to fourth on 57, Wickens fifth on 32 and Paffett sixth on 30.

Martin wins red-flagged Nürburgring opener

Maxime Martin won Race 1 from second on the grid, 2.543 seconds clear of Edoardo Mortara, after a first-lap red flag and a late Mortara-Spengler clash handed Pascal Wehrlein third.

Build-up · #94 #5

Wehrlein arrives with the title in his hands

The DTM came to the Nürburgring for the eighth of its nine race weekends with Pascal Wehrlein in control of the championship. The Mercedes driver led on 140 points, 14 ahead of Mattias Ekström and 30 clear of Edoardo Mortara, with Jamie Green on 107, Bruno Spengler on 104 and Marco Wittmann on 98. The previous round at Oschersleben had been a BMW clean sweep, with the marque winning both races and filling all six podium places, and the performance weights had been reshuffled again for the Eifel. Wehrlein's Mercedes was now the heaviest car in the field at 1,130kg, while the lightest cars, at 1,112.5kg, were the BMWs of Maxime Martin and Martin Tomczyk and the Audi of Adrien Tambay.

The two Nürburgring races were the 15th and 16th of the season's 18, so only four remained, these two and two more in the Hockenheim finale in October, so 100 points were still on offer and Wehrlein's 14-point cushion over Ekström was worth less than a single win. Saturday's race was the shorter of the weekend's two, a 40-minute sprint; Sunday's longer race would include a compulsory pit stop. For Audi's pair of Ekström and Mortara the weekend was a chance to eat into the gap, and for Wehrlein a chance to stretch it.

Qualifying · #22 #36

Rookie Auer takes a surprise pole

Lucas Auer, one of Mercedes' two rookies, took pole with a lap of 1m21.915s, 0.084s quicker than Maxime Martin's BMW and the first pole of his DTM career. Wehrlein qualified third on 1m22.030s, with Spengler's BMW only two thousandths of a second behind in fourth. Mortara was the best of the Audis in fifth, sharing the third row with Maximilian Götz, the other Mercedes rookie, who had set 1m22.072s. Green was seventh, Mike Rockenfeller eighth, Daniel Juncadella ninth and Miguel Molina tenth, so the first ten cars were covered by just 0.206s.

The championship contenders were split. Wehrlein's third place was easily the best of them, because Ekström, his closest pursuer in the table, managed only 17th on 1m22.372s. Gary Paffett was 14th, Wittmann 12th, and the Oschersleben winners had a poor session: Tom Blomqvist qualified 11th and Timo Glock 18th. Paul di Resta, Tambay and Tomczyk set no time, and technical problems kept Tomczyk's BMW out of both qualifying and the race.

Red flag · #53 #17 #31

Contact at Turn 1, then a stoppage

Wehrlein made the best start of the front runners and went for the lead into Turn 1, but Martin was already on the inside and the two touched. The contact damaged the Mercedes' front-right and Wehrlein dropped to fourth behind Spengler and Mortara, while Auer, who had overbraked at the first corner, slipped back through the field. Further back, a three-wide moment at Turn 3 turned Green's Audi around and Molina ran into it; Timo Scheider's Audi was clipped as well, and Green and Molina were out on the spot.

With debris and oil on the track the safety car came out and the race was red-flagged, the cars lining up on the pit straight. Wehrlein climbed out of his Mercedes, walked to Martin's BMW and hammered on the driver's window. Martin, who said he had been on the inside, called it a racing incident. The race was restarted behind the safety car with 30 minutes on the clock.

Restart · #7 #48 #10

Mortara and Wehrlein go through on Spengler

Martin pulled clear of the field straight after the restart, and for much of the race Spengler's BMW ran second ahead of Mortara and Wehrlein without being able to stay with the leader. Around lap 15 Mortara dived down the inside of Spengler at Turn 1. The two touched, and Wehrlein, who had been following, went through as well to take third.

Spengler fought on in a damaged car with the rivals queuing behind him, but a puncture sent him into the pits and he lost seven laps, ending up classified 19th. Wehrlein then chased Mortara for the rest of the race without finding a way past. Blomqvist and Scheider both dropped out, after nine and 11 laps respectively, Scheider with the damage from the opening-lap incident.

The finish · #2 #84 #1

Martin wins, Wehrlein extends his lead

Martin took the flag after 25 laps in 48m17.036s, 2.543s ahead of Mortara, for his second DTM win and his first of 2015. Wehrlein was 0.412s further back in third. Martin also set the fastest lap, a 1m23.502s on lap 7.

Paffett had climbed from 14th on the grid to fourth, passing Götz late on, and Götz held fifth ahead of Auer, who recovered to sixth after losing the lead at the start. Wittmann was seventh, Robert Wickens eighth and António Félix da Costa ninth. Ekström collected a single point for tenth after starting 17th. Wehrlein, who had apologised to Martin after watching the replay and conceding that the BMW driver had simply used a gap, left with 155 points. Mortara moved up to second on 128, one point ahead of Ekström, which put Wehrlein 27 points clear with 75 still available.
