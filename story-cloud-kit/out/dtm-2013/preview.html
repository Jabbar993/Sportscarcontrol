Farfus wins the Hockenheim opener as Werner climbs from 20th to second

Augusto Farfus won the 2013 DTM opener at Hockenheim for BMW Team RBM, 3.692 seconds clear of Dirk Werner, who climbed from 20th on the grid, while a safety car derailed pole-sitter Timo Scheider.

Build-up · #1 #12 #21

A new season with new tools

The 2013 DTM opened at Hockenheim on Sunday 5 May, the first of ten races, with 22 cars on the grid. The paddock was most taken with what had changed on the cars. Hankook introduced a faster option tyre, of which each driver could use one set in the race, and the drag reduction system arrived as a passing aid, so the choice of when to run the options became part of every strategy. The calendar offered ten races, starting here and ending with a return to Hockenheim on 20 October, with Brands Hatch next on 19 May.

The entry list had a fresh look as well. Timo Glock made his DTM debut for BMW Team MTEK alongside rookie Marco Wittmann. Pascal Wehrlein, at 18 the youngest driver to line up in the series, and Daniel Juncadella were the newcomers at Mücke Motorsport, while Jamie Green started his first race for Audi with the Abt Sportsline team. For Audi the stated aim was to get more than two cars into the top ten.

The weekend began in the wet. Saturday morning free practice was run in the rain and was topped by Mike Rockenfeller in a Phoenix Audi, with Mattias Ekström in the Abt Audi and Augusto Farfus in the leading BMW both within a quarter of a second, which left the pecking order very much open for qualifying.

Qualifying · #10 #9 #23

Scheider finds the right moment on a drying track

Qualifying began on a damp track that dried quickly, and the key question was when to switch from wet tyres to slicks. In Q1 Roberto Merhi crashed at the entrance to the stadium and Wittmann stopped on track, and both were among the six drivers who missed Q2. In Q2 a late lap from Juncadella eliminated Martin Tomczyk and Green, who ended 11th and 12th, while Glock went out in 15th place on his debut.

Q3 was the most dramatic part of the afternoon. All ten drivers moved to slicks within the opening minutes, lap times fell by more than five seconds before the end and the top of the timesheet changed six times in the last minute alone. Wehrlein briefly went fastest but was pushed out of the top four before his lap ended, so he started eighth on his debut, behind Ekström, Gary Paffett and Spengler, who had misjudged the drying surface and was seventh. Joey Hand and Juncadella completed the top ten.

In the single-lap shootout Timo Scheider, driving the Abt Audi, knocked Farfus off the top with a 1:35.918 and kept it when Christian Vietoris lost time in the opening sector of his lap. It was Scheider's 11th career pole, 0.116 seconds ahead of Farfus (1:36.034), with Vietoris third, 0.276 off. Robert Wickens was fourth in the second HWA Mercedes. Werner, who qualified at the back of the field, lined up 20th, with Merhi and Wittmann behind him.

Opening laps · #7 #24 #8

Farfus takes the lead with DRS

Scheider kept the lead into the first corner from Farfus, who had started on the new option tyres. Hand struggled to get away from ninth on the grid, and Vietoris dropped from third to 11th on the opening lap. At the first corner Ekström brushed Juncadella and Paffett and damaged the front of his Audi.

For the first handful of laps Farfus ran second to Scheider before using DRS to pass the Abt Audi at the hairpin. Wickens' good run in fourth ended when his throttle stuck open as he approached the hairpin, and he only just avoided taking Spengler with him. He was out of the race within the first few laps.

Safety car · #18 #17

A smoking Audi reshuffles the race

Adrien Tambay's Audi stopped at the side of the track with smoke pouring from the engine, and the safety car was deployed to clear it away. The field dived into the pits and the order was scrambled. Farfus made his first stop while the safety car was out and Werner did the same from the back of the grid, committing himself to a very long middle stint on the options, which lasted far better than expected.

Scheider had no such luck. His Abt team left him out for one extra lap compared with his rivals and he rejoined 19th, his hopes of a win gone. The Mücke pair Wehrlein and Juncadella took the opposite approach, staying out on options through the neutralisation, and Wehrlein led most of the first half of the race, not stopping until lap 25.

Pit stops · #22 #11 #5

Glock's wheel and the long middle stint

Glock had cut through from 15th to second in the first half of the race before he ran wide at the Sachs-Kurve and went through the gravel. After his first stop the right-rear wheel of his BMW came off and his race was over; Glock said afterwards that the wheel had not been secured properly at the stop.

Elsewhere Ekström's bonnet flew off and he spun Edoardo Mortara, for which he was given a drive-through penalty. Mortara, who already had a damaged splitter, did not finish, and Ekström retired later in the race. With the cars ahead gradually coming in for their final stops, Farfus, who had spent the middle section out of position on a different tyre set, found himself back in the lead after overtaking Juncadella for fifth at the Spitzkehre.

The finish · #2 #3 #19

Farfus controls the finish, Werner takes second

Farfus led the closing laps and took the flag after 42 laps in 1:10:11.583, 3.692 seconds ahead of Werner, who had emerged second after his last stop. Vietoris, who had followed a similar strategy to Werner after his poor first lap, passed Paffett with DRS inside the last ten laps to finish third, 9.675 seconds behind the winner. Paffett took fourth, 12.613 seconds back, holding off Spengler, with whom he had banged wheels several times in the Mercedes Arena.

Scheider recovered from 19th to sixth, aided by a sensational outside move on Hand at the Sachs-Kurve, and was the best-placed Audi. Hand took seventh and Rockenfeller eighth, and Wittmann and Merhi, who had both come through from the back of the grid, took ninth and tenth to score their first DTM points. Farfus also set the fastest lap, a 1:34.504.

Aftermath · #4

BMW delighted, Audi left with questions

BMW left Hockenheim with a one-two and five cars in the top ten, Farfus and Werner winning and finishing second, Spengler fifth, Hand seventh and Wittmann ninth. Spengler was a little disappointed, because he felt a podium had been within reach: after his first stop and the safety car he suffered strong understeer, and the team brought him in for a second tyre change to limit the damage. Farfus said the option tyres and DRS had produced a great deal of action and that he had been quick from the start. Werner called the race magical: he had been so disappointed after qualifying that he had struggled to get out of bed that morning, yet he went on to finish second for his first DTM podium. BMW's motorsport director Jens Marquardt praised the whole team, said Farfus had not made a mistake and that the strategy was spot-on, and had sympathy for Glock.

For Glock the day ended in disappointment, but he left with a positive impression of his first weekend. He accepted that he had made a mistake going into the gravel and that the crew had made one at the stop, while Marquardt pointed out that a new team was bound to have a learning curve. Vietoris took his first podium in third for Mercedes, with Paffett fourth, and motorsport chief Toto Wolff said there was a lot to learn about tyre strategy. Wickens said that without the stuck throttle points and perhaps even a podium had been possible, and Merhi scored his first point from 21st on the grid despite a tyre problem at one of his stops.

Audi had a difficult afternoon. Scheider was clear that the team had reacted too late to the safety car, and Audi's DTM head Dieter Gass agreed it had been the wrong call not to bring him in at once. Gass added that Audi suffered from having only two cars in the top ten. Tambay was out early with smoke from his engine, and Ekström and Mortara also failed to finish.

In the championship Farfus led with 25 points, ahead of Werner on 18, Vietoris on 15, Paffett on 12, Spengler on 10 and Scheider on 8. The opening round drew a crowd of 87,000 over the weekend. The DTM next heads to Brands Hatch in Britain on 19 May.

Rockenfeller wins at Brands Hatch from inherited pole as Farfus stops

Mike Rockenfeller led all 98 laps at Brands Hatch for Audi, 7.641 seconds clear of Bruno Spengler, after Martin Tomczyk lost pole for his underweight BMW and Augusto Farfus stopped from second.

Build-up · #7 #2

Britain next for the Hockenheim winners

Two weeks after the Hockenheim opener the DTM made its only visit to Britain, with Brands Hatch hosting round two on Sunday 19 May. Augusto Farfus arrived at the head of the standings on 25 points after winning from second on the grid, with Schnitzer's Dirk Werner on 18 after climbing from 20th to second, and Christian Vietoris third on 15. The short 1.929-kilometre Indy circuit made overtaking difficult even with DRS and option tyres, and three drivers, Martin Tomczyk, Mike Rockenfeller and Farfus, were due to fly straight from the race to the Nürburgring 24 Hours.

Qualifying · #15 #19 #8

Tomczyk takes pole, then loses it

On Saturday Tomczyk produced a lap of 41.158 seconds in the shootout for what looked like his first pole in a BMW. Rockenfeller was 0.035 seconds slower in the Phoenix Audi and Farfus 0.039 behind in third, while Joey Hand opened the shootout with a 41.460 and took fourth, the best qualifying result of his DTM career. Spengler missed the top-four session by about a hundredth of a second and was fifth ahead of Timo Scheider and Marco Wittmann, with Andy Priaulx, Edoardo Mortara and Miguel Molina completing the top ten.

It was a miserable session for Mercedes, which had no car in the top ten. Gary Paffett was only 12th. Jamie Green, in his first home race for Audi, started his final Q1 lap a tenth of a second after the flag and had the time deleted, which left him 18th, and Vietoris and Wehrlein also fell in Q1.

After qualifying the stewards found that Tomczyk's car was underweight. He was sent to the back of the grid and Rockenfeller inherited pole, with Farfus alongside him, Hand third and Spengler fourth, while Paffett moved up to 11th and Tomczyk started 22nd.

Opening laps · #4 #22 #21

Rockenfeller gets away, Merhi makes enemies

Rockenfeller made a good start and held the lead from Farfus into the first corner. Spengler moved up to third, Wittmann gained two places from sixth, and Hand, who got away badly, dropped from third to sixth. Rockenfeller built a small lead on the option tyres in the early laps and was never in danger.

Behind them, there was trouble. Early in the race Roberto Merhi in the HWA Mercedes forced Glock onto the grass at the bottom of Graham Hill Bend, and at the same spot he later put Tomczyk, who was climbing through the field from the back, onto the grass as well. Both BMW drivers were angry afterwards, and Tomczyk finished 13th and Glock 14th, both out of the points.

Pit stops · #1 #3

Strategy gambles and a failure for Farfus

Each car had to make two stops. Paffett and his team made a gamble on the starting tyre, running standard, then option, then standard again, and it paid off, taking him from 11th on the grid into the podium positions. Wickens, who started 13th, made up places with the help of the options at the start, and HWA held him behind Paffett.

Farfus had run second for most of the race and was heading for another podium when a technical failure ended his afternoon after his second stop and left him stranded, 65 laps in. It cost him the points and the lead of the championship. Spengler, who had been running third, moved into second and stayed there to the flag.

The finish · #10 #9 #18

Rockenfeller rules, Wickens gets lucky

Rockenfeller took the flag after 98 laps in 1:11:28.719, 7.641 seconds ahead of Spengler. The winner had controlled the race from the start. Spengler said Rockenfeller was quicker early on and he quicker at the end, but not by enough to pass. Paffett crossed the line third, but was given a five-second penalty for not slowing sufficiently under yellow flags and dropped to sixth, which handed the final podium place to Wickens, 24.368 seconds behind, who thereby took his first DTM podium.

Wittmann was fourth, 24.964 seconds back, and Hand fifth, with Paffett sixth after the penalty. Ekström climbed from 12th to seventh, ahead of Vietoris, Scheider and Wehrlein, who took the last point in tenth. Paffett also set the fastest lap, a 42.124 on lap six. Werner recovered only to 12th from 14th on the grid, and Mortara was classified 21st, eleven laps down.

Aftermath · #11 #23

Audi takes the lead of the championship

Rockenfeller said he had controlled the race from the start, that his Audi had worked perfectly and that both stops had gone well, and that the option tyres had not lasted as well as expected, which he thought was the case for everyone. Audi's DTM head Dieter Gass said the win was a confirmation of the winter's work and that Rockenfeller had dominated for 98 laps without a mistake, while Audi motorsport chief Wolfgang Ullrich pointed out that such dominant victories are rare in the DTM. Ernst Moser, team principal of Phoenix, said the celebrations would have to wait because the team was still involved at the Nürburgring 24 Hours.

BMW was content with a second place, said motorsport director Jens Marquardt, and with three cars in the top five, though six cars in the top ten on the grid might have meant more. Spengler said the pace had been good and the strategy perfect, and he was happy to get on the podium. Wittmann, in only his second DTM race, took fourth, and Hand fifth, though Hand said his start had gone wrong and the car had podium potential. For Farfus the retirement from second place was the day's big disappointment, and Glock and Tomczyk were both openly critical of Merhi after being forced off by the Mercedes driver.

Mercedes motorsport chief Toto Wolff said Sunday had been much better than Saturday, with four cars in the top ten, and that luck had played its part. Wickens could hardly believe his first podium, which he called a miracle, crediting a strong start on the new option tyres and two good stops, while Wehrlein scored his first point in tenth. With Audi, BMW and Mercedes all represented on the podium, Wolff called it good for the series.

The result changed the championship. Rockenfeller led with 29 points after his win, one ahead of Spengler on 28, with Farfus third on 25. Paffett was fourth with 20, Vietoris fifth with 19 and Werner sixth with 18. The DTM would next race in Spielberg, Austria, on 2 June, but Rockenfeller, Tomczyk and Farfus had the Nürburgring 24 Hours to get through first.
