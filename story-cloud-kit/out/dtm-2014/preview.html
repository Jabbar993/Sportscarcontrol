Ekström wins the Hockenheim finale in an Audi 1-2-3

Mattias Ekström passed Jamie Green for the lead after the only safety car and won the 2014 finale at Hockenheim by 2.402 seconds from Mike Rockenfeller, completing an Audi 1-2-3 that took the manufacturers' title.

Build-up

Two prizes left to settle

The 2014 DTM season closed at Hockenheim on 19 October with most of the silverware already handed out. Marco Wittmann had sealed the drivers' title at the Lausitzring, and BMW Team RMG had added the teams' championship at Zandvoort three weeks earlier, where Mattias Ekström's victory had also been Audi's first of the year. That left two questions for the finale: who would take second place in the drivers' standings, and which brand would win the manufacturers' title. Ekström had won that race from eighth on the grid after four safety cars had wrecked the plans of pole-sitter Rockenfeller, with Wittmann second and Tomczyk third, and Audi had not won a race before it.

Wittmann was untouchable on 146 points. Ekström was second on 81, 12 clear of Christian Vietoris on 69, with Edoardo Mortara on 68 and Mike Rockenfeller on 54, so the runner-up place was still within reach of several drivers if the points fell the wrong way. BMW, already holding two of the three titles, wanted the manufacturers' crown as well, while Audi, fresh from its first win of the season, had every reason to believe the order could change. Rockenfeller, the pole-sitter at Zandvoort who had finished 15th there, had the most to recover, and Mercedes, with three wins from the season, were looking to end it on a high at their home event.

It was also the home race of Mercedes-Benz, the last of ten rounds, and it drew 155,000 spectators across the weekend. The race ran over 42 laps with a mandatory tyre change between the soft option and the harder prime compound, a rule that would shape the order more than anything else on the day.

Qualifying · #8 #7 #15

Molina takes the last pole of the year

Audi dominated the final qualifying session in bright sunshine, filling six of the eight places in the last segment. Miguel Molina led Q1 with 1:33.190, ahead of Pascal Wehrlein, Adrien Tambay, Jamie Green and Timo Scheider, while Timo Glock, Martin Tomczyk, Joey Hand, Vietoris and Vitaly Petrov were knocked out. Hand had spent most of the segment with a problem with his driver's door, which was eventually taped shut. In Q2 Rockenfeller set the fastest time, 1:33.172, and the eliminated included Wittmann, Tambay, Robert Wickens, Paul di Resta, António Félix da Costa and Bruno Spengler.

Ekström opened Q3 with 1:33.403, and Wehrlein and Green took turns near the top before Molina went fastest on his last run with 1:33.316. Ekström ended 0.016 seconds behind, with Mortara third on 1:33.406, less than a tenth off the pole time. Augusto Farfus was the only BMW in the shootout and started fourth, Green was fifth, and Wehrlein, sixth, was the best of the Mercedes drivers. Rockenfeller and Scheider completed the top eight in seventh and eighth.

Wittmann missed the top-eight shootout and lined up 13th. Tomczyk, who had been knocked out in Q1, started 20th, with Hand 21st, Vietoris 22nd and Petrov last.

Opening laps · #21 #25 #3

Molina runs wide, Wehrlein charges

Molina lost the lead almost at once. At the second corner he missed his braking point and ran wide, and Ekström went ahead before Green took the lead out of the hairpin on the first lap. Wehrlein, who had started sixth, was already third at the end of the opening lap and passed Ekström for second on lap 2, with Farfus following him through.

Ekström had started on the harder prime tyres, unlike most of the field, and slipped down to sixth by lap 4 as the cars on softs went by. Green led from Wehrlein, Farfus, Molina and Scheider. Bruno Spengler was the first to make his mandatory tyre change, on lap 14, but the leaders stayed out for another six laps, with Green, Wehrlein and Farfus all running well on their soft tyres.

Pit stops · #2 #1

Wehrlein's release costs him the podium

The leading group stopped around lap 20, moving from the soft option to the harder prime. Wehrlein and Scheider came out side by side in the pit lane, and Wehrlein was given a drive-through penalty for an unsafe release, which ended his hopes of a podium. A lap later Ekström and the rest of the field who had started on primes came in to take on the faster tyres, as did Rockenfeller and Wittmann.

That put Ekström and Rockenfeller on options for the second half of the race while Green, Scheider and Farfus ran on primes, and the two Audi drivers began to make up places. Ekström passed Juncadella for sixth, then Spengler, and by lap 30 he and Rockenfeller were fourth and fifth, behind Green, Scheider and Farfus. Maxime Martin's BMW retired after 27 laps, and on lap 31 Mortara and Hand collided in the fight for 19th, which sent Mortara into the pits.

Safety car · #19 #9

Green's lead disappears

Green had built a lead of almost ten seconds when Ekström and Rockenfeller passed Scheider for second and third. Then, on lap 37, Juncadella and Spengler tangled in the fight for 13th, and the Mercedes went off. The stewards later held Spengler responsible for the contact.

The safety car, the only one of the race, wiped out Green's advantage. Behind it the order was Green, Ekström, Rockenfeller, Scheider, Wittmann and di Resta, with Farfus, Molina, Wehrlein and Tomczyk behind. The leaders were bunched together again, Green on worn primes, Ekström and Rockenfeller on much fresher options, and the field was released at the end of lap 40.

The finish · #6 #23 #10

Ekström strikes at the hairpin

The restart decided the race. On lap 41 Ekström passed Green at the exit of the hairpin and took the lead, and on the last lap Rockenfeller went by the Briton in the same place to make it a 1-2-3 for Audi. Ekström won by 2.402 seconds from Rockenfeller, with Green third, 3.914 behind the winner.

Di Resta was right behind Green for the last podium place and finished fourth, 4.163 seconds from the winner and just a quarter of a second off the Audi, the best Mercedes. Wittmann, who had started 13th, was fifth at the flag after a duel with di Resta and also set the fastest lap. Scheider was sixth and Tomczyk seventh after climbing from 20th. Molina, who had lost ground after locking up while trying to regain the lead, was eighth, ahead of da Costa and Paffett.

Aftermath

Audi take the manufacturers' title

The result gave Audi the manufacturers' championship and ended a season in which the brand's only two victories, at Zandvoort and Hockenheim, were both won by Ekström, who finished as runner-up in the drivers' standings. He said winning in front of such a crowd was fantastic, and that despite the many ups and downs the year had ended well. Audi's DTM head Dieter Gass said he was especially pleased for Ekström, whom he described as an old hand who had beaten the young guns with two wins in a row, and added that Audi now wanted the drivers' title back.

BMW finished with the drivers' and teams' titles but lost the manufacturers' crown. Wittmann, fifth from 13th on the grid, said he had got the maximum out of the race, and motorsport director Jens Marquardt said two of the three titles was a fine return for the team, adding that qualifying had left BMW with too much to do. Mercedes finished the season with three wins; Mercedes motorsport head Toto Wolff said Audi had been too strong on the day and that the team could have avoided the penalty for Wehrlein, which was given in the heat of the moment. Di Resta, who had lost places at the start, said fourth was a positive way to end the year and that he would have liked to catch Green for the podium.

The stewards were busy after the flag. Wickens was given a 30-second penalty for causing the contact with Farfus, which dropped him from eighth on the road to 17th and promoted Paffett to tenth and a point. Spengler was handed three hours of community service, to be carried out in agreement with the DMSB, for the contact with Juncadella.

Wittmann finished the season on 156 points, 50 clear of Ekström in second on 106. Rockenfeller's second place lifted him to third on 72, past Vietoris on 69 and Mortara on 68, while Tomczyk was sixth on 49 after his late climb. The 2014 season was over, and the teams turned to their preparations for the winter.

Wittmann wins as Wickens is disqualified at the Red Bull Ring

Marco Wittmann won at the Red Bull Ring to lead a BMW 1-2-3-4, after pole-sitter Robert Wickens was black-flagged from the lead for ignoring a drive-through penalty.

Build-up · #24

Wittmann arrives with a 20-point cushion

The sixth round of the ten-race 2014 DTM season moved from Russia to Austria, where the Red Bull Ring in Spielberg hosted the series on Sunday 3 August. Three weeks earlier at Moscow Raceway BMW rookie Maxime Martin had taken his first pole and his first win, while Marco Wittmann’s fourth place was enough to stretch his lead in the drivers’ table to 70 points, 20 ahead of Mattias Ekström, with Bruno Spengler and Edoardo Mortara tied on 41 and Mike Rockenfeller on 35.

Moscow had also hurt Audi, whose Rockenfeller and Adrien Tambay had collided on a safety car restart, and left Mercedes-Benz without a car in the top six. The Red Bull Ring offered a chance to change the picture on a 4.326-kilometre circuit, over a distance of 47 laps and a mandatory stop in a pit window that opened on lap 16 and closed on lap 31. Robert Wickens, the Norisring winner, was the Mercedes driver most likely to take the fight to BMW.

Qualifying · #12 #17 #25

Wickens snatches pole from Glock by six thousandths

Qualifying was decided on the very last lap. Augusto Farfus was quickest in the first segment, but in the final shoot-out it was Timo Glock who seemed to have pole, with BMW’s Wittmann and Farfus behind him. Wickens had spun, yet he put everything together after the chequered flag had come out and took pole with 1:25.175, only 0.006 seconds ahead of Glock’s 1:25.181. Glock said it hurt, having seen himself first on the video screens during his cool-down lap.

Wittmann was third on 1:25.228, 0.053 seconds off the pole, and Farfus fourth. Pascal Wehrlein put a second Mercedes on the third row in fifth, ahead of Jamie Green, the only Audi to reach the final segment and sixth. Christian Vietoris was seventh and Moscow winner Martin eighth, with Joey Hand ninth and Spengler tenth. Martin Tomczyk was 11th.

It was a bad day for Audi. Ekström, second in the championship, was only 16th and said his car had simply lacked pace although he had made no big mistakes. Nico Müller was 18th, and Tambay, Mortara, Gary Paffett, Vitaly Petrov and Paul di Resta were all knocked out in the first segment, Di Resta ending up 23rd.

Opening laps · #3 #18 #4

Wickens holds Farfus at bay

Wickens got away perfectly from pole and kept the lead while Glock and Farfus chased. Farfus took second from fellow BMW driver Glock at the hairpin on lap 5 and then began to close, attacking Wickens side by side on several laps and backing up a tight queue behind them. Wittmann, meanwhile, made a poor start and fell several places, a mistake he later admitted was his own.

Behind the leaders the stewards were busy. António Félix da Costa stopped early on, and around lap 15 five drivers, among them Mortara, Hand and Martin, were sent through the pit lane for yellow-flag infringements. Hand and Martin finished 12th and 14th and Mortara 16th, out of the points.

Pit stops · #21

Green leads, Wickens is told to come in

Farfus was the first of the leaders to stop and Wickens followed a few laps later, with Glock beside him in the pit lane. Glock’s BMW Team MTEK crew was a touch quicker, and Wickens was released into the space in front of him, forcing the German to brake. The stewards called it an unsafe release and handed Wickens a drive-through penalty.

The stops also elevated Jamie Green, who pitted later than the rest and briefly led the race. His joy ended when it emerged he had kept the option tyres on a lap too long, and the Audi was given a drive-through of its own. Audi’s DTM boss Dieter Gass said a communication problem was behind the late call. Farfus passed Wickens for the lead, and Wickens took it back.

Black flag

The leader is shown the black flag

Wickens did not serve the penalty. Mercedes argued with race control, saying the stewards had confused Wickens’ car with that of Wehrlein, who had also been involved in an incident in the pit lane, and kept him out. After several laps without a visit to the pits the black flag was shown, and Wickens came in on lap 34 to end the race, ending in a disqualification.

Wehrlein, running fourth for a time, was out after 26 laps. As Wickens and Green fell away, Wittmann went to work, passing Glock and then Farfus and taking over the lead once the Mercedes was out.

The finish · #23 #2 #7

A BMW 1-2-3-4

Wittmann led the final laps and took his third win in six races in 1:08:23.185. Farfus was second, 3.298 seconds behind, with Glock third at 5.391 seconds and Martin Tomczyk fourth, completing a BMW 1-2-3-4 with one driver from each of the four BMW teams. Timo Scheider drove from 14th on the grid to fifth for Audi, 6.666 seconds behind, and Tambay climbed from 20th to sixth.

Ekström started 15th and finished seventh, passing Vietoris and Green late on, and Green recovered from his penalty to eighth. Vietoris took ninth, the only Mercedes in the points, and Spengler was tenth. Rockenfeller set the fastest lap, a 1:25.266 on lap 45, but he had started 17th and came home 13th.

Aftermath · #10

Wittmann is 39 points clear with four races left

Wittmann said the race had not been as easy as it looked: he had got away very badly, fought back and then had very good pace on the primes in the second stint, setting the fastest lap on primes on his penultimate lap. He added that he felt “just really lucky”. BMW Motorsport Director Jens Marquardt called the result historic: BMW had never before filled the top four places since returning in 2012, and the last time it happened in the DTM was at Hockenheim in 1992.

Mercedes was furious. Wolfgang Schattling said Wickens could certainly have won and that the penalty was not justified, claiming the stewards had mistaken one car for the other. Glock took a different view, saying Wickens had left him no room and that he had to brake to avoid the wall or the Mercedes, though he conceded the verdict was the stewards’ to give. Gass said Audi had lost the race in qualifying, and also pointed to the communication problem that cost Green.

The result tightened Wittmann’s grip on the title. He now has 95 points, 39 ahead of Ekström on 56, with Spengler on 42, Mortara 41, Tambay 36 and Rockenfeller 35. Spengler is 53 points behind the leader and Mortara 54. BMW also went top of the team and manufacturers’ tables.

With four races left the DTM travels to the Nürburgring on 17 August. Marquardt said the performance weights were bound to make that a real challenge for BMW, and Scheider hoped fifth place would mark the turnaround in a disappointing season. Rockenfeller, who set the fastest lap, left with only a 13th place, while Ekström’s seventh left him chasing the leader from a long way back after a day on which neither Audi nor Mercedes could keep up with the BMWs at the front.
