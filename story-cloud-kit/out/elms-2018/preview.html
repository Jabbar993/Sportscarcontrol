G-Drive wins Monza as TDS charges from the back

Roman Rusinov, Andrea Pizzitola and Jean-Eric Vergne won the 4 Hours of Monza in the G-Drive Oreca #26 by 29.8 seconds, ahead of TDS, which started last after losing pole.

Build-up · #47 #33

A new-look LMP2 field arrives from Spa

The 4 Hours of Monza on 13 May 2018 was the second round of the European Le Mans Series, and it came with the paddock's attention split between three fronts: the Nürburgring 24 Hours, the Michelin Le Mans Cup running alongside, and the build-up to Le Mans five weeks away. The entry was 43 cars, only three fewer guest entries from the World Endurance Championship than at Le Castellet, and 18 of them were LMP2. Le Castellet had gone to the Racing Engineering Oreca #24 of Norman Nato, Olivier Pla and Paul Petit, with the TDS Racing Oreca #33 second, Duqueine Engineering third and G-Drive Racing fourth, so the class arrived at Monza without a clear favourite.

The most talked-about arrivals came straight from Spa. G-Drive Racing, whose Oreca #26 had won the LMP2 class at the WEC season opener a week earlier, returned to its normal full-season ELMS role, and team principal Roman Rusinov spoke openly about adding further WEC rounds if the budget allowed. TDS Racing, which runs the G-Drive technical programme, brought a second Oreca of its own as the only WEC guest of the weekend. It ran as #33 because IDEC Sport had already claimed the team's usual #28 in the ELMS, and carried the regular WEC trio of Matthieu Vaxivière, François Perrodo and Loïc Duval.

There were driver changes elsewhere. Filipe Albuquerque rejoined United Autosports in the #22 Ligier to replace Bruno Senna, and former Formula 1 driver Felipe Nasr made his ELMS debut in the Cetilar Villorba Corse Dallara #47, standing in for Andrea Belicchi, who had stepped back with back problems. Nasr's car was damaged in the Wednesday test and repaired overnight. Free practice then ran to the Orecas' advantage: Ben Hanley put the DragonSpeed #21 top of the first session on 1:36.284, and G-Drive led the second on 1:35.943 with Oreca 07s filling the top seven places. Tacksung Kim brought out a Full Course Yellow in the first session when he beached the Algarve Pro Racing Ligier #25 in the gravel at the second Lesmo.

Qualifying · #21 #29 #31

TDS takes pole, then loses it

The ten-minute LMP2 session ended in confusion. Vaxivière set a 1:35.456 in the TDS Oreca shortly before the red flag came out for Ate de Jong, who hit the barriers at Parabolica in the Algarve Ligier and walked away from a heavily damaged car. Nobody improved in the closing dash after the restart, and Vaxivière had a first ELMS pole. Hanley was second on 1:35.837 for DragonSpeed, ahead of Gustavo Menezes in the APR Rebellion Oreca #31 on 1:36.034, with Nelson Panciatici's Duqueine #29 and Jean-Eric Vergne's G-Drive Oreca #26 completing an Oreca top five. Albuquerque's United Ligier was the best of the rest, about nine tenths off the pace.

That order lasted only until the evening. Post-qualifying scrutineering found tape attached to the bodywork of the TDS car, and every one of Vaxivière's times was deleted; the car was allowed to race but had to start behind the last LMP2 runner. The Rebellion Oreca was then found to have a front splitter below the height allowed by article 3.5.4 of the technical regulations and also dropped to the back of the class. The sanctions handed pole to DragonSpeed's Hanley, with the Duqueine alongside him on the front row, Vergne and Albuquerque on row two, and the two penalised Orecas at the tail of the LMP2 group.

The reshuffle set up a very different race to the one the timesheets had suggested. TDS, the fastest car in qualifying, would have to work through most of the LMP2 field and the slower classes, while the G-Drive car that had been fifth on the road was now on the second row.

Hour 1 · #39 #40

Hanley leads, the Safety Car shuffles the order

The extra formation lap meant the race clock started a little late, and Hanley led the 43-car field through the first chicane from pole, with Rusinov in the G-Drive Oreca immediately on his tail. Rusinov had to hold off Pierre Ragues in the Duqueine for second place before the DragonSpeed Oreca pulled away.

The first Safety Car came after just 16 minutes, when Alexandre Cougnaud lost the Graff Oreca #39 at the second Lesmo and hit the barriers; he was unhurt and racing resumed after around 20 minutes. Hanley pitted under that neutralisation and Rusinov inherited the lead. DragonSpeed's cause was then hurt when Henrik Hedman collided with an LMP3 Ligier and picked up a drive-through penalty. A second Safety Car followed after about 45 minutes, when Enzo Guibbert put the second Graff-run Oreca, the G-Drive #40, into the barriers at Ascari. Guibbert was unhurt, but the car was badly damaged and the cleanup took around 17 minutes.

Hours 2–3 · #22 #32 #23

Four Safety Cars and the United scare

The third neutralisation began as a Full Course Yellow after 1 hour 41 minutes of racing, to recover an LMP3 Ligier beached in the gravel following a collision with a GTE Porsche, and became a Safety Car when a DKR Engineering Norma crashed between Lesmo 2 and Ascari. The race resumed at the top of the third hour, and only five minutes later a tangle of three LMP3 cars at the exit of Lesmo 2 brought out the fourth and last Safety Car, with a long debris cleanup. Andrea Pizzitola controlled the middle of the race for G-Drive, giving up the lead only during stops.

The cautions bunched the field and created a muddled order. United Autosports had left both its Ligiers out, which put Phil Hanson in the #22 car in front at the restart, with Pizzitola second and Will Owen in the sister #32 third. For a moment the two United cars were mistakenly waved past the Safety Car, an error race control spotted. Pizzitola quickly recovered the lead, and when Hanson pitted to hand the car to Albuquerque, G-Drive swept back ahead. Owen was then passed for third by Will Stevens in the Panis Barthez Ligier #23. The pace in the class was close all afternoon, and IDEC Sport's Paul Lafargue produced a long stint that kept the French team's #28 Oreca in the podium conversation.

Hour 4 · #26

Final stops decide the win

With an hour to go the LMP2 leaders were heading for their last visit to the pits. Vergne had taken over from Pizzitola and held a lead of about 22 seconds over Albuquerque. When the Portuguese came in, the TDS Oreca moved up to second place, but he then had to serve a drive-through penalty for causing a collision with a COOL Racing LMP3 Ligier earlier in the race. It took United's #22 out of podium contention for good. Vergne stopped for fresh tyres with 32 minutes remaining and came out with a gap of around half a minute over Vaxivière.

The finish · #28 #24

Vergne cruises home as TDS charges from the rear

Vergne managed that margin in the closing laps and took the flag 29.828 seconds ahead of Vaxivière, completing 124 laps in the #26 G-Drive Oreca. Vaxivière had started from the back of the LMP2 field and made it to second place without the benefit of a clean qualifying lap. IDEC Sport's Paul Loup Chatin, who had taken over the #28 car from Memo Rojas and Lafargue, closed to within about two seconds of the TDS car by the flag and took third place on the road.

Hanley led DragonSpeed's recovery in the closing stages and brought the #21 car back to fourth place, despite the drive-through penalty and the early stop under the first Safety Car. The mid-race cautions had helped to wipe out the time lost, and late fuel stops for Nasr and the United #22 allowed Hanley to climb past them. Racing Engineering's Oreca #24, the Le Castellet winner, followed the same early-stop strategy and came home fifth. Oreca chassis filled the top six places overall; the best Ligier was Panis Barthez's #23 in seventh, and Nasr took ninth in the best of the Dallaras, ahead of the United #22 in tenth.

Aftermath

Three weekends, three wins for Vergne

It was a double for G-Drive in a week. The Rusinov, Pizzitola and Vergne trio followed their class win at Spa with overall victory at Monza, and the result was a third win in three race weekends for Vergne, who had also won in Formula E. It was his first ELMS start, and the team's first ELMS win of 2018 after fourth place at Le Castellet. Because TDS provides the technical support for the G-Drive car, the French team could claim a one-two with its own entry and the winner, despite having lost its pole.

The mistakes were costly for several teams. TDS paid for its tape infringement but recovered to second from the back of the grid, and the Rebellion Oreca that shared the same fate finished eighth. DragonSpeed's penalty cost the team a realistic shot at victory, and United Autosports left Monza with a pair of Ligiers in tenth and eleventh after a race that had put them first and second at the restart. The Graff team also faced a heavy repair job: the G-Drive #40 that Guibbert crashed at Ascari needed major work to be ready for the Le Mans test day on 3 June.

The result left the LMP2 title fight open after two rounds. Racing Engineering had won the opener and kept in touch with a fifth place, while TDS added a second place to its Le Castellet runner-up result. With 18 cars in the class, more than half the field had been in podium contention at some point during the afternoon.

The next date for most LMP2 teams was Le Mans on 16-17 June, with the test day first, and many of them were heading straight there. United Autosports stayed on at Monza for testing with Juan Pablo Montoya, who was to share the #32 Ligier with Owen and de Sadeleer at Le Mans, while Nasr's Le Mans debut with Villorba Corse was set to follow his Detroit IMSA round. For the ELMS the next round was the 4 Hours of Red Bull Ring on 22 July.

G-Drive wins at Silverstone from Panis Barthez pole

Roman Rusinov, Andrea Pizzitola and Jean-Eric Vergne won from second on the grid, a lap clear of DragonSpeed, to give G-Drive a third straight win and a 34-point lead.

Build-up · #40

A double-header on a new surface

The 4 Hours of Silverstone on 18 August 2018 was the fourth of six European Le Mans Series rounds and one half of a double-header, with the FIA World Endurance Championship's Six Hours following on the same circuit the next day. That squeezed the teams doing both: DragonSpeed, Gulf Racing, TDS Racing and Proton Competition split crews and equipment between two pit lanes, and DragonSpeed's Elton Julian called it the biggest operation his team had ever run, with a rehearsed golf-cart dash between the paddocks. In LMP2 the entry was a big one, 16 cars on the grid out of 41 starters in all.

G-Drive Racing arrived as championship leader. Its TDS-run Oreca #26 had won at Monza and again at the Red Bull Ring, while Racing Engineering had opened the season with a win on its series debut at Le Castellet. There were line-up changes at the front. Matthieu Vaxiviere took the third seat in the Racing Engineering Oreca #24 in place of Olivier Pla, who was due back at Spa, and Garry Findlay returned to the series in the second G-Drive car, the #40.

The circuit itself was the main talking point. Silverstone had been resurfaced earlier in the year, and Dunlop's technicians described a smoother track overall but one with short, sharp bumps at the entry to Maggotts and at Stowe that unsettled cars under braking, little drainage in the wet, and a darker surface that held heat, adding perhaps 10 to 15 degrees to track temperatures on a 30-degree day. Sharper kerb edges also made cutting corners a tyre risk. On Friday the Dunlop-shod G-Drive Oreca set the pace in both sessions, with Vergne's 1:45.490 in the afternoon seven-tenths clear of Ben Hanley's DragonSpeed car, which was second in both.

Qualifying · #23 #49 #26

Stevens takes a surprise pole

Michelin runners had not been expected to trouble G-Drive, but Will Stevens put the Panis Barthez Competition Ligier JS P217 on pole with a late 1:43.690, 0.736 seconds quicker than Vergne's 1:44.426 in the #26 Oreca. It was the first pole of 2018 for a JS P217, and it came for a Michelin-shod car against a Dunlop-shod Oreca that had topped both practice sessions. "It's a great feeling to score a pole at home," Stevens said, adding that the team had known since Friday it had a strong package.

Hanley put the DragonSpeed Oreca #21 third on 1:44.539, ahead of Nicolas Jamin's Duqueine Engineering #29 and James Allen in the second G-Drive car, the #40, in fourth and fifth. Anders Fjordbach was the best of the Dallaras in sixth in the High Class Racing #49, with Nato seventh for Racing Engineering and Paul Loup Chatin eighth for IDEC Sport. The United Autosports Ligiers were well down the order, Wayne Boyd tenth in the #32 and Filipe Albuquerque twelfth in the #22.

In the support classes, Eurointernational's Mattia Drudi took LMP3 pole by 0.046 seconds from Ross Kaiser's 360 Racing car, and Dennis Olsen put the Proton Competition Porsche #77 on LMGTE pole, 0.060 ahead of his team-mate Matteo Cairoli.

Hour 1 · #47 #29

Canal leads, Rusinov waits

With 41 cars on the grid, Julien Canal kept the Panis Barthez Ligier ahead of Rusinov into the first corner, but the start was messy further back. Several cars were forced off into the Village complex, among them the Cetilar Villorba Corse Dallara #47, which dropped far down the order and completed only 98 laps, and the Duqueine #29.

Canal led for the first hour with Rusinov at his tail, the pair working through backmarkers together. Roughly an hour in, Rusinov found a way past on the outside and took the lead for G-Drive, a position the #26 would not lose for the rest of the afternoon.

Hours 2–3 · #22 #35 #30

Two retirements and a Full Course Yellow

Early in the second hour the United Autosports Ligier #22 stopped at Village, with Phil Hanson climbing out after the car's fire extinguisher went off and shut down its systems. Moments later the SMP Racing Dallara #35 went off at the same spot and was too damaged to continue. Neither car was classified, and the Full Course Yellow that followed while the cars were recovered let most of the LMP2 field make a third stop and change drivers.

By the end of the second hour Pizzitola led comfortably from Timothe Buret in the Panis Barthez Ligier and Henrique Chaves in the AVF Dallara #30. Vergne took over in the third hour and began stretching the gap, while DragonSpeed's Henrik Hedman lost several places in a short space of time. Behind the leaders there was plenty of contact in the midfield, with the APR-Rebellion Oreca involved in several incidents.

Hour 4 · #24

Nato goes off, the order thins out

With the final hour under way Vergne was well clear, and the race was being decided for the places behind him. The AVF Dallara #30 dropped away when it came in for a new rear wing, a stop that left it eighth at the flag. Norman Nato had been in the thick of the podium fight in the Racing Engineering Oreca #24, but he went off around lap 110, 18 laps from the end, and the car did not return, leaving it unclassified.

The finish · #21 #28 #39

A lap clear, with a late shuffle behind

Vergne took the chequered flag after 128 laps, a full lap clear of the field, and set the fastest lap of the race on the way, a 1:47.225. The fight for the other two podium places was settled in the last few minutes. Paul Lafargue was running second in the IDEC Sport Oreca #28 but had to stop for a splash of fuel with fewer than ten minutes left, which let Nicolas Lapierre, who had taken over the DragonSpeed car for the final stint and had been climbing the order, through for second. IDEC took third on the same lap.

Graff's Oreca was fourth, and the APR-Rebellion Oreca fifth, a lap down, after a race of contact that included a coming-together with the Panis Barthez Ligier. That car, which had started on pole, finished sixth, ahead of the Duqueine in seventh and the AVF Dallara in eighth.

Aftermath · #32 #31

A third straight win and a bigger lead

It was a third consecutive ELMS victory for Rusinov, Pizzitola and Vergne, and it stretched G-Drive's lead in the championship to 34 points over Racing Engineering with two rounds to go. Nato's off had cost the Spanish team a strong finish at the worst moment. For DragonSpeed, Lapierre had a strong weekend, completing podiums in both the ELMS and WEC races at the circuit within 24 hours.

The win came with a shadow over it. The same three drivers had taken the LMP2 class victory at the Le Mans 24 Hours in June only to be excluded for a refuelling violation, and the FIA Court of Appeal was due to hear the case the following month. Rusinov also said he could return to the WEC at the 1000 Miles of Sebring in March.

For the home teams it was a mixed day. Stevens's pole was the first of the season for a JS P217, but the Panis Barthez car finished sixth, and United Autosports left LMP2 with a tenth place and a retirement. Panis Barthez Competition and Graff, which finished fourth here, both said afterwards that they were weighing entries at the 2019 Daytona 24 Hours. Gibson also marked a milestone over the weekend, with its LMP2 engines passing one million kilometres of track use since their debut in September 2016.

The next round of the European Le Mans Series is at Spa-Francorchamps on 23 September, with Pla due to return to the Racing Engineering line-up.

Molina’s last-lap pass gives JMW Ferrari the win over Proton Porsche

Griffin, MacDowall and Molina won for JMW after Molina passed Proton’s Cairoli on the final lap, with EbiMotors third as Gulf and Spirit of Race’s #55 retired.

Build-up · #77 #80

Proton’s damaged Porsches and a guest from the WEC

Eight GTE cars lined up for the opening round, four Ferrari 488 GTEs and four Porsche 911 RSRs, and the build-up was dominated by the state of the Porsches. Proton Competition’s brand-new #77 was heavily damaged at Turn 3 in the first Prologue session, and Joel Camathias, who had been due to share it with Ried and Olsen, parted company with the team a few days later. Dienst, who had driven for Proton in the WEC, took his seat, and the team reverted to one of its WEC-spec chassis. EbiMotors, running a repurposed ex-WEC Proton chassis as the #80 until its own car arrived after Le Mans, had its own Prologue crash when Babini hit the barriers, but the car was repaired in time for the second day.

JMW Motorsport arrived as defending champions with the #66 Ferrari, Krohn Racing’s Tracy Krohn and Jönsson returned to the series, and Gulf Racing UK entered a Porsche as a guest from the WEC. Gulf skipped the Prologue, so its first run on the ELMS GTE Dunlops came in Friday’s first session. In that damp session Cairoli’s #88 Proton was quickest on 2:01.282, with Gulf’s Barker two tenths back and Dienst third on his first run in the #77. In the final practice the #88 led again on 1:54.253, with the #77 second and Gulf less than a tenth further back, and the best Ferrari was Spirit of Race’s #55.

Qualifying · #88

Cairoli on pole, Gulf thrown to the back

Cairoli took pole for Proton with a 1:53.377 on his first flying lap, in a quiet session for the eight cars in the class. It was the team’s seventh ELMS pole, a record, and the first with the new 911 RSR; Cairoli said he had not been feeling well. Barker had set the benchmark at the start of the session for Gulf with 1:53.586 and could not improve, which left the Porsche on the front row until the stewards deleted all of its times after the session because its rear diffuser breached Article 12 of the technical regulations. Gulf started from the back of the grid.

That promoted JMW’s #66 to second, with Molina’s 1:54.562 more than a second off the pole time. Olsen put the #77 Proton Porsche next on 1:54.830, then came Krohn’s #83 (Bertolini, 1:54.905), Spirit of Race’s #55 (Matt Griffin, 1:54.949), EbiMotors’ #80 (Pera, 1:55.183) and Spirit of Race’s #54 (Castellacci, 1:55.790).

Hour 1 · #86

Wainwright drives through from the back

With the Gulf Porsche at the back of the grid, Wainwright produced an excellent opening stint and carved through the class, setting up the fight to the front that his team-mate Barker took on later. Ahead of them the #88 Proton of Cairoli and the Rodas took up the pace-setting, and it led most of the four hours.

Hours 2–3 · #55

Attrition thins the class

The Gulf Porsche, which had climbed to the front and led for a short spell, was out after 45 laps with mechanical problems. Shortly before halfway DragonSpeed’s LMP2 Oreca driven by Hedman collided with Spirit of Race’s #55 Ferrari at the fast Signes right-hander, taking both cars out and bringing out a safety car. With the Gulf and the #55 gone, EbiMotors’ debutant Porsche, which had been in the mix for a podium all along, moved into a secure third place. The Rodas and Cairoli were almost untouchable in the #88, but their comfortable lead started to disappear as the race entered its final hour.

The finish · #66

Molina takes it on the last lap

In the last hour Molina chased down a gap of about 14 seconds in the JMW Ferrari and arrived on Cairoli’s tail in the closing minutes. The Porsche started the final lap in front, but Molina put the Ferrari up the inside, the two cars banging doors, and took the lead. Cairoli pushed hard for the rest of the lap through the traffic, but could not find a way back past, and Molina held on to win. The last lap was the only lap of the race JMW had led. Liam Griffin said the team had been written off early, and that they thought the flag had already been shown before the last lap, so they were slow to realise what was happening.

Aftermath · #83 #54

JMW win, EbiMotors on the podium

The #66 JMW Ferrari of Liam Griffin, MacDowall and Molina won on 118 laps, the team’s first victory since Le Mans in 2017 and the start of a championship lead for the 2017 title winners. Gianluca Roda, Giorgio Roda Jr and Cairoli finished second for Proton in the #88, on the same lap, and Babini, Pera and Narac took third in the #80 EbiMotors Porsche on its series debut, one lap down. Molina set the fastest lap of the class, 1:54.670, with Cairoli next on 1:54.888.

Krohn Racing’s #83 Ferrari was fourth, a good result for Krohn and Jönsson’s return, ahead of Proton’s #77 in fifth and Spirit of Race’s #54 in sixth, 11 laps behind the winner. The #55 and the Gulf Porsche #86 were not classified. The series next goes to Monza on 13 May.

Proton’s #77 Porsche wins the GTE finale as the Rodas take the title

Ried, Dienst and Olsen won for Proton a lap clear of the class, while third place for the Rodas’ sister #88 sealed the title ahead of JMW’s second-placed Ferrari.

Build-up · #88 #55

Porsche against Ferrari for the crown

Only six GTE cars entered the finale, but the title was still open. Proton Competition led the standings on 80.5 points, with Gianluca and Giorgio Roda, sharing the #88 Porsche 911 RSR with Cairoli, a little over ten points clear of JMW Motorsport’s #66 Ferrari of Griffin, MacDowall and Molina. A podium would have been enough for Proton to win its first ELMS title in eight years, while JMW was trying to defend the crown it won in 2017.

The fight for second mattered too, because the champion and the runner-up in GTE would both receive automatic entries to the next 24 Hours of Le Mans. JMW held that place by only two points over Spirit of Race’s #55 Ferrari of Cameron, Griffin and Scott. If the Rodas finished, JMW needed to win with the Porsche fourth or lower.

Qualifying · #66 #77

Molina takes the point for JMW

Molina put the #66 JMW Ferrari on pole with a 1:42.765, a lap that left Olsen’s #77 Proton Porsche 0.308s behind in second. The pole point trimmed the gap to the championship leaders, and Molina said every point counted. Cairoli was third in the #88 with a 1:43.099, so the points leaders started third in the class.

Griffin’s Spirit of Race Ferrari #55 was fourth on 1:43.239, Pera’s Ebimotors Porsche #80 fifth on 1:43.620 and Bertolini’s Krohn Racing Ferrari #83 sixth on 1:44.053.

Hours 1–3 · #83

Proton’s #77 takes control

The class order shifted early, with the pole-sitting JMW Ferrari and Krohn’s #83 both taking turns at the front. By the middle of the race Proton’s #77 had taken charge, with Olsen stretching the gap, while Spirit of Race’s #55 and the second Proton Porsche chased and Ebimotors’ #80 ran among the leaders until it stopped.

The finish

A lap clear, and the title settled behind

A late safety car reshuffled the class, and when racing resumed the order held to the flag: the #77 won on 130 laps, a lap ahead of everyone else in the class. JMW’s #66 took second, with the title-chasing Ferrari pulling clear of the #88 in the closing laps. The #88 finished third, which was enough, and Spirit of Race’s #55 was fourth, just off the podium.

Aftermath · #80

Proton champions, JMW second again

The #77 of Ried, Dienst and Olsen won on 130 laps, with the #66 JMW Ferrari second and the #88 third, both on 129. Spirit of Race’s #55 was fourth on 129, Krohn’s #83 fifth on 127, and Ebimotors’ #80 was not classified after 82 laps. Molina set the class fastest lap, a 1:43.386.

Third place gave the Rodas and Cairoli’s #88 the GTE title by 7.5 points over JMW, whose pole point and second place only trimmed the deficit. Gianluca and Giorgio Roda became the first father and son to win an ELMS title together. Proton and JMW, as champion and runner-up, earned the two Le Mans entries on offer, with Spirit of Race missing out.
