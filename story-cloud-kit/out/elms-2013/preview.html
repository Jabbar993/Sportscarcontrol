Alpine wins wet Hungaroring and levels the title race

Pierre Ragues and Nelson Panciatici won by 38 seconds in the Signatech Alpine #36 on a wet, drying track, and sixth place left Thiriet by TDS level with them on points.

Build-up · #43 #4

A title fight tightening in Budapest

The 3 Hours of Hungaroring on 14 September 2013 was the fourth and penultimate round of the European Le Mans Series. LMP2 was the headline class and Thiriet by TDS Racing arrived as the reigning champions and the points leaders, with wins at Imola and the Red Bull Ring and a podium in the other round. Signatech Alpine sat second in the table with two second places and a third, and its Michelin tyres promised a win if conditions fell its way. Jota Sport had opened the year by winning at Silverstone with Simon Dolan and Oliver Turvey, and Turvey had taken three poles in a row, but a troubled Imola and a quiet Red Bull Ring had pushed the team back into the chasing pack.

The entry carried several changes. Jonathan Hirschi returned to the Murphy Prototypes Oreca alongside Brendon Hartley, after Mark Patterson had shared the car for the first two rounds. Christian Klien, the former Formula One driver, joined Natacha Gachnang in the Morand Racing Morgan #43 in place of Franck Mailleux. SMP Racing came back with the Oreca #37 of Maurizio Mediani and Serguey Zlobin, hoping to turn the data from its Red Bull Ring debut into pace, and Boutsen Ginion Racing's #4 Oreca was entered for John Hartshorne, Alex Kapadia and Khaled Al Mudhaf.

Friday was dry and cool, and the LMP2 cars were tightly bunched, with the top six inside seven tenths of a second in the second session. Turvey finished the day on top in the Jota Zytek after Signatech Alpine's quickest lap was cancelled because Nelson Panciatici had overtaken under a yellow flag; Hartley was second for Murphy and TDS third. Overtaking is difficult at the Hungaroring, and Panciatici said qualifying would be the key to a win.

Qualifying · #34 #38 #49

Hartley takes pole by two seconds in the wet

Heavy rain fell on Saturday morning and stayed. The GT classes ran first, with spins and gravel visits from the start, and the nimbler GTC cars outqualified the GTE field, the AF Corse Ferrari #62 setting the fastest time of the GT session. The prototypes followed, and a deep puddle at Turn 10 triggered no fewer than four red flags that turned the second half of the session into a lottery.

Hartley was ready before anyone else, sending the Murphy Oreca to the end of the pit lane minutes before the green light while the others sheltered, and his 2:09.301 was 2.093 seconds faster than anything else in the field. Patric Niederhauser put the Race Performance Oreca #34 on the front row on 2:11.394, ahead of Panciatici's Signatech Alpine on 2:12.251, who had been lying third behind the Swiss car when the first red flag came. Mediani was fourth for SMP Racing on 2:12.763, a sharp turnaround after Austria. Turvey was fifth on 2:13.350 for Jota; his hopes of a fourth consecutive pole ended when he locked the rear wheels at the end of the start/finish straight and hit the tyre wall, which caused the first red flag.

The LMPC Oreca #49 of Paul Loup Chatin and Gary Hirsch was sixth on the grid, ahead of the TDS Oreca of Mathias Beche, who went into the gravel at Turn 10 to cause the last red flag with about a minute left. Klien spun at the same corner in the Morand Morgan and had to settle for eighth after having been second when he set his time. Kapadia put the Boutsen Ginion Oreca 15th.

Hour 1 · #37 #1

Ragues takes control

The race started on time at 16:00 on a cold, grey afternoon. The track was wet, with dry patches starting to show. Mediani put the SMP Racing Oreca #37 into the lead at the first corner, but Hirschi stayed alongside through the second and Murphy led again within the lap. Pierre Ragues, whose Michelin tyres gave the Alpine #36 more stability in the wet, took Mediani on the second lap and then ran side by side with Hirschi, who took the wider, wetter line to protect his speed, before taking the lead within the first few laps. From there he pulled away so quickly that the Alpine almost lapped the field with its first stint.

Tyre choice divided the order. Murphy started on full wets, which proved the right call, while Thiriet by TDS Racing and Morand Racing chose intermediates in the hope of a drying track but could not get heat into them in the cold. The Thiriet by TDS Oreca #1 collided with another car on the first lap and went in for a new nose, then lost more time on the wrong tyres. The Race Performance Oreca #34, second on the grid, did not return to the track after the opening laps. Turvey lost twelve seconds on the first lap waiting for his tyres to work, and late in the hour a left-rear puncture on the last corner forced him to complete a full lap at low speed.

Hours 2–3 · #77

Hartley's late charge

Ragues stayed in for almost the first two hours and handed the Alpine to Panciatici with a commanding lead. Jota recovered well from its puncture: on intermediates Turvey cut his lap times by around six seconds on consecutive laps and made up almost everything he had lost, working his way back up to second before Dolan took over. Kapadia ran as high as second in the Boutsen Ginion Oreca while others pitted, but a very long stop later ended that car's hopes. Natacha Gachnang recovered the Morand Morgan from its tyre mistake to fifth place.

Hartley took over from Hirschi well behind and set a string of fastest laps. He picked off Zlobin's SMP Oreca and Dolan's Jota Zytek to take second place, but Panciatici had only to control his lead, and the track never fully dried. LMP2 filled the top six places overall, with the Proton Porsche seventh and the winning LMPC Oreca eighth.

The finish · #36 #18

Alpine wins by 38 seconds

Panciatici took the chequered flag after 99 laps and 3:01:18.622, 38.268 seconds clear of Hartley, and the Murphy Oreca was the only other car on the lead lap. It was Signatech Alpine's first win of the season, and the Alpine marque's first victory since its return to endurance racing this year. Hartley and Hirschi's second place was also a first podium of the year for Murphy Prototypes.

Turvey and Dolan took third place for Jota, a lap down, after the puncture had struck just as Turvey was ready to challenge Hirschi for second. SMP Racing finished fourth, three laps down, having led out of the first corner, and Morand Racing was fifth. Thiriet by TDS Racing came home sixth, five laps behind the winner, after Beche drove the final stint with no pressure from behind. The Boutsen Ginion Oreca was 16th, nine laps down, and the Race Performance Oreca did not finish.

Aftermath

The title race is level

The finishing order cost Thiriet by TDS Racing the lead in LMP2. Beche said the team had struggled all weekend: he never got a clear lap in qualifying, Pierre Thiriet could not avoid contact at the start, and the intermediates were the wrong choice in the cold, leaving the car around 15 seconds off the pace for about an hour. After Hungary the team and Signatech Alpine were level at 73 points, with Jota Sport next on 55, 18 points behind, so the championship would go to the final round with the reigning champions no longer in control of it.

Hartley said he had wanted intermediates at the start but that wets turned out to be the better call, and that he had done everything he could to catch Panciatici; the podium was the first for Murphy Prototypes in a while and a reward for a weekend without problems. Turvey said that the puncture had cost a great deal of time on the wet tyres, but that the switch to intermediates brought most of it back. Benoit Morand said the team should have chosen wets rather than intermediates at the start, and that the car felt wrong until the tyres finally gained some heat; Morand Racing still took points in fifth. For Race Performance the afternoon ended in the opening laps, and for Boutsen Ginion the race was lost to a long pit stop after the car had run as high as second.

Stewards' decisions had also shaped the weekend. Panciatici had lost his quickest Friday time for overtaking under a yellow flag, yet the Alpine still won the race comfortably.

The season concludes two weeks later with the 3 Hours of Paul Ricard on 27 and 28 September. With the championship open between Thiriet by TDS Racing and Signatech Alpine, and Jota Sport 18 points back, the last round would decide the LMP2 title.

Murphy wins at Le Castellet as Signatech Alpine takes the title

Brendon Hartley and Jonathan Hirschi gave Murphy Prototypes its first ELMS win, 6.255 seconds clear of Morand, as fourth place made Signatech Alpine champions after Thiriet by TDS lost a wheel.

Build-up · #36 #34

LMP2 title level going into the finale

The 3 Hours of Le Castellet on 28 September 2013 closed a five-round European Le Mans Series season, and the LMP2 title was still undecided on the last day. Signatech Alpine, running the Alpine-Nissan #36 for Pierre Ragues and Nelson Panciatici, arrived level on points with the defending champions Thiriet by TDS Racing, whose Oreca #1 was shared by Pierre Thiriet and Mathias Beche. Oliver Turvey and Simon Dolan, in the Jota Sport Zytek #38, were the outsiders and would need a win and a stumble from both leaders.

The momentum had swung to the French newcomers two weeks earlier at the Hungaroring. Ragues dominated a wet-to-dry opening stint, Panciatici completed a 38-second victory over the Murphy Prototypes Oreca, and it was the team's first win of the season. Thiriet by TDS had a wretched afternoon in the same race, hit by an early collision and a string of unscheduled stops, and finished sixth, five laps down.

The line-ups shifted on the way to France. Boutsen Ginion Racing's #4 became a trio, Alex Kapadia joined by the Belgian Renaud Kuppens and the Radical racer James Swift, while Race Performance's #34 paired Michel Frey with Marcello Marateotto. Morand Racing's Morgan-Judd #43 ran Natacha Gachnang with Christian Klien, and Murphy Prototypes, whose Oreca had set the pace for the best part of 18 months without winning, again fielded Brendon Hartley and Jonathan Hirschi. Practice stayed dry and warm. Panciatici's 1:50.242 beat Beche's 1:50.244 by two thousandths in the first session, and Hartley then found a 1:49.088 at the end of the second, six tenths clear. Turvey warned that traffic could decide things, because the GT cars pulled away from the prototypes on the long Mistral straight.

Qualifying · #38 #4

Turvey edges Hartley for pole

Qualifying was a 20-minute session that became a duel between Turvey and Hartley. Turvey took pole in the Zytek #38 with a 1:48.043 on the final lap, 0.097 seconds ahead of Hartley's Murphy Oreca #18. It was Jota's fourth pole of the season in five rounds, worth the single championship point that goes with it. Turvey had two laps good enough for the top spot, while the Murphy car was far quicker than the other Orecas, which were around a second slower. Hartley had taken pole at the Hungaroring two weeks earlier, in torrential rain, and again set the pace for Murphy Prototypes.

The two title favourites shared the second row. Beche put the TDS Oreca #1 third on a 1:48.974 and Panciatici was fourth on 1:49.102 for Signatech, and the two had traded places through the session, roughly a second off the Jota and Murphy cars, so neither side gained a grid advantage. Klien placed the Morand #43 fifth, only 0.046 seconds behind the Alpine, and Kapadia gave Boutsen Ginion sixth in the #4 Oreca, less than four tenths from third. Frey in the Race Performance #34 and Mediani in the SMP Racing Oreca #37 completed the LMP2 field, which would start among a 26-car grid at 16:30.

Hour 1 · #1

Wheel off at the final corner

Turvey led into the first corner from pole, with Hirschi in the Murphy #18 pressing him through the opening lap before the Jota driver settled and began to open a gap. Klien moved the Morand #43 past Beche for third after several laps, and the TDS car was in trouble soon after.

Within the first half hour the left rear wheel came off the Thiriet by TDS Oreca #1 at the final corner and spun the car round. Beche took it to the pits and, after repairs, rejoined several laps behind the leaders, which ended the defending champions' title hopes. The car finished 22nd, seven laps down.

Hours 2–3 · #18 #43

Hartley hunts down the Jota

Turvey had built a healthy lead in the first half of the race, but the strategies of the front runners converged as the driver changes came. Dolan took over the Zytek, Gachnang the Morand and Hartley the Murphy, who rejoined in third place. Dolan defended hard against Gachnang, which let Hartley close up, and with 27 laps remaining the Kiwi passed both the Morgan and the Zytek within a lap to take the lead. Gachnang then spun while hunting Dolan, lost time, and recovered to move back past the Jota for second with a dozen laps left.

Further back, Signatech Alpine was losing ground. Panciatici did just one lap before the #36 returned to the pits for a replacement steering wheel, and an earlier stop for Ragues had also cost time, dropping the car to fifth. With the TDS Oreca out of the picture, fourth place was all the Alpine needed. Panciatici duly passed Kapadia's Boutsen Ginion Oreca #4 to regain it before the end.

The finish · #37

Murphy's first win, Signatech's title

Hartley took the chequered flag 6.255 seconds clear of the Morand Morgan-Judd of Gachnang and Klien, giving Murphy Prototypes its maiden win in the championship. Turvey and Dolan brought the pole-sitting Jota Zytek home third, 21.691 seconds behind the winner, and Signatech Alpine's Panciatici took the flag fourth, a little under a minute and a half back. Kapadia, Kuppens and Swift were fifth in the Boutsen Ginion #4, on the same lap as the first four, with the SMP Racing #37 sixth a lap down and Race Performance's #34 seventh, two laps back.

With the Thiriet by TDS Oreca out of contention, that fourth place secured the drivers' and teams' titles for Ragues, Panciatici and Signatech Alpine, who had needed a steering-wheel change on the way.

Aftermath

A new champion and a new calendar

The finale ended with two different kinds of result. Murphy Prototypes finally turned its pace into a win: the team had spent the best part of 18 months setting fastest laps and leading without finishing first, and after second place in Hungary, Hartley and Hirschi completed the job at the last round. Morand Racing's second place was a strong answer to the Hungaroring, where a tyre choice had cost the car dearly in the changing conditions.

Signatech Alpine's title was the story of the day. The French squad had taken its first win in Hungary two weeks earlier, and fourth place in the finale was enough for Ragues and Panciatici to take the drivers' crown and for the team to take the teams' prize in the Alpine brand's first season back in endurance competition. The ACO president Pierre Fillon singled out that return, and the car's competitiveness in LMP2, as one of the highlights of the year. For Thiriet by TDS Racing it was the reverse: the reigning champions arrived level on points and left with their hopes ended by a lost wheel.

Turvey and Dolan had the consolation of the pole and a podium, but not the title. Elsewhere the paddock was already looking ahead. The 2014 calendar was released over the weekend with the season stretching from three- to four-hour races, five standalone rounds with no more shared weekends with the World Series by Renault, and Estoril replacing the Hungaroring. The opener at Silverstone on 19 April would follow an official test at Paul Ricard on 1-2 April, and Le Mans Endurance Management chief executive Gerard Neveu said teams and drivers had asked for longer races and more track time.

Proton wins in the rain as Silverstone is cut short

Ried, Roda and Tandy took Proton’s Porsche to the GTE win a lap clear of the RAM Racing Ferraris when the weather ended the 2013 opener after 48 laps.

Build-up · #66 #67

A new-look GTE class

The opening round of the revived European Le Mans Series came with a GTE class built almost from scratch. The ACO had restricted it to GTE Am rules, with cars at least a year old and a gentleman driver in every crew, and the result was a far healthier field than the thin grids of 2012: five Ferrari 458 Italias against three Porsche 911 GT3 RSRs. Races had also been cut from six hours to three, and the series now shared its weekend with the FIA World Endurance Championship.

RAM Racing, a Silverstone-based newcomer, ran the #52 for Mowlem and Griffin and the #53 for Jeanette and Montecalvo, after being forced to convert its brand-new 2013 Ferraris back to 2012 specification. JMW Motorsport, reigning GTE champions, had signed Ferrari factory driver Bertolini only days earlier to share the #66 with Camathias. AF Corse fielded two cars, the #54 and the #55, the latter with Perazzini, Cioci and Leo. On the Porsche side Proton ran Ried, Roda and Tandy in the #77, Prospeed entered the #75 for Perrodo, Collard and Crubilé, and IMSA Performance Matmut paired Milesi with Henzler in the #67.

Friday was cold and wet. Griffin headed GTE in the opening session by 4.5 seconds, mentioning a misfire that went away after changes, and he said GTE cars were closer to the prototypes in such conditions. In the second session, as the track dried, Tandy went straight to the top in the Proton Porsche with a 2:03.454, Griffin 0.65 seconds behind and Collard third.

Qualifying · #52 #75 #55

Mowlem gives RAM a first pole

The GT cars ran first, in a 20-minute session on a track that was still far from grippy. Mowlem was first out in the #52. Tandy beat the first benchmark by a second, Mowlem answered with a 2:03.114, and a late 2:02.358 put the Ferrari on pole.

Tandy improved in the closing minutes to 2:02.653, three tenths short, and the Prospeed Porsche took third with 2:02.685; Perrodo had been given extra time in the car because he had missed the dry running in practice. AF Corse’s #55 was fourth on 2:02.731 and JMW’s #66 fifth on 2:03.015, with the second RAM car, the #53, on 2:04.412, the #54 on 2:05.198 and Milesi’s #67 on 2:08.745. Mowlem said he had pushed every lap and been on for a 2:01 before traffic intervened. It was RAM’s first pole in the series, in its first qualifying session.

Hour 1 · #53 #54

Rain on the formation lap

Everyone started on slicks, and the rain arrived during the formation laps. Those who headed straight to the pits for wet tyres were back out before the safety car was officially deployed, and the new ELMS rule that keeps the pit lane closed for the first three safety-car laps punished the rest. Montecalvo had made the early stop and led GTE through much of the opening hour in the #53. Tandy changed to normal wets at the right moment, then to full wets perhaps a lap late by his own reckoning, and still held a lap lead; by the end of the hour the Proton Porsche headed the class.

Hours 2–3 · #77

Tandy spins and keeps the lead

Tandy was still in front as the second hour began, and kept the lead through a spin and light contact with Milesi’s IMSA Performance Porsche at Copse. He said the car aquaplaned into the other Porsche and that he went to apologise as soon as he got out. Proton also had a clock to watch: Tandy needed to stop before the 90-minute mark to avoid breaching his driving-time limit, and the safety car’s return, which closed the pit lane for three laps, made that a dilemma. Mowlem, who had completed his stint, suggested stopping the race at 70 per cent distance.

The finish

Stopped at 75 per cent

The rain never eased, and the race was brought to an end after 2 hours 15 minutes and 48 laps. With 75 per cent of the three-hour distance run, full points were awarded and no restart was attempted. Proton’s Porsche took the class win on the lead lap, fifth overall, with the RAM Ferraris of Mowlem and Griffin and of Jeanette and Montecalvo, and JMW’s Ferrari, all one lap down behind it.

Aftermath

Proton wins ahead of RAM

Ried, Roda and Tandy won the class in the #77 with 48 laps, a lap clear of the field. The RAM Racing #52 of Mowlem and Griffin was second, ahead of its sister #53 and the JMW #66, all on 47 laps. Prospeed’s #75 was fifth with 46, IMSA Performance’s #67 was four laps down, and AF Corse’s #55 and #54 completed 43 and 42. All eight GTE cars were classified. Tandy’s 2:17.006 was the quickest class lap.

Tandy said Michelin wets suited the Porsche in changing conditions. RAM went on to win the GTE title that year, and the series moved on to Imola on 18 May.

Chatin and Hirsch cruise to a 14-lap LMPC win

Paul Loup Chatin and Gary Hirsch won LMPC by 14 laps from their Team Endurance Challenge team-mates and finished eighth overall, while Algarve Pro Racing’s Oreca was not classified.

Build-up · #49

A three-car class with a title in sight

LMPC reached the Hungaroring as the smallest class on the grid, with three Oreca FLM09s. Team Endurance Challenge ran two of them. The #49 of Paul Loup Chatin and Gary Hirsch led the standings, and the #48 of Anthony Pons and Soheil Ayari was still in contention for the title. The entry list had Alex Loan alongside Chatin in place of Hirsch, but Hirsch was back in the car for Friday’s practice. The Algarve Pro Racing Team completed the class with Cornelius Jones and Nicky Catsburg.

Catsburg topped the morning session, about eight tenths clear of Chatin. In the afternoon Ayari was fastest with 1:40.659, and the #49 was third in class, with Hirsch getting most of the running.

Qualifying · #46

Pole by nearly three seconds

Wet conditions and a string of red flags made qualifying a lottery for the prototypes, and Chatin handled it best in the class. His 2:13.959 gave the #49 pole and a place sixth on the combined timesheet, ahead of LMP2 cars such as the Thiriet by TDS and Morand entries. The Team Endurance Challenge sister car was almost three seconds slower on 2:16.927 but was still ninth overall, faster than any of the GT cars. Jones took qualifying duty for Algarve Pro Racing and went into the gravel at Turn 10 on his out-lap, so the #46 set no time.

Hour 1 · #48

Hirsch among the prototypes

The race began on a wet track that was beginning to dry. Hirsch made the most of an LMP2 opponent taking a tight line at the first corner and ran fourth overall in the opening laps. The #48 was less fortunate and ran into early problems that cost it a great deal of time.

Hours 2–3

Controlling the class

From there Hirsch and Chatin controlled the class. The track never dried completely, with intermittent rain through the afternoon and a fresh sprinkle with about 40 minutes remaining, but the #49 held its pace while mixing it with the LMP2 runners. The Algarve Pro Racing car was never a factor in the class order.

The finish

Eighth overall

The #49 took the flag eighth overall, completing 93 laps, 14 clear of its sister car. Team Endurance Challenge’s #48 completed 79 laps.

Aftermath

The title waits for Paul Ricard

Chatin and Hirsch won LMPC on 93 laps, with Pons and Ayari second, 14 laps down. Jones and Catsburg were not classified, having completed 58 laps, 35 fewer than the winners.

Hirsch said the championship would have to wait a little longer but welcomed the chance to fight with the LMP2 cars, and Chatin said the aim had been to finish first in class. The title was left to be settled at Paul Ricard on 27–28 September.
