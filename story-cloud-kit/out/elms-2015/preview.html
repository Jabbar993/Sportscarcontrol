Jota wins at Red Bull Ring as Thiriet's challenge ends in a spin

Tincknell, Albuquerque and Dolan won from pole in the Jota Gibson #38, 27.478 seconds clear of Thiriet by TDS, whose late stop-go penalty and last-lap spin ended its challenge.

Build-up · #46 #25 #40

A tight LMP2 title fight arrives in Styria

The 4 Hours of Red Bull Ring on 12 July 2015 was round three of five in the European Le Mans Series, and the LMP2 title fight had already tightened to a handful of points. Thiriet by TDS Racing led the standings after winning at Imola and finishing third at Silverstone, but only two points separated its Oreca 05 from the Greaves Motorsport Gibson. The Jota Gibson, second and third in the first two rounds, sat six points off the lead. A month after Le Mans, when many crews had been reshuffled, Jota was back at full strength, with Filipe Albuquerque and Harry Tincknell rejoining Simon Dolan.

The class grew to twelve cars with the arrival of Algarve Pro Racing, whose Ligier JS P2 Nissan, formerly a TDS car, was entered for James Winslow, Michael Munemann and Andrea Roda for the final three rounds. Krohn Racing fielded Julien Canal as a one-off replacement for Ozz Negri, who was on United SportsCar Championship duty in North America. SMP Racing ran both of its brand-new BR Engineering BR01 Nissans, and Murphy Prototypes came with a second place at Imola behind it.

The paddock was still digesting the previous day's announcement of the four chassis constructors for the 2017 LMP2 regulations: Dallara, Onroak, Oreca and Riley/Multimatic. The weekend was shared with Formula Renault 3.5 and the TCR International Series, the sun was out and temperatures on Sunday were forecast to top 30 degrees. In Saturday practice Albuquerque set a 1:23.538 in the morning that nobody beat all weekend, Greaves was next best in the class, and Gommendy topped the messy afternoon session with a 1:24.202 after red flags for a stalled Ibanez Oreca and a series of Race Control interventions.

Qualifying · #38 #48 #37

Tincknell takes pole after a late red flag

Heavy overnight rain and a thunderstorm left the track green for Sunday morning's two 20-minute sessions, with the GT classes running first. In the LMP2 session the lead changed hands repeatedly. Mikhail Aleshin held provisional pole for SMP Racing at half distance, but Tincknell went quickest just past the midpoint of the session and improved twice more, with his rivals unable to match the pace of the Nissan factory driver.

The session was red-flagged with just under five minutes left when Gary Hirsch, who had had a moment on his previous lap, put the Greaves Gibson into the tyre wall at Turn 1; he was unhurt, and the car had been the second fastest LMP2 in practice. When running resumed Tincknell went straight back out and lowered his time to 1:22.983, nearly a tenth quicker than the previous year's pole, as James Winslow had a moment in the Algarve Pro Ligier ahead of the leaders. Nathanaël Berthon took the Murphy Oreca 03R to second, 0.341 seconds behind, so Jota and Murphy shared the front row.

Aleshin's 1:23.447 gave the SMP Racing BR01 its best grid slot yet, ahead of Gommendy, who abandoned his final lap in the Thiriet by TDS Oreca. Nicolas Minassian in the second BR01 and Leo Roussel in the Pegasus Morgan were fifth and sixth, with the Krohn Ligier Judd of Niclas Jonsson seventh and the Greaves Gibson stuck in eighth after Hirsch's crash. Eurasia, the two Ibanez cars and Algarve Pro completed the class, and all twelve LMP2 cars filled the first twelve places on a 29-car grid.

Hour 1 · #33

Two Safety Cars undo Jota's lead

Albuquerque held the lead into the first corner and began to pull away, only for a Safety Car after about eleven minutes to erase an advantage of some six seconds. He rebuilt a gap of around ten seconds before a second neutralisation, after 37 minutes, wiped that out as well. The delays cost Jota the chance to run away, and they changed the race's strategy: Eurasia Motorsport pitted its Oreca 03R under the first caution and, with Pu Jun Jin at the wheel, gained track position that put it in front of the Jota Gibson.

Albuquerque made his stop after 58 minutes at the end of a strong stint that included the fastest lap of the race. Dolan rejoined in third place roughly twelve seconds off the lead, but he quickly passed for second and set off after the Eurasia car.

Hours 2–3

Dolan and Thiriet fight, then Tincknell takes over

Dolan took the lead around the 83rd minute, when Pu Jun Jin made a small mistake on lap 52, and then did the hardest work of the afternoon. Struggling for grip in the heat, he held a lead of some thirteen seconds after the next round of stops and defended against pressure that would define the middle of the race. In the Thiriet by TDS Oreca, Ludovic Badey handed to Pierre Thiriet, who double-stinted, passed the Eurasia car and then closed right up to Dolan, trying everything to find a way past before coming in to hand over to Gommendy.

With Dolan due to hand to Tincknell soon afterwards, the TDS car looked certain to take the lead, but a slow stop and then a Safety Car for debris reshaped the race. Dolan dived into the pits at once and Tincknell re-emerged with a lead that had grown to around fifteen seconds, with just under an hour and a half to run. At the first attempt to restart, David Markozov crashed the SMP Racing BR01 #27 heavily on the approach to the first corner, which ended its race and brought out the cars again behind the pace car.

Hour 4 · #27 #52

Fuel stops, a penalty and a podium lost

Back under green flag conditions Tincknell opened his advantage to almost 19 seconds, but another debris-related Safety Car with about 40 minutes left turned the race back towards Thiriet. Gommendy made his final fuel stop and Tincknell stayed out, so the Jota car came in later for a short splash of fuel with 20 to 25 minutes to run, and it rejoined in second place behind the TDS car.

The order flipped almost at once. Gommendy was shown a stop-go penalty for passing a red light at the pit exit at the start of his stint, and by the time he had served it the Jota Gibson was back in front. The incident-filled closing stages also took their toll elsewhere: roughly ten minutes from the flag Berthon, who had been holding third in the Murphy Oreca, spun after contact with the BMW Team MarcVDS Z4 of Jesse Krohn and fell to sixth.

The finish · #60 #55 #41

A last-lap spin leaves Jota 27 seconds clear

Tincknell extended his lead by several seconds, but the final margin was swelled by an incident on the last lap. As Gommendy came up to lap the two GTE Ferraris fighting for their class win, Mikkel Mac attacked Matt Griffin into the closing corner and both cars made contact with the Oreca, spinning it. Gommendy recovered and held second, but Tincknell took the chequered flag 27.478 seconds clear after 151 laps, the first victory of the season for the Jota car.

The final podium place went to the SMP Racing BR01 #37, one lap down, shared by Aleshin and Anton and Kirill Ladygin. It was the first podium for the BR01, and it came under pressure from Jon Lancaster in the Greaves Gibson, which finished fourth. Krohn Racing took fifth with Jonsson on the final stint and Murphy Prototypes sixth, both on the same lap as the Greaves car, while Berthon's spin cost his team a likely podium.

Aftermath

Jota seize the lead in the LMP2 standings

Jota left Austria with its first European Le Mans Series win of 2015, the third different winner in three races, and the result turned the title picture over. A team that had been six points behind the leaders arrived at the finish with a two-point lead in the championship, with two rounds left: Le Castellet on 6 September and the finale at Estoril in October. It was a result the team was keen to build on, after missing the 2014 title by only four points.

For Thiriet by TDS Racing it was a race that got away. The Oreca had been the strongest challenger to the Jota car for most of the afternoon, but a slow late stop, the stop-go penalty for the red light at the pit exit and the spin on the final lap cost it any chance of winning. Gommendy still took second place, and the car finished on the lead lap, 27.478 seconds behind. Greaves Motorsport's Gibson finished fourth on a day when it also lost ground in qualifying after Hirsch's crash.

SMP Racing could look back on the BR01's first podium, though the day also included the heavy Markozov crash that ended the sister car's race with no classification. Benjamin Durand, managing director of BR Engineering, said the manufacturer would evaluate its options after its LMP2 chassis bid for 2017 was rejected, including a possible step to non-hybrid LMP1, and that Murphy Prototypes was considering buying a car for an end-of-year run in the Asian Le Mans Series. For Krohn Racing, Jonsson said the numerous caution periods repeatedly cooled his tyres and left him struggling for grip, but a safe, consistent run still brought another top-five finish at a circuit that was new to the whole team.

Algarve Pro Racing's debut ended in 15th overall, eight laps behind the winner, while one Ibanez Racing Oreca finished ninth and the other, shared by Yutaka Yamagishi, was not classified. The next round is the 4 Hours of Le Castellet on 6 September.

Greaves wins Silverstone opener by 0.370s after late LMP2 clash

Jon Lancaster, Bjorn Wirdheim and Gary Hirsch won the 4 Hours of Silverstone from pole in the Greaves Gibson #41, 0.370 seconds ahead of Jota after a late collision with the leading Thiriet Oreca.

Build-up · #40 #32 #33

A new-look LMP2 field for round one

The 4 Hours of Silverstone on 11 April 2015 opened a five-round European Le Mans Series season, and almost every team in the top class arrived with something new. LMP2 was down to 11 cars after Algarve Pro Racing pulled its Ligier out of the weekend when the budget fell short. The two AF Corse Orecas, in the Russian colours of the SMP Racing programme but entered under the Italian team's name and renumbered #32 and #34, were another late change. A tyre war added to the uncertainty, with seven cars on Dunlops against four on Michelins, and nearly the whole field was powered by Nissan V8s.

Greaves Motorsport and Jota Sport both ran the upgraded Gibson 015S, the former Zytek renamed and rehomologated. Greaves had pared its programme back to a full ELMS season plus Le Mans, with Gary Hirsch joining Jon Lancaster and Bjorn Wirdheim, two single-seater graduates, in the #41. Jota fielded an unchanged line-up of Simon Dolan, Filipe Albuquerque and Harry Tincknell, who had returned to the team after Nick Yelloly failed to meet his contract's commercial requirements, and was widely tipped as the favourite. Thiriet by TDS Racing, the 2012 series champion, brought a brand-new Oreca 05 for Pierre Thiriet, Ludovic Badey and Tristan Gommendy.

Two newcomers to the series completed the picture. Krohn Racing parked its American GTE effort to run a Judd-powered Ligier JS P2 #40 in Europe, while Eurasia Motorsport arrived from the Asian Le Mans Series with an ex-Sebastien Loeb Racing Oreca. Practice hinted at the order: Tincknell led the opening session by more than a second in the Jota car, and Lancaster answered with the quickest time of the second session for Greaves.

Qualifying · #41 #48

Lancaster lays down a half-second margin

The conditions improved steadily after morning rain, and Lancaster made the most of the 20-minute session. Nathanaël Berthon had briefly held provisional pole for Murphy Prototypes with a 1:49.681 in the #48 Oreca, and Eurasia had led the early running, but the Gibson runners and the pair of Ibanez Orecas left it late before heading out. Lancaster went straight to the top with a 1:48.752, the only lap in the 1:48s, and was not seriously threatened afterwards. It gave Greaves both overall and LMP2 pole, a margin of almost half a second.

Tincknell had the pace to take it from him but spun through the gravel at Turn 1 with under five minutes left. He rebuilt his session with three flying laps that took him from ninth to fifth to second, a 1:49.212 that put Jota on the front row. Gommendy's 1:49.353 gave the new Oreca 05 third, ahead of Aleshin in the #34 AF Corse car and Berthon, while Roussel put the Michelin-shod Pegasus Morgan seventh. Krohn Racing used the session largely as a test to find a reliable race set-up and lined up last of the 11 prototypes. The new LMP3 class made its mark too: Charlie Robertson's Team LNT Ginetta, on 1:59.892, was the first LMP3 lap under two minutes and faster than every GT car.

Hour 1 · #34 #29

Opening-lap chaos, then Jota in front

The race began at 14:30 and trouble came at once. The #34 AF Corse Oreca spun through 360 degrees at the first corner, scattering the GTE Ferraris behind, and the #29 Pegasus Morgan, which had gone onto the run-off to avoid it, speared back across the track into it. Both cars took heavy damage. The Pegasus needed repairs that kept it in the garage for much of the race and never got beyond 64 laps, while the AF Corse car eventually resumed. A Safety Car neutralised the opening lap.

At the restart Hirsch pulled a couple of seconds clear in the Greaves, with the Thiriet Oreca in pursuit and Albuquerque in the Jota closing on both. After the first round of stops the Jota led from the Greaves and Badey's Thiriet, with Minassian's AF Corse Oreca fourth. Negri in the Krohn Ligier pressed De Bruijn's Eurasia hard for fourth place, with several minor brushes, but the Oreca held him off. The #48 Murphy Oreca was the first of the class into the pits, 48 minutes in.

Hours 2–3 · #7 #63

Greaves hits the front as the Safety Car bunches the field

By half distance the order had settled. Wirdheim was in the Greaves and opened a gap of about 17 seconds over Dolan, after a longer stop for the Jota that the faster Greaves exploited. Badey held third for Thiriet, a lap clear of the AF Corse #32, and was closing on Dolan when a slower Ginetta of the University of Bolton team chopped him at Woodcote and put him into a spin that cost around ten seconds.

With an hour and a half to go the second Safety Car came out, after the #63 AF Corse GTC Ferrari ran into the back of the slowed Massive Motorsport Aston Martin, which ended against the wall on the pit straight. The pause tightened the three leading LMP2 cars into a train, wiping out the gaps. At the restart Wirdheim again built a lead, four seconds over Dolan inside four laps, with the Thiriet Oreca making better use of traffic behind. As the third hour ended the Murphy Oreca stopped on the grass at Stowe, one of three retirements from the class that day.

Hour 4 · #44

Three cars, one lead

The final hour turned into a three-way fight. Gommendy passed the Jota to go second at Brooklands, then went after Wirdheim and, at the third attempt, took the lead at Vale. Wirdheim came in with 45 minutes left and Tincknell took over the Jota a lap later, with Lancaster in the Greaves. Gommendy stopped for fuel and tyres but stayed aboard, leaving the pits some 14 seconds clear, while the two Gibsons began to hunt him down. Tincknell set the fastest lap of the race, a 1:50.405.

The Gibson pair were tangling with each other as they came through traffic. Tincknell made a bold move around the outside of four cars at Becketts, only to lose the car on the kerb and rotate with a bodywork panel coming off, and Lancaster was angry enough to flash his lights at him. By the last ten minutes Lancaster had closed to within a second of Gommendy's Oreca, and the flashpoint came with around five minutes to go. Lancaster tagged the Thiriet car, which spun, and he rejoined in the lead.

The finish · #38 #46

A lead decided by 0.370s

Tincknell had lost time in his spin but was back on Lancaster's tail within moments. With the Thiriet Oreca out of the lead fight he took second, and the last minutes became a duel between the two Gibsons for the win. Lancaster held on through the closing laps, and crossed the line 0.370 seconds clear to give Greaves the win.

Gommendy recovered to finish third, 20.063 seconds behind the winner. The Thiriet Oreca 05 had come within minutes of winning on its debut. Krohn Racing took fourth on its series debut, a lap down, with Eurasia in fifth, two laps back. The AF Corse #32 and Ibanez #45 Orecas followed in sixth and seventh, four laps down.

Aftermath

Stewards take no action, but the sparks fly

The contact between Lancaster, Tincknell and Gommendy was investigated, and shortly after the podium ceremony it was announced that no further action would be taken, confirming the result. It was Greaves' win from pole, shared by Hirsch, Wirdheim and Lancaster, and the final-hour move was the talk of the paddock.

The post-race press conference was far less calm than the result. Lancaster looked uncomfortable, Tincknell openly unamused, and Gommendy said very little. Dolan used the platform to give his opinion on the shape of the 2017 LMP2 rules, a live issue that weekend after the ACO, FIA and IMSA published the outline of a single-engine formula aimed at cutting technical costs by about 20 per cent.

For the others it was a more modest day. Krohn Racing's fourth place met its aim of a top-five finish despite losing the first hour of practice to chassis and engine-electronics problems, and Pegasus and Murphy both left with retirements, the Morgan after its opening-lap crash. For Thiriet by TDS, the podium came on the new Oreca 05's debut, within minutes of a win.

With four rounds to come, the three-way fight at the front looked set to define the LMP2 title chase. The series heads to Imola on 17 May.

Formula Racing takes GTE win as AT Racing's pole unravels

Laursen, Mac and Rizzoli won for Formula Racing, a lap clear of BMW Team MarcVDS, after pole-sitting AT Racing's Ferrari was undone by contact and track-limits penalties.

Build-up · #60 #66

A title chase with eight cars

Eight cars lined up in LMGTE with the championship at a pivotal point. Formula Racing's Ferrari #60 led the standings by five points from AF Corse's #55 of Duncan Cameron, Matt Griffin and Aaron Scott, with Proton's Porsche a further five back. AT Racing and BMW Team MarcVDS were level on 36 points, with the Gulf Porsche just behind, while JMW Motorsport was sixth, 14 points off the lead.

The one new face was Rory Butcher, a British GT regular, who made his ELMS debut alongside Robert Smith and Jonny Cocker in JMW's #66 Ferrari. Richard Lietz returned to Proton's #88 Porsche with Marco Mapelli and Christian Ried. Dunlop used a lunchtime test on Saturday to develop its 2016 GTE tyre, running JMW's Ferrari and Proton's Porsche, and the combination of medium and medium-plus Dunlop compounds was chosen for the first time that weekend.

In practice AF Corse's #51 set the early pace in the first session, with Pier Guidi's AT Racing Ferrari close behind. In the afternoon Pier Guidi topped the class from Griffin's #55, with Lietz's Proton Porsche third.

Qualifying · #56 #51

Pier Guidi leads a Ferrari lock-out

Pier Guidi's 1:55.782 put AT Racing's #56 on pole by 0.036s from Bertolini's AF Corse #51, with Mac third for Formula Racing's #60 on 1:56.024, so Ferraris held the first three places. Pier Guidi had spun on his in-lap yet still took the pole, saying the lap itself held a small mistake and some traffic but was good enough.

Carroll's Gulf Porsche was the best of the rest in fourth on 1:56.207, ahead of Butcher's JMW Ferrari on 1:56.225, a promising start to his ELMS debut. Griffin's #55 was sixth on 1:56.545, so the top six were covered by less than eight tenths. Lietz's Proton Porsche (1:57.238) and Priaulx's BMW Team MarcVDS Z4 (1:57.401) filled the last two places. On the grid the class occupied 13th to 18th overall, with the Proton and the BMW starting 21st and 22nd.

Hour 1 · #86

AT Racing leads, then spins

Talkanitsa Jr led the early running in the pole-sitting #56, with Laursen's #60 and Keen's Gulf Porsche pressing him. His run was blunted when a touch from the Algarve Pro Racing Ligier pitched the Ferrari into a spin, and track-limits penalties began to accumulate against the team. Meanwhile Hassid made good progress in the BMW Team MarcVDS Z4, which had started from the back of the class.

Hours 2–3 · #52

Two safety cars, and Formula Racing escapes both

The first safety car came out around 70 minutes in, to recover a stranded LMP3 Ginetta. Laursen led from Priaulx, by then aboard the BMW, with the #60 gaining a large time advantage by avoiding the queue behind the safety car that caught the rest of the class. Mapelli then charged the Proton Porsche past the Gulf Porsche and AF Corse's #55.

Just after halfway the safety car was out again to recover the wrecked Murphy Prototypes LMP2 car, and again the #60 benefited, effectively sealing the race. The rest of the class, the BMW included, fell a lap behind. Krohn took over the BMW and ran on to second, while Cocker worked the JMW Ferrari up from seventh or eighth and a delayed Lietz rejoined in the Proton.

Hour 4 · #88

A fight for the last podium place

With about 80 minutes left Cocker had the JMW Ferrari running fourth or fifth, and 20 minutes later, after the next round of stops, third or fourth, still ahead of Mapelli's Proton Porsche. AT Racing, hit by penalties, had slipped out of the fight at the front.

The finish · #55

Butcher snatches third

Butcher took over the JMW Ferrari for the final stop, rejoined fifth and attacked, passing Carroll's Gulf Porsche and Mapelli's Proton Porsche to claim third. Rizzoli brought the #60 home for Formula Racing's win, with Krohn taking the BMW Team MarcVDS Z4 across the line second, a lap down. Proton's Porsche finished fourth, AT Racing's #56 fifth and AF Corse's #55 sixth.

Aftermath

Formula Racing extend the points lead

Formula Racing's #60 won on 117 laps. BMW Team MarcVDS's #52 was second on 116 and JMW's #66 third on 115, with Proton's #88, AT Racing's #56, AF Corse's #55 and Gulf's #86 completing the classified finishers on the same lap as the JMW. AF Corse's #51, which had qualified second, was not classified. Pier Guidi set the class fastest lap, a 1:58.120.

It was Formula Racing's second victory in succession, and it left the team 19 points ahead of BMW Team MarcVDS with 22 points covering the top four teams going into Estoril. The BMW's second place was its first podium of the season, and third place gave JMW 15 points on Butcher's debut. AT Racing's pole turned into a fifth place in class after contact and penalties. The final round is on 18 October.

AF Corse sweeps GTC at Imola

The #62 Ferrari of Mastronardi, Castellacci and Hall won GTC by a lap from sister car #63, completing an AF Corse one-two-three with the #64.

Build-up · #62

Ferraris set the pace

GTC was the smallest class at Imola, with five cars: three AF Corse Ferrari 458 Italia GT3s (#62, #63 and #64), the TDS Racing BMW Z4 GT3 and Massive Motorsport’s Aston Martin Vantage GT3. Saturday’s wet running favoured the Ferraris. The #63 of Roda, Melnikov and Cioci was nearly two seconds quicker than the best LMGTE car in the first session.

In the drier second session the order flipped inside AF Corse. The #62 of Mastronardi, Castellacci and Hall set 1:44.637, narrowly ahead of its sister car, which managed 1:44.705.

Qualifying · #63 #59

Cioci takes pole

Cioci put the #63 Ferrari on class pole with a 1:43.704, 0.282 seconds ahead of Castellacci in the #62. Perera took third for TDS Racing’s BMW on 1:44.928, while Poulsen’s Massive Motorsport Aston Martin managed 1:45.856 and Barreiros’s #64 Ferrari, on 1:47.828, was a long way back. On the overall grid the five cars started 22nd, 24th, 25th, 26th and 28th, interspersed with LMGTE and LMP3 machinery.

Hours 1–3 · #68

Control from the #62

Gravel on the track brought out an early safety car, and a stopped LMP3 Ginetta caused a second around half distance. After an early scrap with its sister car, the #62 Ferrari led for most of the race. TDS Racing’s BMW ran on well adrift of the Ferraris, and Massive Motorsport’s Aston Martin went on to be not classified, stopping at 84 laps.

The finish · #64

A team-order podium

Hall brought the #62 Ferrari home first, a lap clear of Roda’s sister #63 car. Barreiros took the flag third in the #64 to complete an AF Corse podium sweep. The three Ferraris finished on 129, 128 and 127 laps, so the margins between them were exactly one lap each.

Aftermath

Results

Rino Mastronardi, Francesco Castellacci and Stuart Hall won GTC for AF Corse with 129 laps. Giorgio Roda, Ilya Melnikov and Marco Cioci were second, one lap behind, and Mads Rasmussen, Felipe Barreiros and Francisco Guedes third, two laps down. TDS Racing’s BMW of Perera, Lunardi and Dermont was fourth on 106 laps, 23 behind the winner, while Massive Motorsport’s Aston Martin (Elgaard, Poulsen, Moller) was not classified.

Hall said the team had managed the gap and looked after the car, calling it probably the easiest win he had ever had. The next round is at the Red Bull Ring on 12 July.
