Thiriet by TDS wins Silverstone opener after Dolan crash

Pierre Thiriet, Ludovic Badey and Tristan Gommendy won the 4 Hours of Silverstone in the #46 Morgan from fifth on the grid, after Simon Dolan's crash from the lead led to a late sprint.

Build-up · #36

A 38-car opener on Easter Saturday

The 4 Hours of Silverstone on 19 April 2014 opened a five-round European Le Mans Series season, and the grid that took the start numbered 38 cars from a 39-car entry after one SMP Racing Ferrari was withdrawn when Boris Rotenberg decided to skip the weekend. Eleven of them were LMP2 prototypes, the class that decided the overall result. The round shared the circuit with the FIA World Endurance Championship, and several drivers, among them Matt Griffin, Michele Rugolo and Sergey Zlobin, split the weekend between the two paddocks.

The 2013 champions, Signatech Alpine, arrived with Oliver Webb alongside Nelson Panciatici and Paul-Loup Chatin. Jota Sport, winner of the rain-shortened 2013 race at Silverstone, paired Simon Dolan with Filipe Albuquerque and series newcomer Harry Tincknell, who replaced Oliver Turvey. Greaves Motorsport ran two Zytek-Nissans, the #41 including 16-year-old Matthew McMurry, given his chance on the international scene after impressing at the Paul Ricard test, and the #28 with James Littlejohn, Anthony Wells and James Walker. Murphy Prototypes lined up Rodolfo Gonzalez with Karun Chandhok and Alex Kapadia, the latter drafted in days earlier to stand in for Tor Graves, and Larbre Competition fielded a new Morgan for Gustavo Yacaman, who landed from the United States on the Thursday night and had never seen Silverstone in person.

Friday set the tone. Tincknell topped a chilly first session on 1:51.041 with Christian Klien's Newblood by Morand Morgan only 0.025s behind, and Tom Kimber-Smith led the second on 1:49.499. Franck Mailleux hit the wall at Becketts in the first session in the Race Performance Oreca, a car built up around its second tub after a testing crash at Paul Ricard, while Gary Hirsch's Newblood Morgan was damaged by contact with a GT car in the second, forcing the Swiss team to rebuild the transmission overnight.

Qualifying · #38 #34

Tincknell takes pole by over a second

Tincknell made the most of his series debut. Kimber-Smith ran first on a clear track and reached 1:48.950 on his third lap in the Greaves Zytek #41, but Tincknell then went round the 3.66-mile circuit in 1:47.702 to put the Jota Sport Zytek #38 on pole, 1.152s clear of everyone else. He said the car was fantastic on the lap and that the gap was a welcome surprise.

Mailleux answered for Race Performance with 1:48.854, enough for the Oreca #34 to share the front row, a reward for a team that had worked hard to get the car ready after its testing accident. Chandhok was 0.058s off that in the Murphy Oreca with 1:48.912, and Kimber-Smith was shuffled down to fourth. Tristan Gommendy put the Thiriet by TDS Morgan-Nissan on the third row on 1:49.057, in fifth. The pre-qualifying favourite, Klien, spun on his outlap and clipped the barrier, damaging the left rear bodywork of the Newblood Morgan, the same corner that had been hurt on Friday. After a quick engine-cover change he salvaged sixth on 1:49.715.

Panciatici could only manage seventh for the reigning champions, and Littlejohn was eighth in the second Greaves car. Jan Charouz was ninth for Sebastien Loeb Racing, Jonathan Coleman tenth in the Pegasus Morgan and Yacaman eleventh, on a 1:52.846 set after barely a day at the track. Jota Sport and Race Performance started first and second, exactly as they had finished the year before.

Hour 1 · #28 #41

Albuquerque escapes, Littlejohn leads the stops

The race started at 14:30 in sunny conditions. Albuquerque led away from pole with no trouble through the field, while Gonzalez took second for Murphy before being handed a stop-and-go penalty for a jump start. Albuquerque soon built an 18-second advantage in the class and was untroubled in the opening phase.

Behind, the order was fluid. Littlejohn made up three places in a flurry of moves to run fourth overall, and the two Greaves Zyteks went past Signatech's Alpine to take sixth and seventh in class, McMurry leading the pair. Thiriet got by Michel Frey's Race Performance Oreca for fourth overall. After about 45 minutes Albuquerque handed over to Dolan, who rejoined third behind Littlejohn and Klien, and when the first round of stops was complete Littlejohn headed the race overall with a lead of about 13 seconds over Klien in the Newblood Morgan.

Hour 2 · #48 #24

Dolan retakes the lead, Murphy loses six laps

Dolan went back to the front as the other LMP2 cars made their stops at around the two-hour-to-go mark, and by half-distance he was more than 30 seconds clear of the Newblood Morgan and the Race Performance Oreca. Badey took the Thiriet Morgan past the Sebastien Loeb Oreca for seventh overall, then Romain Brandela passed Wells' Greaves Zytek at Vale to move the Newblood car up to second, with Badey close behind. Chris Dyson went past Chatin to run sixth in class.

The Murphy Oreca's race fell apart when a puncture developed into a delaminating tyre, and Gonzalez drove the car back from Copse trailing rubber. The team changed the front bodywork and the driver, and the car resumed at the back of the class, six laps behind what would be the finish distance of the winner. It was the end of the car's chance of a result near the front.

Hour 3 · #29

Badey's double stint lifts TDS to second

The Murphy Oreca came back out in the third hour with Kapadia at the wheel, and his lap of 1:51.179 was the quickest of the race at that point. Ludovic Badey, in the middle of a double stint, then dived down the inside at Brooklands to take second place overall from Brandela's Newblood Morgan, and Gommendy and Hirsch were soon locked in a fight for third in class, separated by less than half a second.

The Pegasus Racing Morgan stopped on the inside of the circuit at Aintree and did not return, ending its race after 74 laps. Signatech lacked pace in Panciatici's stints and he picked up a warning for track limits, but Chatin completed his required time and Webb later drove the car to the line.

Hour 4 · #50

Dolan crashes from the lead

With an hour left the LMP2 order was Jota, Race Performance, Thiriet, Newblood and the Caterham-liveried Greaves Zytek, and Jota's win looked secure. Then, with just under 50 minutes to run, Dolan was lapping Rugolo's Ferrari before the Hangar Straight, ran onto the grass, and the Zytek snapped back across the track, clipped the back of the Larbre Morgan and hit the concrete wall hard. The Safety Car, the only one of the race, came out at once and Mailleux inherited the lead as the cars went through the pits.

Dolan was conscious and was taken to the medical centre as a precaution, and was later discharged. The final stops were made under the Safety Car. When the race restarted with 22 minutes left, Frey led Gommendy and Hirsch, with six cars on the lead lap in the class. Kimber-Smith, who had taken over the Greaves car late, passed Chatin for fourth.

The finish · #46 #43

Gommendy catches Frey for the win

The 22-minute sprint was clean and hard. Gommendy was straight behind Frey's Race Performance Oreca at the restart and knew Frey had new tyres, but he kept pushing. Frey was held up while dealing with GT traffic, and at the exit of Copse Gommendy was much quicker and took the lead, passing two cars in one go, in the closing minutes.

Hirsch then went after Frey in the Newblood Morgan and the two ran side by side into turn one with two minutes remaining, but the Race Performance Oreca held on. Gommendy took the flag after 118 laps, 3.828s clear of Frey, with Hirsch 4.731s behind the winner. Kimber-Smith finished fourth, 14.388s behind, with Webb fifth for Signatech and the second Greaves car sixth after a stint by Walker. Chandhok set three fastest laps in a row in the closing sprint, the best a 1:50.757, but the Murphy Oreca was already six laps down and finished eighth.

Aftermath

A win for the form team, a sad end for Jota

The winning crew of Pierre Thiriet, Ludovic Badey and Gommendy had come from fifth on the grid. Gommendy said he had not thought he could catch Frey on the restart, knowing the Race Performance Oreca had fresh tyres, but that he had seen the chance and kept pushing, and called Frey's driving very correct. Badey's double stint and Pierre Thiriet's consistent opening had laid the foundations, and the 2012 champion team had been quietly feared by much of the LMP2 field.

For Jota Sport it was a bitter afternoon. Tincknell had delivered pole position and the team had led most of the race, and a cruise to the flag looked certain until Dolan's crash. The car was retired after 97 laps, but the good news was that Dolan was released from the medical centre. The result was provisional immediately after the race while officials were due to look at incidents in the final hour, and the LMP2 order stood as classified.

Race Performance took second with a rebuilt Oreca three weeks after the testing crash, with Mailleux's drive after his Friday crash a feature of the race. Newblood by Morand completed the podium despite two bouts of damage in a weekend dominated by repairs. Greaves Motorsport placed its two Zytek-Nissans fourth and sixth, with McMurry impressing in his two stints, and Signatech took fifth for championship points after a hard day. Murphy Prototypes, which had put together its weekend line-up at short notice, finished eighth after the puncture.

It was the first of five rounds in the 2014 championship. The series moves on to Imola on 18 May, where Thiriet by TDS Racing will arrive with a victory in the bank, while Chandhok, confirmed as third driver for Murphy Prototypes at Le Mans, will look to turn the team's pace into a result.

New Blood by Morand wins at Paul Ricard as Murphy is penalised

Christian Klien, Gary Hirsch and Pierre Ragues won the 4 Hours of Le Castellet by 5.518s in the New Blood by Morand Morgan-Judd, after a late Murphy penalty ended Berthon's chase of the Signatech Alpine.

Build-up · #46 #24 #34

A title race with two rounds to run

The 4 Hours of Le Castellet on 14 September 2014 was the penultimate round of the European Le Mans Series, and the LMP2 championship was tight. Signatech Alpine, the reigning champions, arrived four points clear after winning at the Red Bull Ring, with Paul-Loup Chatin, Nelson Panciatici and Oliver Webb having grown into a formidable unit after a poor start at Silverstone. Race Performance was a further 11 points back. Three rounds had produced three different winners, and the paddock was returning from a long summer break at a circuit where the Ligier of Thiriet by TDS was expected to enjoy an aerodynamic edge on the Mistral Straight.

The entry list carried several fresh faces. Murphy Prototypes was back after missing the Red Bull Ring and drafted in the Brazilian Pipo Derani, who had tested for the team at Snetterton, to join Nathanael Berthon and Rodolfo Gonzalez in the Oreca. Sébastien Loeb Racing returned with a revised line-up, Vincent Capillaire sharing the Oreca with the single-seater racers Arthur Pic and Andrea Roda, both new to prototypes. Greaves Motorsport gave Johnny Mowlem a seat alongside the 16-year-old Matthew McMurry. Jota Sport, buoyed by its Le Mans success in June, wanted a fourth straight pole to go with its championship push. BoP changes before the weekend were confined to the GT cars.

Free practice offered no clear favourite. Panciatici put the Signatech Alpine on top on Saturday morning with a 1:50.117, 0.203s clear of Thiriet by TDS. In the afternoon Christian Klien took the New Blood by Morand Morgan-Judd to the head of the order on 1:49.473, a mere 0.004s ahead of Jota's Zytek, with the Thiriet Ligier third and the Alpine fourth.

Qualifying · #48 #38 #43

Berthon ends Jota's pole run

Berthon took pole in the 20-minute session on Sunday morning, his 1:47.837 in the Murphy Prototypes Oreca-Nissan beating Jota's Zytek-Nissan by 0.289s and ending the Jota team's perfect record on the front of the grid this season. It was 0.566s quicker than last year's pole time and gave Murphy its first pole since Hungaroring 2013. Berthon said the team had worked hard to perfect the car in free practice, adding that the race was a long one. Derani was nominated to start the race from pole.

Harry Tincknell's 1:48.126 put the #38 Jota car on the front row, with Klien third for New Blood by Morand on 1:48.420. Franck Mailleux placed the Race Performance Oreca-Judd fourth, 0.757s off pole. The championship leaders were only fifth: Signatech Alpine's 1:48.956 beat the Sébastien Loeb Racing Oreca of Pic by just two thousandths of a second. Thiriet by TDS, Greaves and Pegasus followed in the order, while the second Greaves entry, with McMurry, was last of the LMP2 runners in qualifying on 1:51.502. Conditions were ideal and 37 cars lined up for a 13:00 start.

Hour 1 · #41

Derani holds off Tincknell, McMurry out at the first corner

Derani led from pole and, in his first LMP2 race, kept Tincknell's Jota Zytek behind for the opening half-hour. Trouble struck the field immediately: the #41 Greaves Zytek was crashed by McMurry at the first corner and was out on the opening lap, leaving the car not classified. Pegasus Racing's Morgan-Nissan also failed to reach the finish. The opening stint was otherwise played out among the Murphy, Jota, New Blood and Signatech cars.

Hours 2–3 · #28 #29

Four cars in the lead fight

The order changed through the first round of pit stops. Pierre Ragues took over the lead for New Blood by Morand and Gary Hirsch then drove a long stint to build what became a winning gap. Hirsch took Simon Dolan's Jota car around the outside at Le Beausset and held off the charging Panciatici, pulling away from the Alpine. Gonzalez, who had to complete the silver driver's mandatory 140 minutes for Murphy, led during the pit-stop sequences and fought wheel to wheel with Klien on the Mistral Straight, the two cars running inches apart at around 175mph.

Jota split Dolan's stints, with Filipe Albuquerque driving the penultimate hour, but the team lacked straight-line speed and was unable to run with the leaders. The Greaves Zytek of Luciano Bacheta and Mark Shulzhitskiy ran as high as third at stages, while the Thiriet by TDS Ligier was out of the race after 89 laps. Klien went to the front with just over an hour remaining, passing Gonzalez.

The finish · #36

Klien wins, Murphy penalised in the closing stint

With 45 minutes to go the order was Klien first and Chatin second, with Berthon, who had taken over the Murphy Oreca for the last stint, closing in. Chatin and Berthon ran nose to tail at one point, separated by a thousandth of a second on one lap, and a fight for second place, or even the win, loomed. Then Murphy was handed a penalty for refuelling at its final stop with the engine running. It cost Berthon time, and he was also baulked on entering the pit lane. His chance of challenging the leaders was gone.

Klien, who double-stinted a set of hard Dunlops, controlled the gap to Chatin in the final half-hour and crossed the line 5.518s ahead after 4:00:33.427 and 125 laps. Murphy still held onto third, ahead of the Jota Zytek of Dolan, Tincknell and Albuquerque, which took fourth when the Greaves Zytek of Bacheta and Shulzhitskiy lost the place on the final lap. The top five all finished on the lead lap. Sébastien Loeb Racing and Race Performance were the only other classified LMP2 cars, two and three laps down.

Aftermath

New Blood win, Signatech extend the lead

It was the first ELMS victory of the season for New Blood by Morand Racing. Klien said the team had deserved the win and he had managed the tyres in the closing laps. Hirsch said it was a year to the day since he first met team boss Benoit Morand, and that he hoped to repeat the win at Estoril. Morand confirmed that Klien was under contract for 2015 and that the team intended to stay with the Morgan-Judd package, while evaluating a second car.

Murphy Prototypes left with pole and a podium, but with regrets. Berthon said he believed the team could have won and admitted to disappointment, although he felt pole and a podium were something to be proud of. For Signatech Alpine, second place was enough to extend the championship lead to 10 points with 26 available at the final round at Estoril. Team principal Philippe Sinault said the aggressive strategy had been designed to take points from the main rivals, and that the team was targeting a possible step up to the FIA World Endurance Championship in 2015.

Jota Sport's fourth place kept it in the hunt, though it now trailed by 10 points and, even with a win at Estoril, would need another car to finish between it and Signatech. Race Performance salvaged six points for seventh. Four rounds had produced four different winning teams, with Morgan taking two, Zytek one and Alpine one.

The next stop is the 4 Hours of Estoril on 19 October, the final round of the 2014 season.

Rugolo passes Bertolini late as AF Corse wins LMGTE at Silverstone

Cameron, Griffin and Rugolo won LMGTE for AF Corse after Rugolo passed pole-sitter Bertolini’s SMP Ferrari late in the race, with IMSA Performance Matmut’s Porsche taking third.

Build-up · #86 #85

Thirteen cars, eight of them Ferraris

Thirteen cars made up the LMGTE field for the four-hour season opener, and eight of them were Ferrari 458 Italias. After the Paul Ricard test and the Balance of Performance decisions the Ferrari looked the car to beat.

Matt Griffin, the reigning ELMS GTE champion, had moved to AF Corse to share the #55 with Duncan Cameron and Michele Rugolo. SMP Racing’s #72 paired Andrea Bertolini with Viktor Shaitar and Sergey Zlobin, a trio that looked formidable on paper, although Bertolini, as the Platinum driver, was limited to roughly one stint. Gulf Racing arrived with a Porsche, the #86, and an Aston Martin, the #85, after moving over from the Blancpain Endurance Series, while Fabien Barthez was in the Sofrev-ASP Ferrari #58.

Qualifying · #72 #66

Bertolini on pole by six tenths

LMGTE shared its 20-minute session with GTC, and all 27 GT cars set a time. Bertolini put the SMP Ferrari #72 on pole with a 1:57.970 around the halfway point, a lap nobody else came close to. Zampieri took JMW’s #66 second on 1:58.590, 0.620 seconds behind, with Griffin third in the #55 on 1:58.835 and Ayari fourth for Sofrev-ASP on 1:59.166. Cressoni was fifth for Kessel on 1:59.471.

Nine cars were inside two seconds of pole, the last of them Kaffer’s AT Racing Ferrari #56 on 1:59.803, while the Gulf Aston Martin #85 qualified last of the class on 2:04.701.

Hour 1 · #54 #81

Lyons takes charge from the pole car

McKenzie’s JMW Ferrari was in front within two minutes, with Zlobin’s #72 behind him, but the pole car then slid back, caught by Lyons’s AF Corse Ferrari #54 and later by Narac’s IMSA Performance Matmut Porsche #76. Lyons made a bold move into Vale past Kessel’s #81, then passed McKenzie at Club to lead the class after about nine minutes. After one hour the top five were the #54, #76, #82, #81 and #66.

Hour 2 · #82 #56

A ten-second class

Ten seconds covered the top six at one point in the second hour. Perrodo took the Crubile Sport Porsche #82 past Perazzini’s #54 for the lead just before the AF Corse Ferrari made its stop. Rugolo then took the lead back for AF Corse in the #55, diving up the inside of McKenzie’s JMW Ferrari at Vale.

Hour 3 · #58

Griffin reels in Shaitar

By the middle of the third hour Shaitar was leading in the #72 and Griffin, in the #55, was closing: just under ten seconds behind, then five. With a little over an hour to go Griffin went round the outside of the loop and Shaitar ran wide on the exit, handing AF Corse the lead. A later stop cost a few seconds, as Griffin had to overshoot his box with Lyons pitting alongside.

Lyons got the #54 past Barthez’s Sofrev Ferrari #58 for fifth, and Hall was briefly the fastest GT driver on track in the Gulf Aston Martin #85 despite running tenth in class.

Hour 4 · #76

Safety car reshuffles the pack

With an hour to go the class ran #72, #55, #56, #58 and #76, and the race changed shape with roughly 45 minutes left, when the leading LMP2 car crashed heavily on the approach to the Hangar Straight as it lapped Rugolo’s #55. The safety car brought the field together, and only four LMGTE cars were left on the lead lap when the race went green again with 22 minutes remaining.

The finish · #55

Rugolo passes Bertolini

Bertolini was at the wheel of the pole car for the closing stint and led the restart with Rugolo right behind. The decisive moment came with about 16 minutes to go: Bertolini lost time in a tangle with Cioci’s AF Corse Ferrari #54 at Aintree, and Rugolo took the #55 into the lead. SMP could not turn pole into victory, and the #72 finished second on the same lap. IMSA Performance Matmut’s #76, with Narac, Armindo and Hallyday, took third a lap behind the winner.

Aftermath · #80 #67

AF Corse’s win, SMP’s near miss

The #55 of Cameron, Griffin and Rugolo won on 112 laps, with Bertolini, Shaitar and Zlobin second on the same lap and the #76 third on 111. Behind them came AT Racing’s #56 and JMW’s #66, also on 111, then the AF Corse #54 and the Sofrev-ASP #58 on 110, with all 13 cars classified. Rugolo set the class fastest lap, 2:00.077.

Rugolo said the contact between Bertolini and another Ferrari had helped him, while Griffin reckoned he had passed the SMP car three times in total. Before the race Bertolini had suggested the Porsches would be quicker at Imola, where the series went next on 18 May.

SMP Racing take GTC one-two as Formula Racing’s pole-sitter drops to 11th in class

Ladygin, Basov and Persiani won in the #71 SMP Ferrari a lap ahead of the sister #73, with AF Corse’s #95 third; pole-sitting Formula Racing finished 11th in class.

Build-up · #98 #71 #75

Twelve GT3 cars and a BoP shake-up

Twelve GTC cars started in Austria: seven Ferrari 458 Italia GT3s from SMP Racing, AF Corse, Team Sofrev-ASP and Formula Racing, two McLaren MP4-12Cs from ART Grand Prix, two Porsches and a Barwell-run BMW Z4. The BoP was reworked once more. The BMW lost 40kg, the Porsche 30kg, the Ferrari had its restrictors cut by 2mm and the McLaren’s turbo boost was adjusted.

Pro GT by Almeras changed its line-up, with Vaxivière and Hassid joining Perera in the #93 Porsche, and Prospeed’s #75 was confirmed as Van Splunteren, Vannelet and Parisy at the last moment. Team Russia by Barwell’s Cocker said the BMW still struggled for torque because of its weight.

Korjus put the ART McLaren #98 top of the first session on 1:31.272, then Kirill Ladygin led the afternoon in the #71 SMP Ferrari, with the Barwell BMW 0.223s adrift.

Qualifying · #60 #93 #63

Magnussen puts Formula Racing on pole

Jan Magnussen took pole in the Formula Racing Ferrari #60 on 1:30.647, a second pole in a row for the Danish team after Imola. Korjus was second in ART’s #98 McLaren, 0.169s back. The next four cars, Sbirrazzuoli’s AF Corse #95, Beretta’s SMP #73, Ladygin’s #71 and Brundle’s ART #99, were covered by 0.163s and all within 0.5s of pole.

Perera’s Pro GT Porsche #93 was seventh, ahead of the Prospeed Porsche #75, the Barwell BMW #78 and the AF Corse Ferrari #63. Sofrev’s #59 and the SMP #57 of Rotenberg were last, the latter 5.3s off the pole time.

Hour 1 · #78

AF Corse lead briefly, Barwell BMW out

Sbirrazzuoli’s AF Corse #95 led briefly in the opening exchanges, but SMP Racing’s Ferraris soon took charge. The Barwell BMW #78 was out almost immediately and was not classified.

Hours 2–3 · #73

SMP take control

The #71 was the class of the field through the middle of the race, with strong stints from Persiani and Kirill Ladygin, backed by quick work in the pits. Persiani said the Michelin tyres were so good that they could triple-stint them without problems. The sister #73 of Beretta, Markozov and Anton Ladygin would finish a lap behind.

The finish · #95

Persiani takes the flag a lap clear

The final hour was relatively serene for the #71 crew. Persiani took the chequered flag one lap ahead of the #73, which held second ahead of AF Corse’s #95 on the same lap.

Aftermath · #99

SMP one-two; the pole-sitter 26 laps down

The #71 won on 151 laps, with the #73 and AF Corse’s #95 both on 150. ART’s #99 was fourth on 149 and the sister #98 fifth on 148, level with the Pro GT Porsche #93, AF Corse’s #63 and the Prospeed Porsche #75. SMP’s #57 was ninth on 147, Sofrev’s #59 tenth on 146, and the pole-sitting #60 finished 11th, 26 laps behind the winner on 125. The #78 was not classified. The #98 set the class fastest lap, 1:31.775.

Persiani said he was proud of the whole team after a perfect race in the pits. The ELMS next goes to Paul Ricard on 13–14 September.
