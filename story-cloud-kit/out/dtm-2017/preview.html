Martin wins red-flagged Norisring Race 2 from seventh on the grid

Maxime Martin won DTM Race 2 at the Norisring for BMW Team RBM, 2.903 seconds ahead of Lucas Auer, after a half-hour red flag for Gary Paffett's heavy crash with Mike Rockenfeller.

Build-up · #7

BMW's day, Ekström's lead

Saturday had belonged to BMW. Bruno Spengler won Race 1 for BMW Team RBM, team-mate Maxime Martin was second and BMW filled five of the top six places, taking its first victory of the 2017 season. Mattias Ekström's third place made him the new championship leader on 77 points, five ahead of René Rast, who had been given a drive-through for crossing the pit-exit line and finished without points. Mercedes had lost Lucas Auer and Robert Wickens on the first lap.

Because every make had run at least one car on wets, the performance weights were left unchanged, so BMW stayed the lightest. Sunday's race brought a mandatory pit stop, and the timing of it was the main strategic choice.

Qualifying · #31 #6 #51

Blomqvist beats Wickens by four thousandths

Auer had been quickest in Sunday morning's third practice, and Ekström led the first runs in qualifying with Auer second. Neither improved on their second sets of Hankooks, and in the last minutes pole position changed hands three times within seconds. Spengler lowered Ekström's time, Robert Wickens went quicker still, and then Tom Blomqvist's BMW Team RBM M4 took pole with a 47.252, four thousandths ahead of the Mercedes. Nico Müller then beat Spengler's time to take third in his Audi, so all three makes occupied the front three places.

Spengler lined up fourth, Ekström fifth and Auer sixth. Martin, second on Saturday, could manage only seventh. Mike Rockenfeller was eighth, Timo Glock ninth and Rast tenth, and the whole top ten was quicker than Saturday's pole time. At the other end Loïc Duval was 17th and Maro Engel last, 0.638 seconds off the pace.

Blomqvist came into the session 15th in the standings after a dismal opening to his season, and now had pole for the second race of the weekend.

Early stops · #22 #77

Wickens leads, Blomqvist slides back and most of the field pits early

Wickens made the best start and took the lead, with Blomqvist, on pole, falling to sixth by the end of the first lap. Several cars, Blomqvist and Auer among them, came into the pits at the end of that first lap to take their mandatory stop, and the rest followed, since getting heat into the tyres was the key; by about lap ten most of the field had stopped.

Auer and Martin got their tyres up to temperature quickest and emerged first and second among those who had stopped. Paul di Resta and Edoardo Mortara stayed out longer, and Duval longer still: he was the last to stop, and led on the road until the safety car restart.

Safety car · #33

Rast and Wickens collide

The first interruption came when Wickens and Rast touched on the way out of the Grundig hairpin and both ended up against the wall. Debris brought out the safety car, with Duval, who had not yet been in, leading from Auer and Martin. Duval got away well on the restart, but as he concentrated on defending from Auer, Martin dived up the inside of both to take the lead. Spengler also passed Auer, but Auer got by Duval later on the lap and was promoted back to second when Spengler slowed.

Wickens went on to finish eleventh; Rast did not finish.

Red flag · #2 #99

Paffett hits the barrier and Rockenfeller

On lap 33 the race was stopped. Gary Paffett and Jamie Green touched on the approach to the hairpin; Paffett's Mercedes slammed sideways into the barrier, was thrown back across the track and struck the Audi of Rockenfeller, who was passing through the hairpin. Both drivers climbed out of their cars and were taken to hospital for checks.

The barrier needed repairs and the field waited for about half an hour. The race resumed behind the safety car, with Martin leading Auer, Wittmann, Ekström and Mortara.

The finish · #36 #48 #11

Martin holds Auer off, and a photo finish for third

Auer made the better start on the restart and went past Martin into the first corner, but Martin stayed in DRS range and took the lead back a few laps later with an inside move at the hairpin. Auer stayed in tow, but he could not retaliate and dropped back in the closing laps, finishing 2.903 seconds behind.

The drama was behind them. Auer spent the closing laps fending off Marco Wittmann. On the penultimate lap Wittmann got a better run out of the hairpin and went to the outside, but had to back off through the Schöller-S, and that let Ekström by for third. On the last lap Wittmann dived for the final hairpin, pushed Ekström wide, and Mortara swept past both of them on the run to the line. Mortara took third by 0.002 seconds from Ekström and 0.027 from Wittmann, giving Mercedes second and third, with Paul di Resta sixth, Augusto Farfus seventh and Jamie Green eighth. Blomqvist, who had started from pole, recovered to ninth.

Martin won from seventh on the grid, and Spengler, who finished twelfth, set the fastest lap. The 66 laps took nearly an hour and a half with the stoppage.

Aftermath · #5 #53

Martin hails a perfect weekend as Ekström and Wittmann clash

Martin said he was thrilled. The car had been very good, he said, and the fight with Auer, full of overtakes both ways, had stayed fair. He had been third at the Hungaroring, second on Saturday and now first, and hoped the run would continue. BMW motorsport director Jens Marquardt called it a great result for the team and the fans, and noted that the race went down to the last corner. Spengler and Martin had won on successive days for BMW Team RBM, after BMW had gone the first six races of the year without a win.

For Mercedes, after a black Saturday, it was a rollercoaster. Auer finished second despite a cramp in his left foot over the last 20 laps, which made him brake with his right foot, and he said it contributed to a late mistake as Wittmann closed in. Mortara, who said the finish felt like a drag race, had watched the duel between Wittmann and Ekström and was its beneficiary; it was his first podium for Mercedes, and team principal Ulrich Fritz described the outcome as a conciliatory end to a difficult weekend.

Behind the podium, Spengler, Saturday's winner, finished twelfth and Rast and Rockenfeller were out for Audi, which left Ekström as the only Audi driver in the top five. Wittmann was given a warning for pushing Ekström off line, his third of the season, which carries a grid penalty for the first race in Moscow. Audi's Dieter Gass questioned why the sanction was so mild; Ekström said he never files protests, and Wittmann defended himself by saying Ekström had changed direction twice and not committed to a line.

Ekström led the standings with 89 points, two ahead of Auer on 87 and 11 ahead of Martin on 78. Paffett, who apologised to Rockenfeller and thanked the medical staff, escaped with bruising to the collarbone and ribs, while Rockenfeller suffered a small fracture of a metatarsal in his left foot, and his place in Moscow was not yet clear. Roughly 120,000 spectators attended over the weekend. The DTM's next stop is Moscow Raceway, with races on 22 and 23 July.

Green wins a wet Hockenheim Race 2 despite a penalty

Jamie Green won Sunday’s race at Hockenheim for Audi by 4.936 seconds from Paffett, overcoming a five-second penalty, as rain and a safety-car restart scrambled the order and Wittmann climbed from 17th to third.

Build-up · #22

Sunday brings rain and a second restart test

Saturday’s opener had gone to Lucas Auer, who won from pole ahead of Timo Glock and Mike Rockenfeller and left the first race at the top of the drivers’ standings. Sunday’s forecast was colder and wetter, with rain threatening a race that needed no more variables: the new tyres had already shown how quickly they could fall away, and the new Indy-style safety-car restart, with the field lined up in two rows, had still not been used in a race.

Qualifying · #16 #33 #15

Glock gambles on slicks and takes pole

The session ran on a damp, drying track. Everyone started on wet tyres, and Edoardo Mortara set the early benchmark before the cars came back in. Glock was the first to try slicks, around the halfway point of the 20 minutes, and immediately jumped up the order, then drifted down it as others followed and the track kept improving. He saved his best for the last minute: 1:31.406 for the second pole of his DTM career and three championship points.

René Rast took the other front-row slot, 0.175 seconds behind, for Audi Team Rosberg, and his team-mate Jamie Green was third at 0.233. Mortara was fourth, Rockenfeller fifth and Bruno Spengler sixth, with Gary Paffett seventh and Saturday’s winner Auer eighth, 0.662 seconds off. Maro Engel was ninth and Mattias Ekström tenth, having won a World Rallycross round that morning before climbing into his DTM car.

The big casualty was Augusto Farfus. His final lap would have put him second, but he crossed the line a fraction of a second after the session had ended, his time was deleted, and he dropped to 18th and last. Marco Wittmann, the champion, was 17th.

Safety car and restart · #6 #77 #36

A first-corner crash, then a chaotic Indy restart

Glock led away from pole, but at the back of the field the first corner went wrong. Robert Wickens tried to dive up the inside of Nico Müller and the two collided, Duval turned in on them, and Wickens ended in the gravel. The safety car was needed to recover his Mercedes, and Rast, who had bogged down off the line, lost places in the opening moments.

The restart was the first Indy-style one of the season, with the cars side by side in two rows, and it went badly for Glock. He lost three places in a few hundred metres to Green, Auer and Spengler, who passed him as the lights went out. Behind them, Rast and Maxime Martin collided at once, and Martin later said the contact broke his suspension; both were out within four laps. Duval parked later, after the damage from the start.

Green was handed a five-second penalty for starting his move too early, to be served at his pit stop, but he built a gap fast enough to cover it.

Rain and pit stops · #11 #2 #7

Slicks, wets and a late stop for Wittmann

Around half distance the rain arrived, and the compulsory stops became a lottery. Glock and Spengler had already stopped for slicks before the rain strengthened and had to come in again, and Glock later admitted he had gambled that the shower would be short. Rockenfeller also stopped early on slicks and then swapped to wets.

The winners were those who waited. Wittmann, who had been carried forward from 17th on the grid by the restart, delayed his mandatory stop until he could take wet tyres directly and rose into the podium positions. Paffett stopped with the pack once the rain was heavy. Auer, on slicks, radioed that he needed wets, pitted, and rejoined behind Wittmann and Paffett after running wide at the hairpin on his out-lap.

Green stopped a lap later than most of those who had waited, still served his five seconds, and regained the lead when Auer came in two laps afterwards.

The finish · #53 #51 #3

Green holds off Paffett

From there Green managed the gap. He won after 32 laps in 56m53.264s, 4.936 seconds clear of Paffett, who had passed Wittmann at the hairpin for second and held it. Wittmann finished third, 8.191 seconds behind, with Auer fourth, 16.985 seconds back. It was Green’s first win since Zandvoort in 2016, and his 1:33.614 on lap seven was the fastest lap of the race.

Müller held off Paul di Resta for fifth by less than half a second, with Rockenfeller seventh. Glock recovered to eighth, ahead of Spengler and Engel, with Ekström 11th and Mortara a lap down in 13th. Wickens, Rast, Martin, Duval and Farfus were the five retirements. Again all three manufacturers stood on the podium: Audi, Mercedes and BMW.

Aftermath · #99 #63

A warning for Green, a lead for Auer

Green’s win was not secured until well after the flag. He stopped on the start-finish straight to celebrate with his mechanics, and race direction investigated both that and the restart. The result stood, with a warning for stopping on the straight. Green said he had been following the race director’s “green” call, and that he had simply tried to keep pace with Glock. Glock thought differently: he believed Green had not stuck to the rules at the restart, and said he did not understand the five-second penalty.

Mercedes had its own reason to be pleased. Ulrich Fritz called Paffett’s drive sensational after his early setbacks, and Paffett’s second place followed Auer’s win and fourth place. Auer left Hockenheim leading the drivers’ standings on 40 points, with Green and Paffett tied for second on 26.

For BMW it was a weekend of contrasts. Wittmann called his third place, from 17th on the grid, “a mega race” and said it felt like a victory, and BMW Motorsport Director Jens Marquardt praised the fight back. Team principal Stefan Reinhold said the new M4 ran superbly in the rain, and admitted Glock’s stop had been too early after the team expected slicks to last. BMW also took two retirements, with Martin and Farfus both failing to finish.

Audi had won with Green, but lost Rast and Duval to retirements. The next round is at the Lausitzring, on 20–21 May.

Di Resta wins Hungaroring Race 1 from 13th on the grid

Paul di Resta won from 13th for Mercedes-AMG after an early stop and a safety car, ahead of BMW’s Timo Glock and Bruno Spengler, while Audi’s front-row lock-out came to nothing.

Build-up · #22

Audi arrive as favourites, Mercedes lead the table

The DTM made its first trip outside Germany in 2017 at the Hungaroring on 17–18 June, the third round of the season and the fifth race. Lucas Auer arrived at the top of the standings for Mercedes-AMG on 69 points, with Jamie Green, a winner at both Hockenheim and the Lausitzring, close behind for Audi. The Hungaroring was also the first stop under a new way of setting the performance weights: from this round the figures were calculated from race pace, so a strong race for one manufacturer would be paid for with ballast at the next outing.

On pure speed the weekend already belonged to Audi. The RS 5 DTM cars had dominated practice and looked the class of the field, and the paddock expected the brand to build on that in qualifying. The man who would turn it into pole was less obvious. René Rast, in his first full season in the DTM, had started from the front row at Hockenheim and taken a podium at the Lausitzring. Pole position was still to come.

The circuit’s character shaped the thinking of every team. Passing is hard at the Hungaroring, which made grid position worth more than at most tracks and left the mandatory pit stop as the one real chance to gain places. How and when to take it, and whether to split the strategies of the cars, was the question each garage had to answer before the lights went out.

Qualifying · #33 #53 #3

Rast takes a first pole as Audi fill the top five

Audi swept the first five places on the grid. Green had put himself on top of the timing screens with 1:34.818, but in the closing minutes Rast went quicker still with 1:34.742, 0.076 seconds ahead, for the first pole position of his DTM career. Mattias Ekström lined up third on 1:35.192, with Nico Müller fourth and Mike Rockenfeller fifth, each within a tenth of one another.

Augusto Farfus, sixth, was the only driver to break into the Audi group, ahead of Loïc Duval, the sixth Audi in the top seven. Auer was the best of the Mercedes drivers in eighth on 1:35.454, with Gary Paffett and Tom Blomqvist ninth and tenth. Rast said the pole was more than he had dared to hope for, and that starting first meant more here than at Hockenheim because overtaking is so difficult. Green, second, made clear that the two team-mates would not do anything silly but both wanted the win, and that the start was his best chance to take the lead.

The back half of the grid held the surprises. Paul di Resta was 13th on 1:35.575, with Timo Glock 14th by just 0.002 seconds, and the reigning champion Marco Wittmann only 15th. Edoardo Mortara was 16th, and Bruno Spengler 17th on 1:36.154. Maro Engel was last of the 18 on 1:36.484. Robert Wickens and Maxime Martin were 11th and 12th, just ahead of di Resta.

Opening laps · #7 #36 #31

Three early stoppers gamble from the back

Rast held the lead into the first corner and Ekström quickly found his way past Green, so that the Audis ran one-two-three as the field came round at the end of lap 1. Further back it was already a messy race. Blomqvist was hit by his BMW team-mate Martin at Turn 2 and dropped down the order.

The BMW and Mercedes crews with little to lose took a gamble. Spengler, starting 17th, came in at the end of the first lap for his mandatory tyre change. Glock, who had been unable to get past Wittmann, stopped at around lap 7, and di Resta did the same. Neither could have known what would happen next. On lap 8 Martin’s damaged BMW stopped on track at Turn 3 with damage to its front end, and the safety car was called out.

Safety car · #5 #51 #15

The pit lane is closed and the field is bunched

With the pit lane closed, the three cars that had already stopped could stay out and catch up the queue behind the safety car without losing time, while the Audis at the front, who had not stopped, found the gap they had built erased. Spengler used the pause to pit for a second time, a choice he later called a necessity, since a single set of tyres would not have lasted from the first lap to the flag. Race officials looked into whether BMW had gained from Martin’s car being left where it stopped, but took no action, and BMW’s motorsport director called the suggestion absurd, saying Martin’s brakes had locked after the contact with Blomqvist.

Racing resumed on lap 11 with a double-file restart. Ekström went round the outside of Rast at the first corner to take the lead, but further back the restart turned into a mess. A chain reaction at Turn 3 involving Müller sent Auer spinning, while Farfus, who had been the best BMW after a good start, was also turned around. Both dropped to the tail of the field, and Auer, the championship leader, never recovered to a points finish.

Pit stops · #16 #63 #11

Audi pay for staying out

The Audis now had to take their stops from the front and drop into traffic. Wickens, who had been running fifth, dropped out around lap 13, and Wittmann pitted at the same point. Ekström and Rockenfeller pitted on lap 17, which put Rast back in front, but the pole-sitter did not come in until lap 22 and rejoined behind both of them.

That left di Resta in front with a quarter of an hour to run, ahead of Glock and Spengler, with Engel still to stop. Green had pitted on lap 21 and, running eighth, was challenged by Mortara at the chicane, where the two touched and Green cut the corner. Wittmann took advantage of the Italian’s lost momentum to pass him. Engel stayed out far longer than anyone else and did not stop until lap 32. Blomqvist, whose damaged BMW had lost its handling, would finish nine laps down.

The finish · #99

Rockenfeller falls 0.35 seconds short of the podium

Di Resta controlled the closing stages and won by 1.393 seconds from Glock after 34 laps in 57:52.984, his first win since the 2016 season opener at Hockenheim. Glock had held back to look after his tyres, and Spengler took the final podium place from a grid slot of 17th. Glock and Spengler both acknowledged that the safety car had been in their favour; di Resta, for his part, conceded that luck had played a part but pointed out that the Audis were clearly strong on pace.

The last battle was the best of the race. Once Engel made his stop with about two minutes left, Rockenfeller was fourth and 2.6 seconds behind Spengler, with a pace advantage of between one and a half and two seconds a lap. He closed the gap and went alongside on the main straight on the final lap, but Spengler had the inside line and held on by 0.350 seconds. Rockenfeller, who set the fastest lap of the race with a 1:37.101 on lap 20, blamed himself for being too cautious at Turn 1. Ekström was fifth and Rast, from pole, sixth.

Audi left without a podium, and their sporting director admitted the team had made a tactical mistake by not sending one car in early as insurance against a safety car. There was worse to come that evening. Green had crossed the line seventh, but several hours later the stewards excluded him after a Mercedes protest, because a gap in the front diffuser of his car had been sealed, which the regulations do not allow. He lost his six points and fell 14 behind Auer, who kept the championship lead on 69 points. Audi also faced extra ballast for Sunday after a race in which their cars were fastest.

Auer wins the Hockenheim opener from pole

Lucas Auer won the first DTM race of 2017 from pole for Mercedes-AMG, 1.942 seconds ahead of Timo Glock, with Mike Rockenfeller third after an early stop from 16th on the grid.

Build-up · #11

A season opener with a new rulebook

The 2017 DTM season began at Hockenheim on Saturday 6 May, the first of two races that weekend, and a good deal of it was unfamiliar. Pit-to-car radio was heavily restricted, so drivers had to judge their tyres and their rivals on their own. The Hankook tyre was new and wore noticeably faster, and tyre blankets were banned, which meant the rubber could no longer be pre-heated in the garage. Qualifying now paid championship points to the top three, and safety-car restarts were to be run in the Indy style, in two tight rows.

Marco Wittmann arrived as reigning champion for BMW. Mercedes-AMG had a new face in Edoardo Mortara, Audi’s Phoenix team had a DTM rookie in Loïc Duval, and nobody knew what the new tyres would do over a full race distance. Lucas Auer put it plainly before the start: nobody knew what to expect in the long run. He had topped Saturday morning’s second practice session on 1:31.712, ahead of di Resta and Mortara.

Qualifying · #22 #2 #77

Auer alone under 1:31 on a Mercedes front row

Auer improved on his practice best by 0.863 seconds in the 20-minute session and took the first pole of the season with a 1:30.849, the only lap under 1:31. Gary Paffett made it a Mercedes front row with 1:31.042, 0.193 seconds back, and Mortara was third at 0.271. The top three collected three, two and one championship points.

Timo Glock was the best of the rest, fourth for BMW Team RMG on 1:31.161, 0.312 seconds off, with Maxime Martin’s RBM BMW fifth. Paul di Resta and Maro Engel made it five Mercedes in the first seven. Audi had a difficult session: Nico Müller was its best car in eighth for Abt Sportsline, Mike Rockenfeller could only manage 16th, Mattias Ekström 17th on 1:32.082, and Duval was last of the 18 starters, 1.601 seconds from pole.

Wickens was tenth, Wittmann 11th and Jamie Green 12th, ahead of Bruno Spengler, René Rast and Augusto Farfus in 13th to 15th. For a first race on a new tyre with no radio and no blankets, the grid was a poor guide to the finishing order, as the afternoon would show.

Opening laps · #16 #6 #31

Auer escapes while Glock cuts through

Auer made a good start and was quickly clear at the front. The busy man was Glock, who got past Mortara and then Paffett in the opening stages and said afterwards that he had a “mega fight” with the two Mercedes before he could break free. Paffett, who had started second, would finish seventh.

Further back there were two incidents, and both brought a drive-through penalty. Robert Wickens spun Tom Blomqvist in the early laps, and Green made contact with Engel. Both Wickens and Green were punished and slid down the order. Engel, who had started seventh, finished 17th; Green went from 12th on the grid to last, more than a minute behind the winner.

Pit stops · #99 #5 #33

Rockenfeller and Ekström play the long game

The compulsory stop decided the afternoon. Rockenfeller, starting from 16th, opted for an unusual, early stop and was the first man into the pits, a call that put fresh tyres on his Phoenix Audi while the leaders were still running their first set. Ekström went the other way. He climbed from 17th into the points on his original tyres, stayed out until around lap 25 or 26, some nine laps longer than most, and was the last or nearly the last driver to stop.

The overcut worked. Ekström came out on fresher, faster rubber, set the fastest lap of the race, a 1:33.201 on lap 29, and moved up to fifth. Rast, 14th on the grid, was also in the leading group by the end. At the front, Auer retained the lead through the stops, though he said the second stint was much harder than the first: he felt uncomfortable on the new set and his tyres began to fall away.

The finish · #48 #3 #53

Glock closes in, Auer holds on

Over the last minutes Glock was all over the back of the leader, the two of them running almost wheel to wheel for several laps. Auer, by his own account, had virtually no tyre left and had to defend while driving flat out, and he never made the mistake Glock needed. He took the flag 1.942 seconds ahead after 36 laps in 57m23.598s, his second DTM victory. “By far the hardest and longest race of my life,” he said.

Rockenfeller was third, 3.351 seconds behind, and Mortara was only 0.633 seconds further back in fourth. Ekström was fifth, Rast sixth and Paffett seventh, and the first seven cars crossed the line inside 6.956 seconds of the winner. Wittmann, the champion, was 10th, one place better than his grid slot. Mercedes, BMW and Audi each had a car on the podium, and Auer left Hockenheim at the top of the drivers’ standings, with a second race still to come on Sunday.
