G-Drive wins Estoril and steals the LMP2 title from TDS

Harry Tincknell, Giedo van der Garde and Simon Dolan won the 4 Hours of Estoril by 51 seconds in the G-Drive Gibson, taking the LMP2 title as the leading Thiriet by TDS Oreca stalled in the garage.

Build-up · #33

A title decided at the last, in Portugal's final ELMS round

The 4 Hours of Estoril on 23 October 2016 closed the six-round European Le Mans Series season with the LMP2 crown still open. Thiriet by TDS Racing's Oreca #46 arrived 14 points clear of the G-Drive Racing Gibson #38, 92 to 78, with Mathias Beche and Pierre Thiriet leading Simon Dolan, Harry Tincknell and Giedo van der Garde in the drivers' standings. The SMP Racing BR01 #32 of Andreas Wirth and Stefano Coletti was 24 points off the top and needed a win and a collapse from both rivals. There was a prize beyond the trophy as well: the winner of the LMP2 title was guaranteed a place on the grid for the 2017 Le Mans 24 Hours.

The field was thinner than at Spa. Thirty-seven cars were entered and 36 reached the grid, with ten in LMP2. Algarve Pro Racing had sent its cars off to open the Asian Le Mans Series in Zhuhai, Team WRT had treated its Spa appearance as a one-off, and Murphy Prototypes' Oreca was too badly damaged from the Spa accident to be rebuilt in time. Eurasia Motorsport reshuffled its crew for the finale: Pu Junjin and Nico Pieter de Bruijn left for what the team called business reasons, and Michael Lyons and Frédéric Vervisch joined Tristan Gommendy in the Oreca #33. Pegasus Racing's team manager Julien Schell replaced Remy Striebig in the Morgan.

The weekend carried plenty of farewells. It was the last ELMS race at Estoril, with the round moving to Portimão for 2017, and the final outing for the Jota-run Gibson 015S. Practice set an uneasy tone: Beche topped a foggy first session in 1:31.637 before a heavy crash for the Ultimate LMP3 car ended it under a red flag, and Saturday morning's second session was run on a wet track that dried late, with Nicolas Lapierre fastest in the DragonSpeed Oreca on 1:35.671 and van der Garde second.

Qualifying · #23 #40

Lapierre takes pole on a drying track

Qualifying was a gamble on conditions. The track stayed wet for much of the session, and DragonSpeed chose intermediates for the Oreca #21 on the strength of the Michelin runners' pace in such conditions. Early on Dimitri Enjalbert and then Paul Loup Chatin led the way, Chatin reaching 1:45.455 for the Panis Barthez Ligier before Olivier Pla's Dunlop-shod Krohn Ligier moved ahead on 1:45.237 as the flag fell. The surface kept improving through the final laps, and Lapierre, who had lifted on his penultimate lap to clear his run, crossed the line on 1:44.146, his third pole of the season.

Chatin held second on 1:44.553, about four tenths back, and Giedo van der Garde was a late third in the G-Drive Gibson on 1:44.758. Pla was fourth and Vitaly Petrov fifth for SMP on 1:45.857, with Enjalbert sixth. The championship leaders were in trouble: Thiriet by TDS lined up seventh, roughly three seconds off the pole after Beche had an early moment, while Eurasia started 20th overall at the back of the LMP2 runners after a difficult session and a Saturday crash for Vervisch in practice. Race day was set for a 13:30 local start.

Hour 1 · #28 #51

Van der Garde jumps the pole car

The start belonged to van der Garde. From the second row he went around the outside of the grid and took the lead through the first corner, running wide, but Lapierre soon took it back. Chaos behind: Fabien Barthez spun into the barriers at turn 3 from the front row, and a lap later Patrice Lafargue did the same in the IDEC Ligier #28 at the same place, both cars rejoining at the back. Gommendy's Eurasia Oreca came through the field from the rear and picked up some damage in contact.

Eight minutes in Lapierre had a gap of nearly four seconds, but the Gibson reeled him in and by lap ten the pair were nose to tail. Van der Garde's attempt to pass the leading GTE Ferrari, the AF Corse #51, at turn 6 brought contact and a black-and-white warning for both cars, with no penalty. Just before the first stops van der Garde retook the lead on lap 31. He pitted a lap later with Dolan taking over, and DragonSpeed's decision to double-stint its tyres kept Henrik Hedman ahead out of the pits. Hedman then ran through the gravel at turn 3, and Dolan passed him around the outside at turn 4 to lead as the hour ended. Hedman's afternoon had only begun to go wrong, with a big lock-up and more contact that dropped the Oreca to fourth.

Hour 2 · #46

The leaders fall apart

Dolan eased away from Pla's co-driver Niclas Jönsson, who in turn pulled clear of Thiriet. Wirth and Julien Canal's Greaves Ligier were a distant fourth and fifth. The Thiriet by TDS car only had to finish fifth or better to take the title, and for a while all looked well.

It unravelled at the second routine stop. Ryo Hirakawa climbed aboard the Oreca #46 and the car would not fire up. It was pushed on to the dollies and into the garage with an electrical fault, and a place on the podium became a tumble down the order. Beche, clearly dejected, confirmed the electrical problem. By halfway Dolan led by nearly 40 seconds from the Krohn Ligier, and the title was slipping away from the French team.

Hour 3 · #66 #2 #41

Safety car, and a crippled rival

The only Safety Car of the race came early in the third hour, after the championship-leading JMW Ferrari, limping with a cracked gearbox casing, was hit at turn 2 by the spinning United LMP3 Ligier of Mike Guasch. Both cars made it back to the pits, but the JMW was retired. The field bunched up, and Dolan, right on the button for the restart, let the lapped DragonSpeed car through and set about rebuilding his advantage. Canal pulled the Greaves Ligier off with a retirement.

At the next stops Dolan handed the Gibson to Tincknell for the run to the flag. Krohn's long stop put Pla back in third behind Coletti, and with 80 minutes left Tincknell led SMP by 43 seconds. Pla caught the BR01 in traffic before the end of the hour and, after some side-by-side contact, got ahead. Thiriet's car returned to the track with about 90 minutes remaining after 44 minutes in the garage, far too late to matter.

Hour 4 · #32

Fresh tyres, a long gap

With 40 minutes left Pla made his last stop from second, more than a minute behind the leader. Tincknell followed two minutes later for fuel only and emerged with the lead intact, and when the stops were done he held a 70-second margin over Coletti, with Pla pressing the SMP car and Lapierre coming up fast behind both. Fresh rubber made the DragonSpeed driver's pass look easy: by the 26-minute mark he was through into third and soon after went by the BR01 for second.

Behind, Lyons had worked the Eurasia Oreca up to fifth, laps down after its earlier issues. The fight for the final podium place was on between Pla and Coletti, nose to tail for a quarter of an hour.

The finish · #38 #21

Rain arrives as Tincknell wins

Rain fell for the final 20 minutes, adding drama to the battle for third. With five minutes to go both Coletti and Pla lost grip at the rear; Pla spun and rejoined about six seconds behind, and the Krohn Ligier finished fourth, just over eight seconds from the BR01. Tincknell, on an untroubled run, took the flag after 145 laps and 4:01:32 to win by 51.157 seconds from Lapierre, Hanley and Hedman in the DragonSpeed Oreca, a car that Hedman's early errors had taken out of the fight for victory. Coletti, Wirth and Petrov brought the SMP BR01 home third.

Gommendy, Lyons and Vervisch took the Eurasia car to fifth, two laps down, with IDEC sixth four laps behind. The fastest lap of the race, 1:32.898, went to Lapierre. Panis Barthez finished seventh in class, and the stricken Thiriet by TDS car was classified 28th overall, 24 laps behind. Pegasus's Morgan, which had taken the flag ninth overall, was later excluded.

Aftermath

G-Drive takes the title, TDS falls short

The result handed the LMP2 championship to G-Drive Racing, the Jota-run team, and to Dolan, Tincknell and van der Garde, who came into the weekend 14 points down. Tincknell said the team had won it by being the most consistent through the season. Van der Garde, a rookie in endurance racing after a year without a drive, said it was not bad for a first season, while Dolan said the team's behind-the-scenes work had made the difference and that the Gibson 015S could now be retired as one of the most successful chassis in LMP2 history.

For Thiriet by TDS it was another painful finale. A team that had led the standings into the last race finished without the title after electrical trouble that cost it more than 20 laps. Lapierre said DragonSpeed's aggressive tyre choices had not been best for Hedman and that his final set of new tyres allowed him to climb to second. Wirth complained of traffic from slower LMP3 cars, and Pegasus Racing's Morgan, originally ninth overall, was excluded from the results.

G-Drive, as LMP2 champion, earned an automatic invitation to the 2017 Le Mans 24 Hours, one of four awarded through the ELMS.

The next season will look different. The 2017 entry list was due to be revealed on 2 February, and the LMP2 class expects to grow: RLR MSport announced a Ligier JS P217 programme, Villorba Corse confirmed Andrea Belicchi alongside Giorgio Sernagiotto and Roberto Lacorte in a new Dallara, and the Oreca 07 was about to begin testing. The series returns to Portugal at Portimão in 2017.

TDS makes it three in a row at Paul Ricard

Pierre Thiriet, Mathias Beche and stand-in Mike Conway won the 4 Hours of Le Castellet from pole in the Oreca #46, a lap clear of SMP's BR01 and DragonSpeed's Oreca after a late podium scrap.

Build-up · #46 #33 #48

A home round and a famous substitute

The 4 Hours of Le Castellet on 28 August 2016 was the fourth round of a six-race European Le Mans Series season, and it came with the LMP2 title fight tightening. G-Drive Racing arrived at Paul Ricard as championship leaders, but Thiriet by TDS Racing had won the previous two rounds and was closing fast. This was the French team's home event, held a few minutes from the Oreca factory, and it brought a late change to its line-up: Ryo Hirakawa was committed to the Super GT Suzuka 1000km, so Toyota LMP1 regular Mike Conway took his place in the Oreca 05 Nissan #46 alongside Pierre Thiriet and Mathias Beche.

The weekend began badly for Eurasia Motorsport. Pu Junjin crashed the #33 Oreca heavily in Friday's Bronze Test, suffered a shoulder injury and bruising, and was ruled out of the weekend, leaving Nico Pieter de Bruijn and Tristan Gommendy to share the car as a two-man crew. The team rebuilt it in time for Saturday morning. Murphy Prototypes shuffled its crew again for the Oreca 03R #48, with Sean Doyle joined by Jonathan Coleman and former GP2 and GP3 racer Kevin Ceccon, who made his sportscar debut, while IDEC Sport returned with its Judd-powered Ligier after missing Austria and Andrea Roda stepped into the Algarve Pro car.

On track, DragonSpeed set the pace early. Nicolas Lapierre led Free Practice 1 with 1:48.475, 0.147 seconds clear of Beche, and Beche topped the second session with 1:48.505, still half a tenth shy of Lapierre's Friday best. The 12-car LMP2 field ran in 30-degree heat.

Qualifying · #21 #41 #38

Beche takes a second pole

Beche put the #46 on pole with a 1:47.033, nearly half a second quicker than anyone else and 0.481 clear of Lapierre's DragonSpeed Oreca #21 on 1:47.514. It was his second pole of the season after Silverstone, making him the first driver in the championship to start from the front twice in 2016. Nathanael Berthon placed the Greaves Ligier #41 third on 1:48.420, ahead of Stefano Coletti in the lone SMP Racing BR01 #32.

Andrea Pizzitola (Algarve Pro) and Ceccon (Murphy) made up the third row, while the championship-leading G-Drive Gibson #38 had an unusually quiet session. Giedo van der Garde could manage only ninth on the grid, 2.2 seconds off the pole, with Panis Barthez Competition's Paul Loup Chatin, the pole-sitter at the Red Bull Ring, tenth. Pegasus and Krohn Racing occupied the last two LMP2 places.

Hour 1 · #23

Conway breaks away

Conway and Lapierre got away cleanly from the front row, but Berthon's co-driver Memo Rojas, who had started third, was fifth after one lap behind Wirth's SMP and de Bruijn's Eurasia, and lost two further places to Chatin and Harry Tincknell. Chatin then had to pit with an apparent puncture and, although he fought back into the top five, the #23 hit more trouble after its first stop and played no further part at the front.

Conway pulled out a lead, Lapierre closing back up after half an hour without finding a way past, and the pair were 17 seconds clear of the rest. Behind them Tincknell was the star turn, passing de Bruijn with a tow from a GTE Ferrari down the Mistral Straight and then taking third from Wirth. TDS called Conway in after about 45 minutes, earlier than many rivals, who double-stinted their starters, and Thiriet took over the Oreca.

Hour 2 · #25

A well-timed stop

G-Drive's Tincknell stayed on board through his first stop and soon caught and passed DragonSpeed's Henrik Hedman for second, about 15 seconds behind Thiriet. Hedman then lost places to de Bruijn and to Julian Leal in the SMP BR01, who snatched third out of the final corner and moved up to second when Tincknell handed over to Simon Dolan.

The decisive moment arrived at the end of the second stints. Thiriet pitted right behind Leal just as a full-course yellow was called to recover a stranded LMP3 car, and the timing handed the #46 a lead of more than a minute at half distance. Dolan was in third, Julien Canal fourth for Greaves and Gommendy fifth for Eurasia, with Ben Hanley sixth for DragonSpeed, Jonathan Hirschi seventh in the Algarve Ligier and Ceccon eighth.

Hour 3

Dolan hunts the SMP

With an hour and 40 minutes remaining Dolan was all over the back of Leal's SMP, only for the BR01 to dive into the pits for its third scheduled stop and hand over to Coletti. Thiriet came in from the lead with 1h20 to go and Beche took the wheel for the first time, with instructions to avoid risks in traffic.

Further back Hanley attacked de Bruijn, who had returned to the Eurasia car, but could not find a way through. Gaps in the middle of the LMP2 order were still fluid, though nobody was threatening the leader.

The finish · #32

A podium decided with three minutes left

Beche brought the #46 home to win from pole by a full lap after 124 laps, the team's third consecutive ELMS victory and one that took it to the top of the championship. Conway also set the fastest lap of the race, a 1:49.548.

The fight for the rest of the podium went to the last minutes. Dolan finished his stint in third and van der Garde took over the G-Drive Gibson with 45 minutes left, but it was hopelessly short of straight-line speed. Eurasia swapped de Bruijn out for Gommendy to satisfy the driving-time rule for two-driver crews, and in the closing stages Lapierre hunted down Gommendy, who in turn had van der Garde in his sights. Both passed the G-Drive on the Mistral Straight inside the last 10 minutes.

Coletti held second in the SMP #32 with about a minute in hand, and behind him Lapierre found a way past Gommendy through a pack of lapped cars with just over three minutes to run and took third. The SMP finished second, a lap behind, in front of the DragonSpeed Oreca, Eurasia, G-Drive, Greaves, Algarve Pro, Krohn, Murphy and IDEC Sport, all within three laps of the winner. Panis Barthez was 11th and Pegasus 17th.

Aftermath

TDS takes the championship lead

Thiriet by TDS Racing left Paul Ricard with a third win in a row and, with it, the lead of the LMP2 championship, overhauling G-Drive Racing. Beche said it was great to win at a track where the team had suffered bad luck in the past, joking that someone had to finish after Conway and Thiriet had done the work, and said the focus would stay on taking the season one race at a time.

Conway explained that he had been on used tyres in the opening stint, planning to manage them, but the car felt good and he enjoyed a long duel with Lapierre. Lapierre said the fight was mostly decided by traffic, that he had only one realistic chance to pass, and that the team had expected TDS to be nearly untouchable. His podium was DragonSpeed's second in four rounds in its first year in the championship.

Off the track, the 2017 picture was already taking shape. DragonSpeed had two new Oreca 07 Gibsons on order, and Graff confirmed it had bought one for an LMP2 programme next year, while Eurasia and TDS were expected to upgrade their existing Oreca 05 Nissans. Panis Barthez Competition said it would field a new Ligier. The future of SMP's BR01, a car built to a two-metre width that did not fit the revised LMP1 privateer rules, was a question for the Russian team.

The series moves on to the 4 Hours of Spa-Francorchamps on 25 September, with Hirakawa returning to the TDS line-up in place of Conway, and the finale at Estoril on 23 October.

Graff wins as United clinches LMP3 title

Trouillet, Petit and Guibbert won in Graff’s #9 from pole, while second place in United’s #2 gave Brundle, Guasch and England the LMP3 title with a round to spare.

Build-up · #4 #5 #15

United one result from the title

LMP3 arrived at Spa with the championship nearly settled. Brundle, Guasch and England led the standings in United Autosports’ Ligier #2 by 30 points with two rounds left, and a finish ahead of the Duqueine #19 and Graff #9 with a margin of 27 points or more would give them the crown in Belgium. It was the series’ first visit to the circuit since 2011.

The class came with 18 cars, all of them Ligier JS P3 Nissans, after Eurointernational stayed away. The By Speed Factory #5 was run by SPV Racing for the weekend with a late line-up of Rodkjaer, Polley and O’Hara. Oak Racing’s #4 had Peugeot-Citroën chief executive Carlos Tavares sharing with Maris in place of Merlin, and McCaig replaced Warburton in RLR MSport’s #15. United’s #2 headed the first practice session, ahead of Panis Barthez and Yvan Muller Racing; sister car #3 led the second, with the #2 and Graff’s #9 behind.

Qualifying · #9 #3 #26

Graff takes its first pole of the year

A frantic final few minutes settled LMP3 qualifying, with the top seven cars covered by just over a second. Guibbert put Graff’s #9 on pole with 2:14.392, the team’s first LMP3 pole of 2016. Bell was 0.064 seconds behind in United’s #3, and Moore took Tockwith’s Ligier #26 to third after coming close to a repeat of his Paul Ricard pole.

Gachet was fourth for Panis Barthez, Brundle fifth in the points-leading #2 and Ehrlacher sixth for Yvan Muller Racing’s M.Racing-YMR car. The #5 SPV entry never set a time, as the team fought braking-system trouble in the garage, and Maris’s Oak #4 was the slowest car that did, on 2:18.602, from 37th on the grid.

Hour 1 · #2

Brundle jumps to second

Guibbert kept the lead of the class from pole, while Brundle went from fifth to second on the opening lap in the #2. The race was neutralised early, and when it settled down both United cars took the longer mandatory stop at the first opportunity after about an hour. That dropped the #3 to ninth and the #2 to 11th once the cycle was complete, with Guasch, who had taken over the championship leader from Brundle, quickly climbing back towards the top ten.

Hour 2 · #18

Laurent leads at the break

The Murphy LMP2 crash at Bruxelles brought a safety car, and a second crash at the same place by the Pegasus Morgan stopped the field again just after the half-way mark. By then Thomas Laurent was ahead in the M.Racing-YMR Ligier #18, though Graff’s #9 had led the class for most of the opening two hours.

Hours 3–4 · #17 #6 #13

United recovers, Tockwith loses a podium

The long safety-car period compressed the field, and United’s two cars worked their way back to second and third. Tockwith’s Moore and Hanson had looked good for a podium with about 90 minutes left, but a puncture for Hanson sent him off the road and back to the pits, and the #26 eventually finished 12th. Lahaye’s Ultimate #17 started 11th in the class and climbed steadily to fourth. The 360 Racing Ligier #6 made a similar rise to fifth, ahead of Inter Europol’s #13.

The finish

Graff holds on

Petit, Trouillet and Guibbert brought the #9 home first, with the two United Ligiers behind them. The race ended under a full-course yellow while Pizzitola’s LMP2 Ligier was recovered from the gravel, so the order was frozen as the cars crossed the line. Seven LMP3 cars finished on the winner’s lap, 92 laps in all, four fewer than the overall winner.

Aftermath · #10 #16

United crowned in second place

Graff took the win with Petit, Trouillet and Guibbert, while United’s Brundle, Guasch and England finished second in the #2 and that was enough to win the 2016 LMP3 drivers’ title with a round to spare. The team had won the first three races of the season, at Silverstone, Imola and the Red Bull Ring. Brundle said the weekend was a great way to finish the job with a team that produced an excellent car race after race, and Guasch likened the class to European Formula 3 and called the year wonderful.

Bell, Patterson and Boyd completed the podium in the sister #3. Lahaye, Heriau and Lahaye were fourth in the Ultimate #17, ahead of the 360 Racing #6, Inter Europol’s #13 and the second Graff, #10 of Falb, Rayhall and Potolicchio, in seventh. RLR MSport, Villorba Corse and Duqueine’s #20 took eighth to tenth; Panis Barthez’s #16 was 11th and Tockwith’s #26 12th, with Oak’s #24 and #4 next. The M.Racing-YMR #18, Duqueine’s #19, Race Performance’s #8 and the SPV-run #5 were not classified.

Graff was due to stay in the European Le Mans Series in 2017, with a new Oreca 07 and a continued LMP3 programme. The final round, at Estoril in October, now only had the LMP2 and GTE titles to decide.

JMW’s Ferrari leads and wins in LMGTE at the Red Bull Ring

Smith, Butcher and Bertolini won for JMW’s Ferrari #66, a lap ahead of AF Corse, AT Racing and Aston Martin, after starting second to Cairoli’s Proton Porsche.

Build-up · #60

Cairoli sets the pace

LMGTE arrived with no settled order. Aston Martin Racing’s #99 had won at Silverstone and Proton’s #77 at Imola, while JMW’s Ferrari #66, disqualified at Silverstone, was second at Imola. Eight cars were entered: four Ferrari 458s from AF Corse, AT Racing, JMW and Formula Racing, two Proton Porsches and a single Aston Martin.

Cairoli led from the start. His 1:28.824 in the #88 Porsche topped Friday’s practice, 0.240s ahead of Butcher’s JMW Ferrari, and on Saturday morning he went faster again, a 1:27.992 that left Pier Guidi’s AT Racing Ferrari 0.335s behind. Race Control also called several cars up for track-limits warnings in FP2, among them Formula Racing’s #60.

Qualifying · #88 #66

Cairoli completes the sweep

Cairoli took pole for Proton Competition with a 1:27.652 on the last of his five laps, and said the Ferraris were very close but that the team had managed its tyres well. Bertolini was second for JMW, 0.121s behind, and Pier Guidi third for AT Racing; those three were the only drivers under 1:28.

Cioci and Griffin were fourth and fifth in the AF Corse Ferraris, with Turner sixth for Aston Martin, Henzler seventh in the second Porsche and Mac eighth for Formula Racing.

Hour 1 · #55

Butcher takes the lead

Butcher passed the polesitting Porsche, started by Roda, in the opening laps and picked his way through the slower LMP3 cars. Scott, in AF Corse’s #55, tangled with an Oak LMP3 car but recovered to fifth in class. Seefried took the second Proton, the #77, to third within half an hour. On lap 27, while being lapped by the leading LMP2 car, Butcher touched the Panis Barthez Ligier and both ran off, but the Ferrari was able to carry on.

Hours 2–3 · #77

The lead changes hands

Butcher stayed in until the first scheduled stop and handed to Smith. Seefried did a second stint in the #77 and overtook the Talkanitsa Ferrari, and Smith then spun at Turn 1 when an LMP2 car passed around the outside, which gave Seefried the class lead. The full-course yellow near half-distance let most of the class make a scheduled stop. When the Porsche and the Aston Martin made theirs, Bertolini led with an hour and 45 minutes left, and Cairoli was second.

Hour 4 · #99

Ferraris move up

With an hour and 10 minutes to go Bertolini handed the JMW back to Butcher, and Cairoli stayed in the Porsche on the same lap. The final stops dropped the #88 to fifth in class. Aguas in the AF Corse #51 and Pier Guidi in the AT Racing #56 moved up to second and third, with MacDowall fourth in the Aston Martin.

The finish · #51 #56

An all-Ferrari podium

A full-course yellow in the last ten minutes, to recover Krohn’s beached LMP2 Ligier, changed nothing at the front. The JMW #66 won on 151 laps. The AF Corse #51 of Perazzini, Cioci and Aguas and the AT Racing #56 of the Talkanitsas and Pier Guidi were second and third, a lap down.

Aftermath

JMW’s first win of the year

The Aston Martin #99 was fourth and the Proton Porsche #88 fifth, both on 150 laps, with the other Proton, #77, sixth on 149 ahead of Formula Racing’s #60 and AF Corse’s #55. All eight cars were classified, and Cairoli set the class fastest lap, 1:29.166.

Bertolini said the key to being at the front was running without problems and respecting other drivers, and credited the team’s Bronze and Silver drivers. JMW had been disqualified at Silverstone.
