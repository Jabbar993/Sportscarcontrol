Rast wins from 16th on the grid after a safety-car tyre stop

René Rast won DTM Race 2 at Hockenheim for Audi from 16th on the grid, 11.922 seconds ahead of Nico Müller in an Audi 1-2-3, after a gamble on fresh tyres under the safety car.

Build-up

After a wet Saturday, a dry Sunday

Race 2 followed a Saturday on which Marco Wittmann had won the first race of the turbo era from pole in heavy rain, 13.727 seconds ahead of Mike Rockenfeller, with Robin Frijns third. René Rast, who had chased Wittmann in second place, parked his Audi with gearbox failure with six laps to go. Aston Martin left Saturday with its first DTM points, from Daniel Juncadella’s ninth place, although Paul di Resta, who had started third, retired. Sunday was cool but dry, and the second race again had its own qualifying session in the morning.

Qualifying · #25 #4 #33

Eng answers a difficult weekend with pole

Philipp Eng had lost most of the previous two days to electrical trouble on his BMW, and he answered with a pole position of 1m28.972s, 0.416 seconds clear of Wittmann. The lap was more than three seconds quicker than the pole time at Hockenheim a year earlier, a measure of how much faster the turbo cars were.

Wittmann, Saturday’s winner, was second on 1m29.388s. Frijns and Nico Müller put the Abt Audis on the second row, 0.740 and 0.762 seconds off pole, ahead of the BMWs of Bruno Spengler and Timo Glock. Jamie Green and Loïc Duval shared the fourth row, with Joel Eriksson and Sheldon van der Linde on the fifth. Rockenfeller, Saturday’s runner-up, could only manage 11th.

It was a much poorer morning for Rast. He launched the front of his Audi over the kerbs entering the stadium on his first run, then had a lap deleted for exceeding track limits, and a fuel-system problem was also part of his difficulties. He could only qualify 16th. WRT’s Jonathan Aberdein and Pietro Fittipaldi were 12th and 13th, ahead of every Aston Martin: Juncadella was best of the four in 14th, di Resta 15th, Ferdinand Habsburg 17th and Jake Dennis last.

Safety car · #28 #99 #3

Duval’s off brings out the safety car

Eng led away from pole while Rast, from 16th, worked his way up to 12th within six laps. On lap seven Rockenfeller slowed with a technical problem and Duval went into the gravel trap at the Sachskurve, and the safety car came out to recover the Audi. It was a double retirement for Phoenix.

The neutralisation changed the race. Mandatory stops cannot be made under the safety car. Di Resta had come in just before it was called, so his tyre change counted as his stop. Rast’s team brought him in under the safety car for fresh tyres, a gamble because the stop would not count and he would need another one. The safety car stayed out for only a lap, and Rast had to hurry out of the pit lane to catch the back of the field.

The restart was untidy. Fittipaldi hit Wittmann into a spin, and Green turned van der Linde around; both were given drive-through penalties. Rast, on his new tyres, picked his way past the spinning cars.

Pit stops · #21 #53 #11

Fresh tyres carry Rast to the front

On fresh rubber Rast went through the field. Within a few laps he was second, and he then passed Eng at the Spitzkehre hairpin to take the lead. The Audis seemed to have the better long-run pace: before the stops Eng dropped behind Frijns, and was in the clutches of Müller by the time he stopped.

Rast made his own mandatory stop three laps later than the leaders, and Frijns stayed out a lap longer still after a strong stint on a single set of tyres. Di Resta, who did not need another stop, moved into the lead when Frijns pitted, 15.9 seconds clear of Rast, but Rast was gaining about three seconds a lap on the Aston Martin. As di Resta’s tyres faded, R-Motorsport brought him in for a second stop.

That left Rast back in front, comfortably clear of Müller on fresher tyres. Wittmann, delayed by the spin, was working his way back up the order on old tyres.

The finish · #51 #7 #16

An Audi 1-2-3 from 16th on the grid

Rast took the flag after 37 laps in 58:53.819, 11.922 seconds ahead of Müller, for a win that came from 16th on the grid. Frijns, who had made his late stop and then charged back, passed Eng for third four laps from the finish and ended just 0.262 seconds behind Müller to complete the Audi 1-2-3 and a second podium of the weekend.

Eng finished fourth, 17.952 seconds behind the winner, ahead of Spengler in fifth. Glock held off the quicker di Resta by 0.305 seconds for sixth, with di Resta seventh as the best of the Aston Martins. Wittmann recovered to eighth, ahead of Green, who took ninth despite his drive-through, and Eriksson. Dennis was 11th, Aberdein 12th and Habsburg 13th, with van der Linde 14th. Fittipaldi was 15th, a lap down, and set the fastest lap of the race on lap 35; Juncadella was 16th, three laps behind. Rockenfeller and Duval did not finish.

Aftermath · #76 #62 #23

Wittmann leads the standings; Zolder next

Rast called it one of his craziest races ever. Team Rosberg’s strategists had worked out beforehand that an extra tyre change after a safety car might pay off, and he said that from 16th, or anywhere outside the top ten, a driver has to gamble. The stop had been nervy: with the safety car out for just one lap, he had to hurry out of the pits to catch the field. Once on fresh tyres he estimated an advantage of two to three seconds a lap over cars ten laps into their sets, and he thanked the team after a bad day on Saturday. He added that tyres were the biggest challenge of the new cars, a battle in which a driver is mainly trying to survive and keep the car out of the gravel, and Audi’s motorsport chief Dieter Gass said tyre wear after a safety car restart gives a fresh set a clear edge.

For the others the day brought mixed feelings. Eng missed the podium after starting from pole, saying Frijns and the other Audi drivers had been very quick in the last five laps, but he was glad to have been able to race after Saturday’s trouble and praised his mechanics. Frijns finished the weekend with two third places. Di Resta said that two stops had not been the original plan: the team had spotted Duval’s Audi in the gravel and gambled on a safety car, and the second stop was forced by tyre wear. Seventh place, after qualifying third in the wet on Saturday, left him and Aston Martin pleased with their first weekend.

The result reshaped the standings. Wittmann, despite being spun, led on 34 points, with Frijns on 31 and Rast, who had scored nothing on Saturday, on 25. Müller was fourth on 22. Apart from Wittmann’s Saturday win, every podium place of the weekend had gone to Audi.

The DTM next goes to Zolder in Belgium on 18–19 May. For Aston Martin, whose four cars were at the back in dry qualifying and finished 7th, 11th, 13th and 16th on Sunday, the task is to close that gap. Habsburg said the dry session showed the true picture but that the deficit to tenth place was only around seven tenths, and that closing it would put the team in the mix.

Rast wins Zolder Race 2 as Eng takes the points lead

René Rast won Race 2 at Zolder for Audi Sport Team Rosberg by 9.845 seconds from Philipp Eng, who took the championship lead after a safety car and a late fight for third.

Build-up

A safety car reshapes the standings

Saturday’s opener had turned on a safety car. Philipp Eng won from seventh on the grid for BMW Team RMR, with Joel Eriksson second and Nico Müller third, after the leaders Bruno Spengler, Marco Wittmann and René Rast were caught before their mandatory stops. Wittmann, who started from pole, finished seventh but kept the championship lead on 43 points, ahead of Eng on 40 and Müller on 37.

Rast had dropped out of the race with a technical problem. At Hockenheim he had followed a difficult Saturday with a Sunday win from 16th on the grid, so Zolder offered him a second chance to recover on the second day.

Qualifying · #31 #3

Van der Linde takes pole by 0.006 seconds

Sheldon van der Linde took the first DTM pole of his career for BMW Team RBM, his 1:21.590 beating Rast’s 1:21.596 by just 0.006 seconds. The rookie had run into the gravel at Turn 4 straight after setting his time, but kept enough momentum to rejoin and stayed on top when the session ended.

Eng qualified third on 1:21.714, ahead of Robin Frijns and Timo Glock. Mike Rockenfeller was sixth after the clutch problem that left him 16th on Saturday, with Wittmann seventh, Jonathan Aberdein eighth, Jamie Green ninth and Eriksson tenth. Jake Dennis led R-Motorsport in 13th, ahead of Pietro Fittipaldi and Spengler, who was 15th.

The session was stopped by a red flag in the closing minutes when Paul di Resta stopped his Aston Martin on track, and he did not start the race from 18th. Ferdinand Habsburg lined up 17th and Daniel Juncadella 16th.

Opening laps · #33 #25

Rast passes the pole-sitter on lap 11

Rast made a poor start and van der Linde kept the lead. Eng, attacking in the opening laps, made a mistake and dropped from third to fifth, which left Rast free to chase the leader. Rast spent the first stint alternately backing off and pushing to look after his tyres, since wear was seen as the key to passing at Zolder.

He finally got by at Turn 1 on lap 11, and van der Linde pitted at the end of that lap. Eng had stopped early, gained a place on Glock with the undercut and passed Frijns on track with a move down the hill. Rast came out of the stops ahead of the others but had to defend hard on cold tyres.

Safety car · #4 #11 #16

Wittmann hits Frijns and the safety car comes out

Once the stops were done, Wittmann hit the rear-left of Frijns’s Audi in the fight for fifth and spun him off. Frijns said afterwards that his Audi had lost power at that moment. The recovery of the Audi brought out the safety car for five laps, and the race was extended by five minutes plus one lap to make up the time.

Wittmann was penalised for the contact. At the IndyCar-style restart he and Glock ran onto the grass and lost ground, and Wittmann eventually finished 13th. Rast held his lead through the restart despite a minor error at the Villeneuve chicane, and Eng slid past van der Linde to take second.

The finish · #53 #99 #76

Rast wins by 9.845 seconds as Green steals third

Rast pulled clear after the restart and won by 9.845 seconds from Eng, who also set the fastest lap. The fight for the last place on the podium went to the final lap. Green, on a two-stop strategy and fresh tyres, had come through the pack, and he passed van der Linde for third at Turn 4. Rockenfeller dived down the inside, ran across the grass in the process and failed to take third, but he did take fourth from van der Linde, who finished fifth.

Dennis was sixth, the best result of the season so far for R-Motorsport. He had passed Glock, who stopped one lap from the end and was classified 14th, a lap down. Spengler, Müller, Fittipaldi and Eriksson completed the top ten, with Duval 11th, Aberdein 12th and Wittmann 13th. Juncadella and Habsburg retired with technical problems, Frijns was out after the contact, and di Resta did not start.

Aftermath

Eng leads the championship, Wittmann loses ground

Rast called his Audi fantastic and said he would like to win on a Saturday for a change, rather than only on Sundays. He said that the points lost in the retirement on Saturday had been valuable, as they had been at Hockenheim, and that it was good to have held contact with the leaders over the weekend.

Eng left Belgium leading the championship after a win and a second place. He said he could get used to the lead. He also said that he had been too ambitious on the opening lap, trying to go around van der Linde on the outside, locking a wheel and letting three cars through, and that a better start could have given him more.

Wittmann went into the weekend as championship leader and left without points on Sunday, a day after the safety car had cost him the chance of a win. He accepted the penalty and apologised to Frijns, who said he had lost power at that moment, while DTM chief Gerhard Berger thought the incident was a misjudgement and not deliberate. Berger also named van der Linde as the man of the race, even though the BMW driver lost third place on the last lap and finished fifth.

For R-Motorsport, Dennis’s sixth place was the high point of a weekend that also brought a double retirement on Sunday and di Resta’s non-start. The next round is at Misano on 8–9 June.

Müller wins Lausitzring Race 1 after Rast stops from the lead

Nico Müller took DTM Race 1 at the Lausitzring for Abt Sportsline by 2.496 seconds from Robin Frijns after pole-sitter René Rast slowed with a technical fault, and Audi filled the podium.

Build-up · #33 #51

A title fight inside Audi, and a bumpy circuit for the tyres

The DTM came to the Lausitzring for round seven, on 24–25 August, with Audi controlling both championships. René Rast had won the Sunday race at Brands Hatch two weeks earlier and arrived on 206 points, 37 clear of Audi team-mate Nico Müller. BMW’s Marco Wittmann was a further 22 points behind Müller, and Philipp Eng was fourth. Rast had scored in ten of the twelve races so far, with four wins, and much of his cushion had been built in qualifying. Müller’s message ahead of the weekend was that beating him meant winning races and finishing ahead of him, and that his own qualifying had to improve.

Audi also had a second target. If it outscored BMW by 20 points across the weekend, it would take the manufacturers’ title with two rounds still to run. The Brands Hatch weekend had left BMW openly sniping at Audi, and Wittmann, the only BMW still within reach of the leaders, needed the two Audi title rivals to slip.

The 4.570-kilometre Grand Prix circuit is run anti-clockwise and rewards top speed on the start-and-finish straights, while its infield is slow and full of chicanes. Some drivers call it a mogul run because of the bouncing, particularly under braking. Its fine-pored asphalt offers little grip, so Hankook warned that a fresh, unheated set of tyres after a pit stop would need careful handling, and that the new turbo cars, with more than 600 hp, would spin their wheels more often. Many teams had been splitting their races into one short stint and one very long one, and drivers who could stretch the grip without a drop-off were free to react to events rather than follow a fixed stop window.

Qualifying · #53 #21 #76

Rast takes his sixth pole with a last-minute lap

Rast needed a late lap to get there. He had been as low as eighth after mistakes on his first run, while Mike Rockenfeller held provisional pole after the first runs. In the closing minutes Rast and Müller traded best sectors, and Rast crossed the line with a 1m35.241s, his sixth pole of the season and worth three points.

Jamie Green split the title rivals in second on 1m35.286s, 0.045 seconds off. Müller finished just behind Rast’s lap and had to settle for third on 1m35.438s, with Robin Frijns fourth on 1m35.537s, which gave Audi the first four places on the grid. Wittmann was fifth on 1m35.581s, the best BMW and 0.340 seconds from pole, and Rockenfeller sixth.

Pietro Fittipaldi was seventh in the WRT Audi, though moments after his fastest lap the right-hand door of his car came loose and sent him into the pits. Eng was eighth for BMW, Timo Glock ninth after skating across the gravel early in the session, and Joel Eriksson tenth. Loïc Duval was 11th and Bruno Spengler 12th. Jake Dennis was the best of the R-Motorsport Aston Martins in 13th, Paul di Resta 16th and Daniel Juncadella 17th, while Ferdinand Habsburg was last on 1m38.487s, 3.2 seconds off the pole time.

Shortly after qualifying the sporting commission confirmed that R-Motorsport had asked for the seals on four of its engines to be broken. Under the rules, none of the four Aston Martins could score manufacturers’ points on Saturday, though the driver and team classifications were unaffected.

Opening laps · #11 #25

Müller beats Wittmann to second, then Rast slows

Rast defended the lead from pole without trouble. Behind him Wittmann broke into the Audi group before the first corner and fought Müller for second, and Müller held him off through Turn 1. Green had a slow getaway and lost places, and Frijns soon moved up to third. Eng made the best start of the BMWs, passing Rockenfeller and Fittipaldi to complete lap one in sixth. The three Aston Martins of Habsburg, Di Resta and Dennis ran side by side early on and touched, without lasting consequences.

On the seventh lap everything changed at the front. Müller was within a second of Rast when the leader slowed through the Turns 2–5 complex and pulled onto the unused banking. Müller and Wittmann went by. Rast rejoined, but about 30 seconds off the lead and at the back of the field, and he stopped for good in the pits on the next lap. He said afterwards that he had lost power and the power steering, that it happened twice and that he had nearly gone off, so there was no point continuing. Audi said at the time that the cause was not yet identified.

Pit stops · #4 #16

Frijns jumps Wittmann, and the pit lane costs Green

With the mandatory tyre change approaching, Duval was the first to stop, on lap 10. Frijns followed on lap 12 and was the first of the front-runners in. Wittmann, running second behind Müller, came in at the end of lap 13, and Müller was called in a lap later after a stop of 8.5 seconds, which was slower than ideal but still left him ahead of both. Frijns’s tyres were already up to temperature, and he dived to the inside of the BMW, whose tyres were cold, at the Turn 6 left-hander. That move decided second place.

The pit lane was busy. Eng lost a place to Fittipaldi in the pit lane on lap 15. Green and Aberdein were each given a drive-through penalty for pit-lane infringements, which dropped Green to 15th. Rockenfeller and Green stopped on lap 17, and Rockenfeller held off Green and Duval before setting off after the podium. Glock’s BMW stopped for good soon after his stop, while Sheldon van der Linde lost time late on when he had to come in to change the left door of his car.

The finish · #99 #28

Müller wins by 2.5 seconds as Rockenfeller passes Wittmann

Müller took the flag after 34 laps in 56m53.891s, 2.496 seconds ahead of Frijns. Rockenfeller finished 3.206 seconds behind the winner, after a run of fast laps that closed him on Wittmann. His first attempt at the BMW, at Turn 1, failed, and he got through on the outside on lap 27 to complete an Audi one-two-three. Wittmann was fourth, 5.807 seconds off the win.

Eng finished fifth after a late fight with Duval, who took sixth ahead of Fittipaldi. Eriksson was eighth and Spengler ninth, and Green recovered from the penalty to take 10th. Dennis was 11th, just outside the points, followed by Juncadella and Di Resta, with Aberdein 14th, Habsburg 15th and van der Linde 16th. Frijns set the fastest lap, a 1m38.176s on lap 16. Glock and Rast did not finish.

The result left Müller 14 points behind Rast, who stayed on 209 points to Müller’s 195, with Wittmann 50 behind the leader. It was Müller’s second win of the season, and his first at the Lausitzring. He said it was not how he had wanted to win, but that his team had known it would have to take big points whenever Rast had a problem. Wittmann conceded that BMW did not have the pace of the Audis.

Wittmann wins a soaked Assen opener as Müller's late stop falls short

Marco Wittmann won Race 1 at Assen from pole for BMW Team RMG, passing Nico Müller's Audi after the Swiss driver's late stop, with Müller 3.137 seconds back and René Rast third.

Build-up · #33

The DTM's first visit to Assen, with two Audis fighting for the title

The DTM arrived at TT Circuit Assen on 20–21 July for the first time, a circuit built with motorcycles in mind. The round fell at the halfway point of an 18-race season, and Race 1 was the ninth of them. Audi's René Rast of Team Rosberg led the championship, with his fellow Audi driver Nico Müller of Abt Sportsline the closest challenger, and the pair had already tangled at the Norisring two weeks earlier.

For BMW, the weekend followed a difficult home round. Marco Wittmann, a former champion, had struggled badly at the Norisring, which made Assen a chance to bounce back. R-Motorsport's Aston Martin Vantage DTM, new to the series in 2019, ran four cars, while Audi and BMW fielded the rest of an 18-car field. Both races of the weekend carried a mandatory pit stop, and the whole field ran on Hankook tyres.

Qualifying · #11 #28 #21

Wittmann takes pole by 0.158 seconds

Wittmann put his BMW M4 DTM on pole with a 1m25.434s lap on the first flying runs, and nobody beat it. Rast was 0.158 seconds slower in the first runs and ended up second on 1m25.592s, his final attempt starting well in sector one before he lost time in the last two. The second runs were delayed by a red flag when Jake Dennis's R-Motorsport Aston Martin stopped at the Ramshoek bend and had to be towed back to the garage; Dennis did not return to the session.

Loïc Duval made it an all-Audi second row for Phoenix on 1m25.749s, with the WRT car of Pietro Fittipaldi fourth, 0.002 seconds behind him on 1m25.751s. Timo Glock was fifth for BMW Team RMR on 1m25.853s, with Müller sixth, two thousandths slower, ahead of Sheldon van der Linde on 1m25.856s. Bruno Spengler, Philipp Eng and Joel Eriksson were next, with Jonathan Aberdein 11th for WRT.

Robin Frijns, the local driver, was 12th on the timesheet but lined up last in 18th behind the rest of the grid. Jamie Green, Mike Rockenfeller and the Aston Martins of Daniel Juncadella, Paul di Resta and Ferdinand Habsburg filled the middle of the grid, with Dennis 17th.

Safety car start · #23 #16

A safety car start and a bruising duel for the lead

Rain intensified towards the start, with hail on the grid, and the race began behind the safety car with no formation lap; the field was released after three neutralised laps. Rast attacked Wittmann immediately, going side by side with him through the opening corners, but the BMW driver held the lead through the slow first sector and Rast chose not to take a risk as championship leader.

The duel ended when Juncadella's Aston Martin stopped at the side of the track and the safety car came out again. Wittmann got a good restart on lap six and pulled more than a second clear, while Rast ran slightly wide at the Geert Timmer Bocht chicane. Behind them Müller, starting sixth, climbed the order, passing Glock in a tight fight at the De Strubben hairpin that sent the BMW across the gravel, then Fittipaldi and Duval. Fittipaldi slipped from fourth on the grid to 11th by the flag.

Pit stops · #51 #4

Müller stays out as the rest stop

Rast pitted at the end of lap 15 and Wittmann a lap later, and the BMW driver rejoined ahead only for Rast to catch him while his tyres were slow to warm up. The pair resumed their battle, and Rast later said that scrap cost enough time to let Müller back in front.

Müller was the gambler. He ran third in a long first stint, mandatory stop still to come, which risked a safety car ruining his race, and came in at around lap 25 or 26 of the 31. He rejoined just ahead of Wittmann and Rast on the wet outlap. Frijns had gone off backwards into the barriers after running over a kerb, ending his race with 21 laps completed.

The finish · #25 #27 #7

Wittmann passes Müller and wins by 3.137 seconds

Müller led for only a short time: his cold tyres were slow to come in and Wittmann caught him quickly, making the pass for the lead before the Meeuwenmeer kink. Müller said the new rain tyres took longer to work than he had hoped and that Wittmann deserved the win. He got heat into the tyres before Rast could attack and held second, so two Audis shared the podium with the BMW.

Wittmann won his third race of the season, finishing 3.137 seconds ahead of Müller and 8.094 seconds clear of Rast, in 1h01m15.672s over 31 laps. Müller set the fastest lap of the race, a 1m44.598s on lap 30. Eng finished fourth for BMW Team RMR, 25.313 seconds back, fending off Glock, who was only 0.221 seconds behind in fifth. Aberdein was sixth for WRT after starting 11th, ahead of Green, Duval, Rockenfeller and van der Linde.

Spengler finished 15th, ahead of only Eriksson among the classified cars, while Dennis led the Aston Martins home in 12th from Habsburg and di Resta, 14th. Juncadella was out after three laps. The result left Rast leading the championship on 145 points, with Müller 24 behind, Eng third and Wittmann fourth, 45 points off the lead.
