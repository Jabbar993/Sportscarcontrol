Mortara wins a wet-dry Zandvoort DTM race for Audi

Edoardo Mortara won at Zandvoort for Audi Sport Team Rosberg from fourth on the grid, passing Mike Rockenfeller on lap 32 as rain returned, while pole-sitter Timo Scheider stalled at the start.

Build-up · #11 #7 #5

A title fight with three names

The seventh of ten rounds, on Sunday 26 August 2012, arrived with the championship tightening. Gary Paffett of Mercedes-Benz led on 103 points, but Bruno Spengler had won the previous round at the Nürburgring from pole and sat second on 83, with Jamie Green third on 81. Martin Tomczyk, the reigning champion, was fourth on 69, and Edoardo Mortara and Mike Rockenfeller were both on 49. Paffett's lead over Spengler stood at 20 points. Six races had produced four different winners, with Paffett and Spengler each taking two and Green and Mortara one apiece, and four races remained, Zandvoort included.

BMW was in its first season back in the series and had come to the Netherlands on the strength of that Nürburgring victory, with Spengler a clear threat to the leader. Audi had put three cars in the top four on the grid at the Nürburgring, and Timo Scheider said the team had worked hard at the last races and at a test at Magny-Cours to find a step forward. The Audi drivers hoped to carry that progress onto the Dutch dunes.

Zandvoort promised changeable weather. Rain fell on Saturday morning before qualifying, and the forecast for race day was for more showers rolling in off the North Sea. Whoever read the damp track and timed the pit stops best looked likely to profit, and the three title contenders from Mercedes and BMW had a good deal to lose if they misjudged it. Paffett, Spengler and Green were covered by only 22 points going into the race.

Qualifying · #4 #9 #22

Audi fills the first five places

Qualifying was held on a track that began the session damp and dried steadily, so that the order was shaped as much by timing as by outright pace. Scheider took pole in the Abt Sportsline Audi with 1:32.365, about a quarter of a second clear of Rockenfeller in the Phoenix Audi. It was a result he had been waiting for: he said it was 21 months since he had last been at the very front in qualifying, after some hard months for him and the team.

Behind them came the two Team Rosberg Audis, Filipe Albuquerque third and Mortara fourth, with Mattias Ekström fifth in the second Abt car. Audi therefore held the first five places on the grid, and Adrien Tambay made it a sixth Audi in the top ten by qualifying tenth. Jamie Green was sixth for Mercedes, Dirk Werner seventh for BMW Team Schnitzer, and Paffett eighth after struggling for pace, with Christian Vietoris ninth.

The day was far worse for BMW's leading names. Spengler and Augusto Farfus both fell in the first session, ending up 18th and 20th, while Tomczyk was 11th, narrowly missing the cut for the third segment. Joey Hand was 17th, Andy Priaulx 16th and Werner, seventh, was the best-placed BMW driver. Ralf Schumacher lined up 15th and David Coulthard 22nd, directly behind Rahel Frey in 21st.

Opening laps · #12 #20 #10

A stall, a pile-up and two Safety Car periods

The start undid the pole-sitter. Scheider stalled his Audi on the grid and was swallowed by the pack, which handed the lead to Rockenfeller. Further back, Robert Wickens and Miguel Molina made contact on the run to the first corner, and Wickens was then spun by Vietoris two turns later, left sideways on the track. Frey could not avoid him, and Wickens, Molina and Frey were all out on the opening lap, which brought out the Safety Car. Vietoris retired after one lap.

Spengler gained several places in the opening laps from 18th. Paffett, up from eighth on the grid, ran fifth after the first lap, only for Green to dive inside him at the restart and take the place. A few laps later Scheider's bonnet, damaged in contact with Coulthard's Mücke Mercedes, came off on lap six, and the carbon debris on the track forced a second Safety Car. Scheider retired after six laps.

Rain returns · #1 #3

Rockenfeller leads until the showers return

Rockenfeller controlled the middle part of the race from the front, with Mortara following in his wake. A battle for third developed between Ekström, Paffett and Tomczyk, and Paffett, who had moved up through the pit stops, got through on Ekström and then closed on the leaders. Green lost ground with slow pit stops that dropped him a long way down the order before the rain gave him a chance to recover.

Rain set in again on lap 28 and the race turned. Tomczyk, by his own account, locked his wheels as he tried to go round Paffett into Tarzan, and he slid into the Mercedes and spun it. Paffett fell outside the top ten, while Tomczyk retired moments later with the damage and went straight to the Mercedes pit wall to apologise to Norbert Haug. Albuquerque went through the gravel to avoid the wreckage, and he was classified 15th, nine laps down. Out in front, Rockenfeller found the changing grip harder as the leader than Mortara did as the follower, as he put it afterwards.

The finish · #21 #18

Mortara passes Rockenfeller at Tarzan

On lap 32 Mortara closed up and passed his fellow Audi driver at Tarzan, with slight contact as they ran side by side. Rockenfeller stayed close as the track dried out again but could not find a way back past, and Mortara took the flag after 43 laps in 1:16:17.679, 1.513 seconds clear. Rockenfeller called the move borderline and said he was very disappointed, though he added that he had not wanted to take unnecessary risks. Ekström was third, 8.417 seconds behind, having won his duel with Paffett.

Green took fourth, 13.283 seconds back and the best of the Mercedes drivers, after recovering from the slow stops once the rain returned. Tambay was fifth in the Audi Sport Team Abt car, his best DTM finish. Spengler completed a remarkable recovery to sixth from 18th, and Paffett, who passed Werner, Farfus and Schumacher in the last laps, rescued seventh. Werner, Farfus and Schumacher were eighth, ninth and tenth, ahead of Roberto Merhi and Susie Wolff.

Aftermath · #8 #16

Green moves up to second as Mortara closes in

Mortara called it the best race of his life, a tough battle with Rockenfeller in conditions that could catch you out on every lap. It was his second win of the year after his victory at the Red Bull Ring, and the three Audi teams each had a driver on the podium. Audi's motorsport head, Wolfgang Ullrich, said the car was back where the team always wants to be, capable of winning, and that he was pleased with how the whole team had performed. Scheider called his race a disaster, saying everything that could go wrong had done so.

The result tightened the table. Paffett stayed on top with 109 points, but Green's fourth place lifted him past Spengler into second on 93, 16 behind, with Spengler third on 91. Mortara moved up to fourth on 74, 35 points behind the leader, with Tomczyk fifth on 69 and Rockenfeller sixth on 67. Three races remained, and Spengler stood 18 points behind the leader after his recovery drive.

BMW took some comfort from the recovery. Jens Marquardt called the afternoon maximum damage control, singled out Spengler and Farfus, and said the Tomczyk-Paffett contact was the key moment, a racing accident in which Tomczyk had misjudged the grip when the rain arrived. Norbert Haug accepted Tomczyk's apology, and said Paffett had been on course for the podium before he was turned around. Paffett said he had been looking good for at least a podium before the contact. The stewards still penalised Tomczyk with a five-place grid drop for the next race, which Tomczyk said he thought was harsh but accepted.

Green described the race as topsy-turvy: a good start, a first stop that was too slow, then a chance to get back into it when the rain arrived, so that fourth was quite a good result. Spengler said sixth was the best the car could manage and that he was glad to see the flag in such difficult conditions. Audi put the attendance over the weekend at 39,000, Mercedes at 37,000. The next round is at Oschersleben on 16 September, where Tomczyk has to take his penalty.

Green snatches Norisring win at the last corner

Jamie Green passed Martin Tomczyk at the final corner of a rain-hit Norisring to win for Mercedes by 0.687 seconds, after a first-corner pile-up sent pole-sitter Gary Paffett to the back.

Build-up · #11 #21

Paffett arrives with a 36-point cushion

Round five of the ten-race 2012 DTM season took the series to the Norisring street circuit in Nuremberg on Sunday 1 July. Gary Paffett of HWA Mercedes led the drivers' standings on 83 points, with two wins and two more podium finishes from the first four races. Mattias Ekström was second on 47, ahead of Jamie Green on 44, Bruno Spengler on 43 and Martin Tomczyk on 36, with Mike Rockenfeller on 31 in sixth.

The previous round at the Red Bull Ring had gone to Edoardo Mortara, who won from pole for Team Rosberg's Audi, while reigning champion Tomczyk had taken second for BMW Team RMG. It left BMW with a podium to build on as it made its first DTM visit to the Norisring with the BMW M3 DTM.

The race had a scheduled lap distance but was capped at 75 minutes, with mandatory tyre stops, so the number of laps would depend on the pace of the race and any interruptions.

Qualifying · #5 #16 #12

Paffett takes pole as Mercedes lock out the front row

Saturday was hot, with air temperatures of 34 to 35 degrees, and the four-part qualifying session was decided by fractions. Paffett took pole position with a lap of 49.139 seconds, and Green was second on 49.171, only 0.032 seconds behind, which gave Mercedes an all-silver front row.

Farfus put his RBM BMW third on 49.255, 0.116 seconds off the pole time and the best grid slot of any BMW driver, and Ekström's Abt Audi was fourth. Ralf Schumacher, Green's Mercedes AMG team-mate, lined up fifth, with Tomczyk sixth and Spengler seventh. Christian Vietoris was eighth in the second HWA Mercedes, Priaulx ninth, Mortara tenth, and Rockenfeller 11th.

Further back the grid was less settled. Coulthard was 14th and Wickens 17th, in a session where traffic cost several drivers a clean lap, and Timo Scheider's Abt Audi was only 21st. Of the four cars to reach the final session, Ekström's was the only Audi.

Opening laps · #3 #20 #8

Chaos at the first corner

Heavy rain fell just before the start and the race began on a soaked track. At the first corner a chain reaction spread through the field: Paffett was turned around from pole position, Ekström's Audi was too badly damaged to continue and was out after a single lap, and Mortara's Rosberg Audi was also caught up. Wickens and Dirk Werner were both held up as they tried to avoid the stopped cars. The clerk of the course sent out the safety car.

Paffett rejoined near the back of the field. Ralf Schumacher moved into the lead of the race, with Tomczyk, who had dodged the trouble from sixth on the grid, and Spengler close behind.

A drying track · #6 #1 #19

Tomczyk leads as the track dries

Schumacher's lead did not last. He slowed with an electrical problem and dropped back, and he retired after 45 laps. That handed the lead to Tomczyk with Spengler second, and for a long time BMW looked on course for a one-two. The track dried for a spell and then another shower soaked it shortly before half distance.

Green had started second but ran fourth for much of the first half after the turmoil at the start, and moved up to third when Schumacher slowed. As the dampness faded, his Mercedes became the fastest car on the track. Paffett, meanwhile, worked his way back up the order from the tail of the field.

The finish · #7 #9 #15

Green passes Tomczyk at the last corner

Green's pace in the closing stages was far beyond that of the BMWs. With three laps left he went inside Spengler at Turn 4, then closed down Tomczyk and passed him at the final corner on the last lap. The flag fell 0.005 seconds after the 75-minute limit with 78 laps run, and Green won by 0.687 seconds from Tomczyk. Spengler took third, 2.335 seconds behind the winner, so BMW had two cars on the podium. Green also set the fastest lap, 54.451 seconds on lap 72.

Paffett recovered to fourth, 14.666 seconds behind Green. Coulthard, who started 14th, was fifth, 36.861 seconds back and the best result of his DTM career, and Rockenfeller, from 11th, was sixth as the only Audi in the top ten. Priaulx was seventh, Vietoris eighth, and Wickens and Werner took ninth and tenth to score the first points of their DTM careers.

Aftermath

A fourth Norisring win for Green

Green said the tyres had been the key to his win and that he had been quick as the track dried out. He felt he had no chance in the first 70 laps, then sensed some potential with four to go. It was his fourth victory at the Norisring. Spengler said afterwards that there had been no way to stop him, and the Hankook wet tyre was praised for staying consistent as the surface dried.

For Tomczyk, who had been on course for BMW's second win since its return, the defeat was bitter. "The worst thing that could have happened" was his verdict. He said he would have defended harder from Green had he known it was the last lap, but the information from his team about the laps remaining had differed. The 75-minute limit arrived sooner than expected, and the race had originally been scheduled to run longer than the 78 laps completed.

It was a poor weekend for Audi. Ekström, the best-placed Audi driver in the standings, scored nothing and fell from second to fifth, and Audi's motorsport chief Wolfgang Ullrich said the race had been practically over for them in the first corner, when almost all their cars became entangled. Rockenfeller's sixth place from 11th on the grid was the best Audi result, and he said the key had been getting through the first corner unharmed on the inside, after which he concentrated on fast laps in changeable conditions.

Paffett's run of podium finishes, which had covered all four races before Nuremberg, ended with his fourth place, but he kept the lead of the standings, although his margin shrank from 36 points to 26. Wickens and Werner scored 2 and 1 points, the first of their DTM careers, and BMW had four cars in the top ten with Spengler, Tomczyk, Priaulx and Werner. In the championship Paffett led on 95 points from Green on 69, 26 behind, with Spengler third on 58, Tomczyk fourth on 54, Ekström fifth on 47 and Rockenfeller sixth on 39. The next round was at the Nürburgring on 19 August.
